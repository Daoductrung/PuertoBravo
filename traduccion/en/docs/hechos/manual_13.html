24. People on the street

The people you pass are not extras. Each one has a first name and a surname, an age, a job, a personality and a mood for the day, and no two games have the same neighbors. The first names, surnames, jobs and phrases come from lists that can be expanded or changed with the mod editor, in the People on the street section. On foot, with someone next to you, the J key opens the conversation.

  • You can say hello, ask about the neighborhood, ask what's new, ask what they do for a living, pay a compliment, tell a joke, buy them a coffee or give them some spare change.
  • Ask about a place: they tell you where there is a pharmacy, a garage or whatever you are looking for, and mark it on your GPS.
  • The news they tell you comes from what is really happening in your game: the weather, whether there is a holiday or a strike today, whether the police are looking for someone, where there is work.
  • Ask for their number and become friends: if they like you, they join your contacts. They are on the phone, in Your contacts, with their job and their town. You can call them and meet up with them for a drink, as explained in the section Meeting at a place and time.
  • Each person reacts according to their personality and your reputation: people answer better to someone with a good name among the neighbors, and with fear to someone known to be dangerous.
  • You can also insult or threaten them to make them give you money. It's a crime, and people remember.
  • Write whatever you want to say: you type a sentence in your own words and the person answers according to what they understood.

People act too

The people you talk to don't just answer. The game itself decides, with no artificial intelligence, based on each person's personality and how much they trust you:

  • They walk with you for a while: if they like you, especially if they are chatty or friendly, they may start walking with you. You hear their footsteps, they comment on something, and after a while they say goodbye. They don't get into vehicles, and if the police are after you, they leave.
  • They hit you: if you keep talking to someone you have offended, they may throw a punch at you. People with a temper stay and fight; others hit you once and leave. They started it, so hitting back is self-defense.
  • They report you: if you insult someone and they walk away, they may call the police.

All of this can be turned off in Options, The street, The people you talk to act on their own too. And there are two new things you choose, which are always in the conversation:

  • Ask a favor: it takes trust, and one a day per person. What they do depends on your situation: if the police are after you at a low level and there are no officers nearby, they hide you and the search stops; if you are low on health, they patch you up; otherwise, they give you a tip about something nearby and mark it on your GPS, or they speak well of you in the neighborhood.
  • Ask them to join your gang: you need a territory and room in the gang. They also have to trust you, your name has to be known on the street, and they need the personality for it: very shy people and older people say no, each in their own way. They ask for a bonus, smaller than what recruiting from the phone costs. If they accept, they stay with you under their own name, fight at your side and get paid like the others.

On an online server you can't recruit people from the street: there, gangs are made of players. The numbers and phrases are in the game data.

People react, and you can hear them

Each person has their own voice, a man's or a woman's, and uses it to scream and complain. When someone is frightened by a gunshot, an explosion, your shout with H or a robbery, you hear them scream as they run off, from where they are. When they take a hit, they complain in their own voice. And the game says in words what is happening: "A woman screams and runs off, to the east", or "A man jumps out of the way" if it's because of the horn. If several react at once, it says so in one go: "Three people run off". To avoid overload, it says no more than one of these sentences every eight seconds; the screams are all heard, though. If you already know that person, it says their name. The sentence can be turned off in Options, The street; the sounds can't.

Someone with a woman's voice has a woman's name when you talk to her, and the same goes for men.

People who talk as you pass

Some people walk in pairs. When you walk past two people, or go by slowly, you sometimes hear what they are saying: a murmur and a stray sentence about the weather, football, work, prices or the neighborhood, and sometimes what the other one replies. They come from the same phrases people use to answer you, so they rarely repeat. You don't hear them in the middle of a chase, a fight or a scene.

How often you hear them is chosen in Options, The street, People who talk as you pass: never; rarely, which is the default, one every two or three minutes at most; now and then; or often.

Talk about anything, with no artificial intelligence

To write whatever you want, you don't need to turn anything on or have internet. The game comes with a base of more than eleven hundred phrases spread across almost seventy topics, and it recognizes what you are talking about: the weather, work, family, football, food, music, the neighborhood, the port, the police, gangs, money, health, holidays, animals, love, your sorrows and your joys, and many more.

  • Each person answers according to their personality: the chatty one tells you their life story, the curt one answers in two words, the shy one hesitates, the cocky one shows off.
  • Trust counts too. Someone who doesn't trust you won't even tell you their name; someone who likes you calls you by your name.
  • The sentences are built from pieces, and each person remembers what they have already said, so it's very hard to hear the same thing twice. If you keep on the same topic, they point it out.
  • Saying thank you or apologizing raises trust a little. Asking for money or talking about weapons lowers it.
  • If you write something it doesn't recognize, it answers the way anyone would answer an odd question or a passing remark.

With AI mode turned on, which is explained next, the artificial intelligence answers; if it's not turned on, or if it fails, this base answers.

AI mode

In Options, People and artificial intelligence, you can turn on AI mode. With it, when you write what you want to say to someone, an artificial intelligence answers, playing that person with their personality and their mood at that moment, and knowing where you are, what time it is and what the weather is like. You can talk about anything and it never answers the same way twice.

You can use whichever service you like, or several at once:

  • Local model: a small artificial intelligence that runs on your own computer. It's free, needs no internet or key, and nothing you write leaves your computer. It answers somewhat more slowly and with less wit than the internet services. How to install it is explained further down.
  • Google AI Studio, Gemini: it has a free tier. You get the key at aistudio.google.com.
  • OpenAI: paid. You get the key at platform.openai.com.
  • Anthropic, Claude: paid. You get the key at platform.claude.com.
  • Another compatible service: any that uses the OpenAI format, such as OpenRouter or Groq. Some have free models. You enter its address and its key.

You don't need to know anything about models or keep track of them. The game asks each service which models it has, picks the one from the cheapest range, remembers it for a week and, if one day that model disappears, finds another on its own. On the paid services, a conversation costs fractions of a cent.

In the Service option you choose which one is used. With Automatic, which is the default, the game tries the free ones first: the local model and then Google AI Studio. After that, the other services that have a key. If one fails or reaches its limit, it moves on to the next without you having to do anything.

  • The keys are entered on that same screen, can be pasted with Control V and are stored encrypted on your computer. They don't appear in the session log.
  • Test now asks a test question and tells you which service and which model answered, and how long it took.
  • What gets sent is what you write, the person's profile, the time, the neighborhood, the weather and how the neighbors see you. Your saved game, your files and none of your personal data are sent. On Google's free tier, what is sent may be used to improve their products; with the local model nothing leaves your computer.
  • Without AI mode, or if no service answers, the game itself answers, with no internet.

The AI can suggest actions, and the game decides

This is a separate option, in the same menu, and it's off by default. It only counts with AI mode turned on, and it doesn't work with the local model. With it, besides answering, the model can suggest that the character do one thing from this list, and no other: walk with you for a while, leave, get angry or join your gang.

  • The model suggests; it doesn't decide. What happens is decided by the game, with the same rules as without artificial intelligence: for someone to join your gang you need the territory, the trust, the reputation, the personality and the bonus, no matter who suggests it.
  • That's why there is no point writing to it that it should obey you, or trying to talk it into something the rules don't allow.
  • There is no money and there are no items on the list. No character gives you anything because of what you write.
  • When the action happens, the game describes it after the answer.

With the option off, the artificial intelligence can only make the character leave or get angry, as before.

Getting it running, in three steps

  1. In the main menu or in the pause menu, go to Options, People and artificial intelligence.
  2. Choose one of two things. The simplest and free option is to install the local model: it downloads once, you can leave it in the background with B, and when it finishes AI mode turns on by itself. The other is to enter the key for an internet service, for example the free one from Google AI Studio, and set AI mode to yes.
  3. Press Test now to check that it answers. Then, on the street, stand next to someone, press J and choose Talk to them in your own words: you type and that person answers you.

Installing the local model

It's all done from inside the game, without leaving it:

  1. Go to Options, People and artificial intelligence, and press Enter on the Local model line.
  2. Choose one of the three models and press Enter. The help for each one, with F1, tells you how much space it takes, how much memory it uses and how long it takes to answer.
  3. Wait for the download to finish. The game reads out the percentage; Escape cancels it. Everything goes in the data\ia_local folder.
  4. When it finishes, AI mode is turned on and the model starts loading. Go back to the previous menu and press Test now.

The three models, actually measured with the game's conversations:

  • Balanced, the recommended one: 770 megabytes on disk and 1 gigabyte of memory. It's the one that answers most sensibly, in short sentences. On a modern computer it answers in 1 second; on an old four-core one, in about 6; on a two-core one, in about 10.
  • Light: 510 megabytes on disk and 1.2 gigabytes of memory. It's the fastest on modest computers, about 4 seconds with four old cores, but sometimes it answers things that don't make much sense.
  • Medium: 1.2 gigabytes on disk and 2.3 of memory. Only for modern computers, where it answers in 1 or 2 seconds. On an old one it takes more than 10.

Keep in mind what it is: a small artificial intelligence. It speaks correctly and follows the conversation, but it makes things up, for example where a pharmacy is, and it doesn't have the wit of the internet services. To ask about real places, use the Ask about a place option in the conversation menu.

The game takes care of the model: it starts it when the game opens if AI mode is going to use it, stops it if you turn off AI mode or choose another service, and always closes it when you quit. Even if the game closes because of an error, or you close it from the Task Manager, the model closes with it: nothing is ever left open or using memory.

In that same menu you can change models, which replaces the previous one, or remove it, which deletes the folder. The files are downloaded from the official pages of the llama.cpp program and of the models, and the game checks that they are exactly the expected ones before using them. If you already use Ollama or LM Studio and have a model loaded, you don't need to install anything: the game detects it.

Recorded voices in the scenes

The mission scenes are voiced: each character in the story has their own voice, different from the others, and the text still appears in braille and in the history. There are more than twelve hundred recordings, between the game and its added stories.

  • They call you by your name. If you play as Dani, Álex, Sam or Cris, the other characters say that name, and they address you as a man or a woman depending on what you chose.
  • You have a voice too. Each of the four characters has a male voice and a female voice; the one that plays matches how they address you.
  • With a character you created, the lines that say your name can't come prerecorded: the game generates them on your computer, with the same character voices. This is explained further down, in Your own character's voices.
  • A line that has no voice is read by your screen reader, as always. This happens with very few, and with those from mods that don't come with recordings.
  • With a gamepad connected, the lines that name a key are read by the screen reader, so it can say the button.
  • In Options, Voice, the Voices in the scenes option turns them off or on. With no, everything is read by your screen reader or, if you don't use a screen reader, by the Windows voice.

The voices are not actors: they are computer-generated with Phonon, a voice engine from the company Gradium, with its Phonon 40m model. That model has a Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial license, CC BY-NC 4.0; the full text comes with the game, in the file LICENCIA_Phonon_Gradium.txt. Up to version 1.6.2 the voices were different ones, made with Microsoft's VibeVoice, and they only played with Dani as a man: when you update, the game replaces them with the new ones.

Your own character's voices

If you create your character, in the story they call you by the name you typed. To make that play with a voice and not with the screen reader, the game comes with a voice generator and uses it on your own computer, offline.

  • When the game starts, a screen appears that says Preparing your character's voices. It prepares the first lines that name you and tells you how it's going. It takes a few seconds; on a slow computer, a bit longer. With Escape or Enter you start without waiting.
  • The rest, about thirty-five lines, gets prepared while you play, without you having to do anything. On a fast computer it's a matter of a couple of minutes; on a slow one, quite a bit longer.
  • If you reach a line before it's ready, your screen reader reads that one. Nothing is left unsaid.
  • It's only done once per character: what has been prepared is saved in data\voces_generadas. If another day you create a character with the same name, the same voice and the same form of address, it's already done. That folder can be deleted whenever you like; whatever is missing gets prepared again.
  • What you say in the scenes doesn't need to be generated: it plays with the voice you choose when creating the character, from those of Dani, Álex, Sam and Cris, male or female depending on how they address you.
  • In Options, Voice, the Generate the voices of your own character option turns it off. With no, the lines that say your name are read by your screen reader or the Windows voice. If you notice the game stuttering while the voices are being prepared, turn it off: it stops generating right away, even with the game in progress. If you turn it back on, it picks up where it left off.
  • The generator needs a processor with the AVX2, FMA and F16C instructions, which almost all processors made since 2013 or 2015 have. The game checks all three before using it. On an older one the game works the same and all the other voices play, but the lines with your name are read by the screen reader. The option itself tells you whether your computer can do it.
  • The first time, the game tests the generator separately, in the background, before using it: it takes a few seconds, while you type your character's name. If the generator fails on your computer, it's that test that fails and not the game: the game carries on, remembers it so it doesn't keep trying, and the lines with your name are read by the screen reader. The test runs again by itself when you update the game. If you press Listen to how you will be called while the test is running, the game tells you to wait a moment.
  • Uncommon names may not sound right the first time. That's why, when creating the character, you can listen to how you will be called and, if needed, write how it's pronounced.

Recording your own voices

If someone records the lines of a mission, from the game or from a mod, the game uses those recordings: the sound files go in the data\voces\propias folder, one per line, and each line that has a recording plays with that voice.

  • The files can be WAV, OGG, FLAC or MP3, and they can be in subfolders.
  • Each file is named with the mission, the step number and the line number, separated by underscores. For example, oc01_1_2 is the second line of the first step of mission oc01.
  • That file, with nothing after it, is the one that plays with Dani as a man, and with any protagonist if the line doesn't depend on who it is. The lines that name the protagonist, that change depending on whether they are a man or a woman, or that the protagonist says themselves have more forms, and the name is followed by an at sign and who it's for: oc01_1_2@alex if they call them Álex, oc01_1_2@m if they address them as a woman, oc01_1_2@alex_m if it's both, or if the protagonist says the line.
  • You don't need to work out the names: the mod editor, with Control G, writes the full script of a mod, with each line, who says it, the name its file must have and its other forms.
  • You don't have to record them all. For someone playing with a protagonist who has no recording of a line, that line is read by the screen reader; someone else's never plays.
  • Your recordings, or ones someone else gives you, go in data\voces\propias. They take priority over the ones that come with the game, and updates don't touch that folder.
  • The voices that come with the game are in data\voces, in one subfolder per story. Each version comes with its own and, the first time it starts, the game puts them there and removes the previous version's.

25. Every day is different

Puerto Bravo doesn't repeat itself. Each game is born with its own luck, and every midnight decides what the next day will be like.

  • Most days are normal. On others there are sales at the gun shops, market days with cheaper food, or shortages with everything more expensive. There are holidays, with the streets full of people and few cars; transport strikes, with hardly any traffic; holiday getaway days, with traffic jams; days when there isn't a soul around, and cruise days, with tourists everywhere.
  • The game announces it at midnight with an audible alert. The T key, which tells you the time and the weather, reminds you; the news station reports it and the people on the street talk about it.
  • Also, each shop has its own price each day, a little above or below the shop next door. It's worth comparing.
  • Other things also change on their own: the weather, the cars on the road and the parked ones, the people you pass, street incidents, the stock market, the lottery and, in adult mode, the suppliers' prices.

26. Realistic life: hunger, thirst, sleep and hygiene

It's optional and off by default. It's turned on in Options, Combat and difficulty. With it you have to eat, drink, sleep and wash, as in real life.

  • Thirst kicks in before hunger: about nine hours of game time without drinking. When you are very thirsty you run slower and, if you ignore it, you lose health. You quench it with a bottle of water, sold in bars, restaurants and shops; with a small beer; by eating in your kitchen; by sleeping in a hotel; or, for free, at places with a fountain, such as some squares and parks, which have the Drink water from the fountain option. In the mod editor, any place can have a fountain: it's the Has a drinking fountain checkbox. Shops also have water jugs, sports drinks and canned coffee. Eating also quenches some thirst.
  • Hygiene: after a long day you'll want a shower. You shower in the bathroom at your home, at the hotel when you spend the night, and in the gym locker rooms, free if you trained that day and for three euros if you didn't. Going into the sea removes some of the dirt, but not all of it.
  • In the drugstore section of the supermarket, the bazaar and the gas station shop there is deodorant, wet wipes, cologne, and a toothbrush with toothpaste. You carry them with you and use them from the phone, in Inventory, Food and groceries you are carrying. They help you get by, but without a shower you never get completely clean.
  • Being dirty has consequences. When you need a shower, a date brings you half as close, because it shows; at work you do less and sell fewer cars. And if you already smell bad, on top of that, people on the street trust you less when you talk, nobody wants a kiss or to spend the night with you until you shower, and at the club and the casino the doorman won't let you in.
  • Going around very hungry, very thirsty or smelling bad slowly lowers your mood, and the game reminds you.
  • Hunger takes about fourteen hours of game time to reach the limit, and sleep about twenty. Eating anywhere takes away hunger; sleeping in one of your homes or in a hotel takes away sleepiness.
  • When you are very hungry or very sleepy you move more slowly and aim worse. If you ignore them, you slowly lose health.
  • The game warns you when they start to press, and the V key tells you how you are doing. Shift V says only hunger, thirst, sleep and hygiene, without the rest.
  • To eat, go into any bar, restaurant, pizzeria or tavern and choose a dish. With realistic life you can eat whenever you are a bit hungry, even if your health is full. If you don't know where there is one, ask anyone on the street where you can eat and they mark it on your GPS.
  • Without realistic life, food only serves to recover health: that's why, if your health is at maximum, the game says you are not hungry. And neither thirst nor hygiene count.
  • How much it takes to be dirty or to smell bad, what lowers each thing and where you're not allowed in are in the game data, and they can be changed with the mod editor.
  • From one of your homes you can order food and drink delivered. It's explained right below.
  • You can also do the grocery shopping, store it at home and cook: home-cooked food is much more filling and cheaper. It's explained further down, in Shopping by walking around the store, The pantry and the fridge, and Cooking at home.

Opening hours, optional

It's off by default. It's in Options, Life, reputation and consequences, Opening hours, and it has three settings:

  • No: everything is always open and every person is always in their place, as before.
  • People: each person in your social life is in their place only during their hours, and has one day off a week. Their profile says so: from what time to what time they are usually there, what day they have off, and whether they are there now. Outside those hours, call them or suggest meeting up. When you arrange to meet, the game only offers you the hours when they're not working.
  • People and venues: in addition, each venue opens and closes at its own hours, and some close one day a week. For example, shops open from nine to nine and close on Sunday; banks, in the morning and on weekdays; bars and taverns, until two in the morning; clubs, at night. Each venue shifts by an hour either way, so they don't all open at the same time. When you reach one that's closed, the game tells you when it opens.
  • Whatever a mission or a job in progress requires is always open, and so are your homes and your businesses. Hospitals, police stations, hotels, stations and the other places with no opening hours are always open.
  • The first day of the game is a Monday.
  • The opening hours of each type of venue, their closing days and people's hours are in the game data, and they can be changed with the mod editor.
