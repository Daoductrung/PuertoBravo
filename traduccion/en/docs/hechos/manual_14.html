Food delivery

When you are in one of your homes, or at its door, the phone has one more option: Order food delivery. It's also in the house menu. It works with realistic life and without it.

  • The menu: sandwich, pizza, set menu, water and soft drink, each with its price and what it does. Enter adds an item to the order, and pressing it again removes it. Up to four fit. The last option, Place the order, orders it: you pay for what you ordered plus delivery.
  • The wait: it takes around half an hour of game time, which is one minute of real time, and a little different each time; longer if it's raining. They tell you roughly how long. While you are in a menu, time doesn't pass: go out to the door and wait nearby.
  • The arrival: the doorbell rings, the delivery person says their line and you eat it. It takes away the hunger and thirst of realistic life, each item its share, and gives some health.
  • If you're not there: the delivery person calls your phone and waits at the door for a while. If you get back in time, they hand it over. If not, they leave with the order and you don't get your money back.
  • Only one order at a time. With the police seriously after you, no delivery person will come near.

The menu, the times and the lines are in the game data, and the delivery price is in the rates.

Shopping by walking around the store

In food shops you can do the grocery shopping for home. It works with realistic life and without it, and it doesn't force you into anything: anyone who doesn't want to cook can keep eating in bars or ordering delivery. There are two ways to shop, and both sell the same things at the same price.

  • Walking around: in the shop menu, Walk around inside the place. You pick up a basket and go from one aisle to another with the Left and Right Arrows; you hear your footsteps toward that side, and when you arrive the game says which aisle it is, what it's like and how many products it has.
  • Enter on an aisle opens its list of products, each with its price and whether it's for cooking or for eating as is. Enter or the Right Arrow on a product puts it in the basket, with its sound; the Left Arrow puts it back on the shelf. F1 tells you which recipes it goes into, where it's stored at home and how many days it keeps. Escape goes back to the aisle.
  • The C key, while you walk around the shop, tells you what you have in the basket and how much it adds up to. The last option of each aisle says it too. With a gamepad, X or Square: what you have in the basket; in the aisle, A or Cross or D-pad right puts the product in the basket and D-pad left puts it back.
  • The checkout is next to the door. With Enter you see the basket product by product: Enter or the Left Arrow on one puts back one unit, and the first option, Pay, rings it all up, with the sound of the register. If you don't have enough money, the shopkeeper tells you how much is missing and you can put something back right there.
  • With nothing in the basket, the checkout is the usual counter: Enter takes you back to the shop menu, where the first aid kit, the lockpicks and the rest are.
  • Without walking around: in the shop menu, Buy takeout food for home. It's the list of everything in the aisles; Enter buys one unit and pays for it on the spot.

Each kind of shop has its own aisles. Supermarkets have everything: fruit stand, butcher shop, fish counter, dairy and eggs, bakery, canned goods and pantry, drinks and ready-to-eat food. Food markets have fresh produce, and somewhat cheaper. Grocery stores and village shops have no meat or fish, and are somewhat more expensive. Gas stations and bazaars only have drinks and ready-to-eat things, and are the most expensive. Hardware stores and marine supplies shops don't sell food for home. Every day, in every shop, there is one product on offer, which is announced when you go in, and some product may be missing: the game says there is none left today.

What the shop menu sold until now stays the same: the food you eat right there to recover health, the bottle of water, the first aid kit and the rest.

Leaving without paying

If you are about to leave the shop with something in the basket, through the door or with Escape, the game asks what you want to do: go to the register to pay, leave the basket and exit, leave without paying, or keep shopping.

  • Leave the basket and exit: the basket stays in the shop with everything in it. Nothing happens.
  • Leave without paying: you take everything, but it's shoplifting. You may be seen, more easily the more you carry: then the police are after you, your standing with the neighbors drops, and you're not allowed into that shop for a couple of days. If nobody sees you, nothing happens.

The pantry and the fridge

What you buy for home you carry with you, in the bag, until you put it away. Twenty items fit. It's stored in the kitchen of one of your homes: Walk around inside the house, with the arrows to the kitchen, Enter, and Store what you are carrying. Fresh food goes in the fridge and the rest in the pantry.

  • Each home has its own fridge and pantry: what you store in one isn't in the others. To take something from one home to another, you take it, carry it with you and store it there.
  • Space is limited: fourteen items fit in the fridge and thirty in the pantry. Each unit of a product and each cooked portion takes one space. With the Fitted kitchen and big fridge furniture, ten more fit in the fridge. Whatever doesn't fit stays with you.
  • Fresh food goes off after a number of game days: fish after two days; meat after three; vegetables after four or five; eggs after ten. Rice, pasta, legumes and cans never go off. The game always says how long each thing has left: expires today, expires tomorrow, or expires in so many days.
  • When you open the fridge or the pantry, the game warns you about what expires today or tomorrow. What has already expired is thrown away automatically, and the game tells you what it threw out: nothing ever makes you ill, but the money is lost. The same happens with what you carry.
  • In the fridge and the pantry, Enter on an item lets you eat or drink it, if it can be eaten as is; take one unit with you, to eat out; or throw it away.
  • What you carry that can be eaten as is, like a sandwich, a piece of fruit or a bottle of water, you can eat wherever you like: open the phone with M, Inventory, Food and groceries you are carrying. What is for cooking waits until you get home.
  • To check what you have without going into the kitchen: the house menu has the Kitchen option, with what is stored and the recipes you can make; and the phone, in Inventory, tells you what is in the kitchen of each of your homes.

When you're not hungry and your health is full, you don't eat anything: the game says you are not hungry, so the food doesn't go to waste.

Cooking at home

To cook you have to go to the kitchen: Walk around inside the house, to the kitchen, and Enter. If you have something stored or are carrying food, the kitchen menu opens; if not, the usual happens: you make yourself something with whatever there is, once a day. That option is still in the kitchen menu, as Eat a quick bite of whatever is around the house.

  • Cook opens the list of recipes: first the ones you can make now, and for the rest, what you are missing. F1 tells you what each one contains, how long it takes, how many portions it makes and how filling it is.
  • Each recipe uses one unit of each ingredient, from what is stored in that home or what you are carrying. Oil and salt are taken for granted.
  • You hear chopping on the board, the frying pan or the pot, depending on the recipe, and the plate on the table. It lasts a few seconds; with Enter you don't wait. The recipe's time, between five minutes and an hour, passes on the game clock.
  • It makes several portions. You eat one, if you are hungry, and the rest go in the fridge, where they keep for one to four days. If they don't fit in the fridge, you carry them in a lunch box; and if you can't carry them either, they are lost.
  • Home-cooked food is much more filling than packaged food and much cheaper than eating out: one portion takes away almost all your hunger, gives quite a lot of health and lifts your mood.
  • Now and then a dish turns out excellent, and is more filling and more cheering; or it gets a bit burnt, and is less filling. Things that aren't cooked over heat, like salad, don't burn.
  • Your skill in the kitchen improves with practice, from level zero to level ten, and is saved with the game: at a higher level you burn things less and turn out excellent dishes more often. The fitted kitchen also helps keep things from burning, and what you make in it gives double health.
  • With adult content, if your partner lives with you and is at home, they eat some of what you cook, say so, and it brings you a little closer, once a day.

The recipes: Tuna and tomato sandwich, Mixed salad, Fried eggs with potatoes, Grilled hake with lemon, Macaroni with chorizo, Homemade burgers, Spanish potato omelette, Chicken and rice, Seafood paella, Chickpea stew and Lentils with chorizo. For the fish, seafood or meat ones you have to shop at a market or a supermarket.

The products, the aisles of each kind of shop, the recipes, what fits in the fridge and the pantry, and everything else can be changed with the mod editor.

27. Updates

The game updates itself, whenever you want. When it starts it checks whether there is a new version and, if there is, it lets you know; it doesn't download or install anything unless you ask.

  • To update, go to Options, Updates, and choose Check for updates now. If there is a new version you can read what's new in it and choose Download and install now. The game reads out the progress; Escape cancels. If the connection drops halfway, the download resumes by itself from where it was, several times, before giving up.
  • During the download, any key tells you how far along it is: the percentage and the megabytes downloaded out of the total. The same goes for downloading the artificial intelligence model.
  • Downloading in the background: during the download, press B and keep playing. When it finishes, the game lets you know, and you install it whenever suits you from Options, Updates, Install the downloaded version. If you close the game first, the download continues from where it was next time.
  • When the download finishes, the game closes, updates and opens again by itself within a few seconds. If something went wrong, the version you had stays. In that case, the sounds, music and radio that come with the game may be left with those of the new version: that's fine, the game works the same with them, and the next update that succeeds brings everything up to date. Your music and your stations are never touched.
  • If you have a server open on this computer, the console one or the panel, you no longer need to close it before updating. The game tells you it's open, explains what is going to happen and asks for permission. If you accept, the server warns the players with a countdown, waits for their games to save, saves everything and closes; the game updates and, when it finishes, the server starts again by itself, just as it was, and the players rejoin on their own and carry on where they were. It takes about a minute. If the update failed, the server starts again anyway with the version you had, and what it saved when closing isn't lost: the update doesn't touch its configuration or its data, whether it succeeds or fails. If what you have open is the panel with the server stopped, even if you have never started it, there is nobody to warn: the panel closes by itself and opens again when it's done, stopped as it was. The server and the panel come back minimized and don't take the focus: the focus stays on the game. When it opens, the game tells you whether the server has started again. If you choose Not now, nothing is touched and the download is kept. That question is a menu like the others: with a gamepad you move through it with the D-pad, accept with A or Cross, go back with B or Circle, and Y or Triangle explains what is going to happen.
  • If that server is started by a Windows service or scheduled task that you set up, the game warns you beforehand: it can ask it to close, but it can't start it again the same way. Afterwards you'll have to start it however you have it set up.
  • Before downloading, the game works out how much disk space is needed and, if there isn't enough, says so with figures: how much is needed, how much there is and how much you need to free up. Space is needed at the same time for the download, for the new version and for a copy of the previous one, in case it has to go back.
  • Before closing to install, it checks what usually breaks an update: that there is still space, that Windows allows writing to the game folder, and that the mod editor isn't open, because Windows doesn't allow replacing a running program. If something isn't right, it says so, doesn't close, and the download isn't lost: fix it and go to Options, Updates, Install the downloaded version.
  • If the installation still fails, when the game reopens it tells you the reason: which file couldn't be replaced and why, and what to do. You have it again in Options, Updates, Why the last update failed. The installer writes everything down, step by step, in the actualizacion.log file in the data\logs folder; if you send it to us, we'll know what happened. It doesn't contain your folders or your name: only the name of each file is kept.
  • Your saved games, your options, your music and your mods are not touched.
  • Update from: with the Left and Right Arrows you choose where versions are checked for and downloaded from. There are four possibilities: the game server; GitHub; both, which uses the newest version and, if one fails, the other; or a custom address, which you type in the next option and which must start with https.
  • Wherever it comes from, only a version signed by the game's publisher is installed: if someone changed the file along the way, the game rejects it.
  • Check when the game starts: this can be turned off. Then it only checks when you ask.
  • If it can't connect and you use AdGuard, an antivirus with network protection or a firewall, add Puerto Bravo to its exceptions.
  • Before downloading, besides the space, the game checks that Windows allows writing to its folder. If it doesn't, it says so right away, with the most common reason: the game is in a protected folder, such as Program Files, or an antivirus is blocking it.
  • If during the download it's the disk that fails and not the network, the game tells the difference and says so: the disk is full, Windows doesn't allow writing, or the antivirus has quarantined or is holding the file. It no longer tells you to check your connection when the problem is on your computer.
  • If you have the game open twice, it says so before closing to install: close the other window and go back to Install the downloaded version.
  • If the game couldn't check for a new version when it opened, it doesn't bother you with an error, but it's noted in Options, Updates, with the reason. If that notice always appears, the game can't tell you about new versions: look into the network filter, or change Update from.

Components: languages, voices, documentation, sounds and mods

Some things are published separately from the game, without waiting for a new version: a new or corrected language, its voices, corrected guides, a sound pack or a mod. Each one is a component, with its own version number.

  • They are in Options, Updates, Components. The game looks for them in the same source as the versions, the one you have in Update from: the game server, GitHub, both, or your address.
  • The list says for each one what it is, which version is published, whether you have it, and how much space it takes. Enter installs or updates the selected one, and F1 says what it includes. If there are several new ones, the first line, Install everything new, installs them all at once.
  • They are installed without closing the game, with the progress read out as with updates, and Escape cancels. A sound pack is heard right away; a language or voices, the next time you open the game.
  • With Check for new components at startup, which is on by default, when the game opens it checks whether there are new components and lets you know in the main menu. It doesn't install anything unless you ask.
  • Like the versions, each component is signed by the game's publisher, and each one can only write in its own place: a language, in its folders; a sound pack, in data\sonidos. They never touch your saved games, your options, your music or your own voices.

Updating manually

If there is no way to do it from the game, because of an antivirus, a network filter or folder permissions, you can put the new version in place yourself. The steps are also inside the game, in Options, Updates, Update manually, with an option that opens the downloads page and another that opens the game folder.

  1. Download the ZIP file of the latest version from the downloads page, github.com/hxebolax/PuertoBravo/releases/latest. Its name starts with PuertoBravo followed by the version number. If the network is blocked on your computer, download it on another one or with your phone.
  2. Close the game, and also the mod editor and the online server, if you have them open.
  3. Open the ZIP and copy everything inside it into the folder where you have the game, over what is there. When Windows asks, choose to replace the files. Don't delete the folder first: your saved games, your options and your music are inside, and the new version doesn't include any of that, so nothing gets overwritten. Your mods aren't deleted either.
  4. Open the game. In Options, Updates, the first line says the installed version: it should be the new one.

If Windows won't let you copy, the game is in a protected folder: move the whole game folder somewhere else, for example inside Documents, and repeat step three.

28. If something goes wrong: the session log

Every time you open the game, it writes down what happens in a folder. This is so that, if something goes wrong, the person who makes the game can see what happened without being there. You don't have to turn anything on.

  • The logs are in the data\logs folder. Each session has its own folder, with the date and time in the name, for example 2026-11-14_18-30-05_juego. The mod editor saves its own there too, ending in editor.
  • The last five sessions are kept. When a new one starts, the oldest one is deleted automatically, so they never take up much space.
  • Inside each folder there is a LEEME.txt file that explains what it contains. They are text files: you can read them before sending them.

What is recorded

  • The version of the game and of Windows, the screen reader, the sound, the mods, the packs and your game options.
  • The screens and menus you open, the keys you press while playing and what the game says.
  • The state of the game every few seconds: town, position, vehicle, health, current mission.
  • What happens with the network and with updates, the warnings and, above all, the errors in full detail.

What is never recorded

  • Nothing you type in text boxes: messages, passwords, addresses or keys.
  • Chat messages, your online name and your friends' names.
  • Internet addresses, your Windows user name, your computer's name and the paths of your folders.
  • The names of your music and the contents of your saved games.

How to report a bug

  1. If you notice something odd while playing, press F12: it leaves a mark in the log, so it's known at what moment it happened. You can press it as many times as you like.
  2. Close the game. If the game closed by itself because of an error, it tells you the name of the session folder itself.
  3. Go to data\logs and find the folder of the session where it failed. The last one is the most recent. In Options, Log for reporting bugs, there is an option that opens the folder for you directly.
  4. Compress it into a ZIP: in Windows File Explorer, on the folder, context menu, Send to, Compressed folder.
  5. Send it, explaining what you were doing. The best thing is to open an issue on the project page, github.com/hxebolax/PuertoBravo/issues, and attach the ZIP there. You can also send it via Mastodon to @HXeBoLaX on comunidad.nvda.es.

29. Your files

The game doesn't install anything: it's a folder that can be copied as is to another computer or to a USB drive. Everything it uses is in the data folder, next to the executable.

  • data\partidas: your saved games. To take them to another computer, copy this folder.
  • data\config: your options and the gamepad button mapping.
  • data\radio\mi_musica: your music for the My music station.
  • data\mods: the mods, both the ones that come with the game and your own.
  • data\sonidos, data\radio and data\musica: the sounds and the music, which you can change as explained in the guide cambiar_sonidos_en.html.
  • data\docs: this manual and the guides, each with its language code at the end: manual_es.html, manual_en.html.
  • data\logs: the logs of the last five sessions, for reporting bugs.
  • data\voces: the scene voices that come with the game, in one folder per story and per language: historia_principal_es the Spanish ones, historia_principal_en the English ones. Yours go in data\voces\propias, which updates don't touch.
  • data\idiomas: the game's languages, one per folder. See "Translating the game into another language", in the mods section.
  • data\ia_local: the local artificial intelligence model, if you install it.

Updates don't touch your saved games, your options, your music, your voices or the local model.

Saved games are encrypted and signed. The game only loads saved games that it saved itself: a tampered saved game, or one written by hand, is rejected.

The game data, the mods, the saved games and the options are protected: they can't be opened with a text editor to change them, and a tampered or damaged file is rejected instead of being half loaded. To create or change content, use the mod editor.

If you pass the game on to someone else, don't give them your data\partidas and data\config folders.
