Life, reputation and consequences

In Puerto Bravo you can be a good person, a criminal or both at once. Everyone chooses, and each path has its responsibilities and its consequences. The game keeps two separate scores, which don't depend on each other:

  • Your standing among the neighbors, from 0 to 100: how the city sees you. It rises with honest work, studies, donations, volunteering and good deeds. It drops with the crimes people see you commit, with arrests and with scandals. It has four steps: the neighbors avoid you, below 10; you're just another person; the neighbors like you, from 40; and you're a model citizen, from 75.
  • Your street reputation: how the criminal world sees you. It rises with missions, contracts, the neighborhoods you conquer and heists. It drops when you're arrested.

You can have both high, just one or neither. Everything is checked in one place: the phone, in Life, reputation and consequences. There you can read how the city sees you, how the street sees you and what you are gaining or losing because of it, and you'll find your criminal record, your job, your studies, your businesses, the bank and what you're going to be charged, what is known about your other life, the matters waiting for a decision from you and how far you've got in the story of the straight path.

None of this forces anyone. Whoever wants to play as always can: what you choose yourself, like a permanent contract, a degree, a loan or upgrading a business, doesn't show up unless you go looking for it, and what comes your way without looking for it can be adjusted or turned off in Options, in the Life, reputation and consequences section. By default it is set to mild.

How the city sees you: what standing brings

  • Pay. You earn ten percent more in honest jobs if people like you, and twenty if you're a model citizen. The bank gives the model citizen double interest.
  • Shops. In shops, in bars and restaurants and in gun shops you get a 5 percent discount if people like you and 10 if you're a model citizen. If the neighbors avoid you, they charge you more: 7 percent on mild, 12 on normal and 18 on demanding.
  • Credit. In shops, bars and restaurants, pharmacies and clothing stores, if you're short of money and people like you, they put it on your tab: up to 60 euros, or up to 150 for a model citizen. You pay it whenever you want on the phone and, if you let a week go by, it's collected automatically with the bills.
  • The neighbors. If they like you, they sometimes look the other way at a minor offense, and then it neither counts nor lowers your standing. If they avoid you, they're keener to call the police and the crime counts more.
  • The police. From 50 standing they overlook some of your offenses, as always. Also, if people like you and your record is clean, once a day an officer gives you the benefit of the doubt over a minor offense. With a bad reputation or a criminal record, at the normal and demanding levels, an officer stops you now and then and makes you lose a few minutes.
  • At the demanding level, where people avoid you, some shops refuse to serve you on some days.

How the street sees you: what street reputation brings

  • Bodyguards in the taverns charge you up to 12 percent less if your name rings a bell, and more if nobody knows you.
  • Bribes go better the more street reputation you have: the one for the officer who has caught you and the one for the inspector who has found something in your business.
  • With 20 street reputation, gangs respect your businesses. They also do if you have your own gang, a neighborhood, a pact or an escort.
  • Without any of that, anyone with a business is easy prey: every now and then a gang comes by asking for money "for protection". It's a pending matter with a deadline, and you decide: pay for a few weeks; report it, which goes better the more the neighbors like you; refuse and hold out, with the risk that your business wakes up damaged and earns less for a few days; or stand up to them, if you have some street reputation or an escort. If people like you, a neighbor warns you the day before.

Criminal record

Every arrest goes on your record, more serious the more you were wanted: a minor offense with one or two wanted levels, a medium one with three and a serious one with four or five. The fine you pay on the spot to an officer is also recorded, as a minor offense.

  • What they close off: joining the police, positions of trust and, if they're serious, loans and mortgages. While you have a record, every crime counts a bit more. If you have a permanent contract, your boss finds out: a warning, or dismissal if the offense is serious or the job is a position of trust.
  • They expire on their own: minor ones after 10 days, medium ones after 25 and serious ones after 50.
  • They can be reduced in two ways. With community service, at a police station, at a soup kitchen or from the phone: four unpaid hours take five days off the most serious one, once a day. And with a lawyer, who appeals the conviction: it drops one degree, or disappears if it was minor, only once per entry.
  • You can check them on the phone and at any police station, with what is on record, when each one expires and what it's closing off to you.

What you did before this version doesn't count: a game already in progress starts with a clean record.

Permanent contract: a professional career

At the job centers, as well as the usual one-off jobs, there are permanent contracts. They're optional: without a contract, you work when you want and nobody holds you to account.

  • There are five trades: hospitality, shops and warehouse, shipyards and repair shops, transport, and emergency and rescue services, which is a position of trust and requires a clean record. Each one says which jobs count as a shift: a working day is one shift, and with a vehicle, three services make one shift.
  • When you sign you get a boss with a name and a personality, a number of shifts to work every seven days and a payroll. You get paid for each shift as always; the payroll arrives separately, at the bank, at the end of the period.
  • At the end of each period, your boss evaluates you: what counts most is whether you've worked your shifts and, also, how you've done them, which depends on your mood and your experience. With a good rating you get your payroll plus a bonus; with an adequate rating, the payroll; with a poor rating, a warning and a reduced payroll; and if you haven't shown up, a penalty with no pay. Two days before the period ends, if you're short of shifts, your boss reminds you.
  • With three warnings you're fired, and that company wants nothing to do with you for a few days. A good evaluation wipes out a warning.
  • With three good evaluations you're offered the next position, if you meet what it requires: a degree and, in the higher ones, good standing. There are four positions in each trade. The top two carry real responsibility: now and then, at the end of a shift, you have to make a decision about your people, a customer or the accounts, and what you choose counts in the evaluation.
  • If you leave on good terms after three periods without warnings, your boss signs you a letter of recommendation: in your next job you start one position higher, if you have what it requires, and the bank looks on you more favorably.

Studies with a degree

At the academies, as well as the one-off courses that raise a skill, there are degrees. Each one takes several sessions, one a day, of three hours, and each session is paid for. You can study and work on the same day.

  • There are seven: hospitality, retail, industrial trades, lifeguarding and first aid, professional transport, administration and management, and business management.
  • They open up the best positions in permanent contracts. The administration degree opens the manager positions and the expansion of your businesses, and requires some standing. The business management degree, which requires the administration one first and quite a lot of standing, opens the highest positions and the top level of your businesses.
  • When you finish, a fanfare plays, your standing goes up and the degree is yours forever. The game then tells you which permanent contract it's useful for and where it's signed, which is at the Job center. A degree doesn't open a business: it lets you get promoted at work, and some let you upgrade the businesses you already have.

Your businesses: a company that grows

Businesses are bought as always, by going to them, and now they're also run. When you go into a business of yours, its menu has the management options:

  • Upgrades. Three levels above the starting one: the renovation, the expansion, which requires the administration degree, and making it a well-known brand in the region, which requires the business management degree and 60 standing. Each level makes it earn more and take one more employee.
  • Employees. Each one earns a daily wage, which comes out of the till, and makes the business earn more than they cost. With staff hired, the business holds up even if you don't drop by.
  • The business's name among the customers. It rises if you spend two hours tending it, once a day, and little by little with employees and low prices. It drops if more than five days go by without you showing up. With a good name it earns up to 25 percent more; with a bad name, less. If you have staff hired, the business holds up without you: even if you don't go, its name stays around 45, a little below normal, which barely takes anything off what it earns, and as soon as you come back and tend it, it rises again. With nobody hired, on the other hand, it sinks. So, with employees in each one, you can have businesses in several towns without going to all of them every few days.
  • Low, normal or high prices: with high prices you earn more on each sale and the name drops; with low prices, the opposite.
  • Normal or extended hours: opening more hours earns more, but needs at least one employee.
  • Inspections. Every so often one comes by one of your businesses. If everything is in order, the inspector signs, leaves and the business gains name.
  • Dirty money. What you earn from drugs, robberies, contracts from the board and the tributes from your neighborhoods is money you couldn't justify, and the game keeps track of it: you can see it on the phone, in Life, reputation and consequences. You can't just take it to the bank: if you have dirty money, the bank only accepts up to three thousand euros in cash a day without asking questions; above that, it asks for explanations, doesn't accept the rest and alerts the tax office, which brings forward the inspection of your businesses. There are four ways to launder it:
    • Little by little at the bank, without going over the daily limit.
    • At the casino: Launder money into chips. You buy chips with cash, play a little and cash out as winnings. Twelve percent is lost along the way, and no more than five thousand euros a day.
    • With the discreet fixer, at the casino and in the clubs: you give him at least five thousand euros in cash, he keeps a quarter and, three days later, deposits the rest in your bank, clean. Once in a while he disappears with the money.
    • With a business of yours as a front, as explained just below.
    All this counts with consequences and taxes turned on; without them, dirty money carries no weight. On an online server, the server keeps track of the money.
  • Front. This is where the two paths cross. The money from robberies, contracts from the board and the tributes from your neighborhoods is money you couldn't justify, and the game keeps track of it, which fades on its own over the days. A business set up as a front puts part of that money through its till every night, in exchange for a commission. If an inspection comes while the front is on, or while you have a lot of unjustified money, there's a problem: pay the fine and leave everything clean; try to bribe the inspector, which if it goes wrong brings the fine, a serious criminal record entry and the front page of the newspaper; or, if you have a lawyer, have them appeal it and cut it in half.

A newly bought business, and the ones you already had, earn exactly the same as always as long as you don't change anything.

The bank: loans and mortgages

  • The bank looks at your creditworthiness: your standing, your stable income, which is your payroll and what your businesses earn, and your criminal record, missed payments and seizures.
  • Personal loan. You request it at any bank, in Loans, mortgages and obligations. They offer you up to a certain amount, which they pay into your account, and you pay it back in eight installments, one every seven days, with more or less interest depending on your creditworthiness.
  • Mortgage. You request it when buying: in the house or business you want, next to buying in cash, there's buying with a mortgage. You pay 30 percent as a down payment and the rest in sixteen installments. The house or business is yours from the signing.
  • Installments are collected automatically, first from your bank account and then from what you have on you, with a warning two days before. If there isn't enough, they add a surcharge, try again three days later and you lose standing. After three missed payments in a row, seizure: they don't take anything from you all at once, but they keep half of your payroll and of what your businesses earn until the debt is settled, and in the meantime the bank won't lend to you.
  • Any loan can be paid off in one go, with a small discount.

Taxes and bills

Every seven days you're charged for the electricity and water of your homes, their property tax and a tax on what your businesses have earned in that time. They're small amounts: on mild, about 21 euros per home, a bit more for the expensive ones, and less than four percent of what the businesses earn. You get a warning two days before with the amount, it's collected automatically and you can check it whenever you want on the phone and at the bank. If there isn't enough money, it's left owing with a surcharge; after three missed payments, the same seizure as with loans. A new game, and one you had already started, have fourteen days of grace before the first charge. With the Taxes and bills option set to no, there are no bills, taxes or inspections.

Doing good, and temptations

As well as the usual donations, volunteering and street events, every now and then, on foot and with nothing else going on, you come across a situation: a wallet on the ground, someone who has fallen, two people forcing a shutter, a fight between neighbors, a neighbor loaded with bags, a lost child, someone sleeping rough or a forgotten phone. A menu opens and you decide: help in one way or another, go on your way or take advantage. Helping raises your standing and your mood; sometimes it costs a while, a few euros or a first aid kit, and once in a while it goes wrong. Taking advantage gives easy money and, if someone saw you, the neighborhood will know. With Escape you go on your way. They appear with the street events option turned on, and never in the middle of a mission, a job or a chase.

The double life

You can be both: liked by the neighbors and respected on the street. It has its rules.

  • If you have something to lose, which is a contract, a business, a loan or the neighbors' esteem, whatever gets known about your other life is noted down. It rises every time you're seen committing a crime, and quite a lot with each arrest and with an inspection that finds something on you. It drops on its own with quiet days.
  • What nobody sees doesn't count: discretion is the first protection.
  • When the rumor is already going around, the game tells you. If it keeps rising and you have a good name or a contract, a journalist prepares a story: it's a pending matter with a deadline. You can pay them to keep it quiet, let it come out or, if you have a lawyer, have the lawyer stop it.
  • If it comes out in the newspaper, you lose standing, your businesses lose name and your boss finds out: a warning, or dismissal if the job is a position of trust. On the street, though, you gain street reputation.
  • Ways to protect yourself: don't get seen; a business as a front for the money; bribes, with their risk; and a lawyer, who is hired on the phone for a weekly amount and, as long as you pay, makes everything that gets known weigh half as much, holds back the press and appeals the fines.

The straight path: a story without crimes

From the start there are two paths, and the game tells you so when you leave your aunt's after seeing her for the first time. The street: Encarna's errands take you to Chispas's repair shop and to the usual story. The straight path: at the Puerto Bravo Job center, Aurora, the career advisor, is waiting for you with a story of her own in nine chapters that can be done entirely without committing a single crime. You can follow both at the same time.

  • It tells how someone who has just got out of prison makes a place for themselves: the first contract, the first payroll, going back to studying, having a business, the neighbors, the gang asking for money and the inspector asking for an envelope.
  • Some chapters wait for your life to move forward: having a contract and having worked a few shifts, earning a degree, gaining some standing or having a business. While something is missing, the phone tells you what it is.
  • It has temptations and decisions, and four endings: the honest one, the cautious one and, for whoever accepts the gang's deal and leads a double life, two more.
  • Its missions don't start on their own. You go into the place as always and the mission is the last option in its menu, before Go back; the game reminds you when you go in. In a place with no menu, like the town hall, the game asks. Completing them raises your standing, not your street reputation.
  • Its scenes are played with recorded voices, like those in the rest of the story: each character has their own, they call you by your name and speak to you as a man or as a woman. With a character you created, the lines that say your name are generated by the game on your computer.

The options

In Options, in the Life, reputation and consequences section:

  • Consequence level: off, mild, normal or demanding. By default, mild. Off leaves the game as in earlier versions: standing only counts for pay, bank interest and the police; businesses earn the same as always; nobody asks your business for money and there is no double life. On mild, the bad things weigh a little more than half as much as on normal. On demanding, half as much again, and shops that won't serve you appear. The perks are the same at every level.
  • Taxes and bills: yes or no. By default, yes.
  • Criminal record: yes or no. By default, yes.
  • Love life suitable for everyone: yes or no. By default, yes.
  • Social life is lived in person: yes or no. By default, yes. It is explained in the section Social life is lived in person.
  • Opening hours: no, people, or people and venues. By default, no. It is explained in the section Opening hours, optional.

At any level you can sign a contract, study, take out a loan and upgrade your businesses: that's your choice.

On an online server

On a server the money is refereed by the server, so there it's the server that decides, the same for everyone, and not your consequence level.

  • Permanent contracts: they're signed at the job center as always, with their boss, their shifts, their evaluation, their warnings and their promotions. The difference is the money: the server counts the shifts that are valid, the calendar is the server's and the payroll is paid by the server, with its own figure, straight into your bank. It doesn't use up the hourly earnings cap. If the period ends while you're away, the evaluation reaches you when you log back in.
  • If the server is from an earlier version, or its owner has turned off payrolls, there are no permanent contracts there, and the game tells you. The one you bring already signed stays as it is.
  • Studying for a degree is always possible: it only costs your character money.
  • Criminal record entries are noted down and expire with the server's days, and they count for positions of trust. They belong to your character: they don't change how the server's police treat you.
  • How shops treat you according to your standing, if the server has it turned on: a small discount if people like you and a surcharge if they avoid you, the same for everyone, whatever level you have.
  • These don't exist there: loans, mortgages, taxes and bills, business management (businesses earn what the server sets), credit at shops, extortion, the double life and street situations. The game tells you if you try.
  • The story of the straight path doesn't move forward there either: it's for playing offline.
  • When you leave the server everything goes back to how you have it in Options: nothing the server turns off stays turned off.

The phone, in Life, reputation and consequences, tells you what there is on the server you're on.

With a controller

All this is done through menus: the phone, the job center, the academy, the bank, the police station and your businesses. With the controller you go through them and choose like any other menu, and there are no new keys.

What you hear

When you go into a venue the door sounds and, while you're inside, its background ambience plays; when you leave, the door again. A rooster announces dawn, an owl dusk and the chimes of a bell midnight.

Life and its consequences have four sounds of their own: a door slam and three falling notes when you're fired; knuckles knocking, papers and a stamp when an inspection comes to one of your businesses; a receipt printer and a low note when you're charged a bill, a tax or an installment; and a short fanfare when you earn a degree. A promotion to a higher position sounds like the usual rank promotion.

20. Mood and street reputation

The V key tells you your status: health, money, weapon, wanted level and vehicle. Your mood rises when you eat well, rest, win, do sport or help, and drops with injuries, arrests and failures; with a low mood you perform worse. Street reputation rises with heists and opens doors in the criminal world. Standing among the neighbors is the other side of the coin: it rises with an honest life. They are two independent scores, and what each one brings is in Life, reputation and consequences, in the section on honest life and on the phone.

21. Social life: people, dates and partners

The people you can have a relationship with: meeting them, meeting up, dates, being a couple and living together. It works with adult content and with the love life suitable for everyone; what only exists with adult content is in the next chapter.

Love life suitable for everyone

Without turning on adult content, the phone has Love life: the same people you can meet, with chats, dates, gifts, a kiss or a dance, becoming a couple, couple plans and giving them money to buy something. If the relationship is going very well and you've had quite a few dates, you can ask them to get engaged; once engaged, you can live together in one of your homes; and a few days later, get married, with a simple wedding the neighbors look on kindly. None of this has adult content, which stays behind its own option. With adult content turned on, your partner's profile is the usual one and also gains the engagement and the wedding. The Love life suitable for everyone option, which comes set to yes, removes it if you don't want it.

Social life is lived in person

With the Social life is lived in person option, which comes set to yes and is in Options, Life, reputation and consequences, the people in your social life are treated as in real life. It works the same for the love life suitable for everyone.

  • By phone, from each person's profile on the phone, wherever you are: call them, which brings you a little closer, once a day; talk to them in your own words; meet up at a place at a time; send them a gift, delivered, which costs the delivery and is appreciated a little less than in person; give them money; and break up.
  • In person: chat, which brings you closer than a call; suggest a date; give them a gift in person; a kiss, a dance and the other gestures; spend the night together; couple plans; ask them to be your partner, to get engaged or to move in with you; get married; and ask them to forgive you. You're face to face at the place where they usually are, at their time; at your home, if you live together and they're in; or while they're with you.
  • If you're not together, the profile tells you where they are and what you can do by phone.
  • What makes hours go by, like a date, the night or a plan, can't be done in the middle of a mission.
  • A date is lived. In person, Suggest a date and choosing the plan makes them come with you, and the GPS takes you to a place that suits that plan: a cinema for the movies, a bar, a tavern or a club for going out for drinks, a club or the fair for dancing, and a restaurant for dinner. The plan happens when you arrive, told in scenes, and afterwards you stay together until you say goodbye. Going for a walk is going together, with no fixed place.
  • With the option set to no, everything is done from the phone while in the same town, as before.

Meet up at a place at a time

  • In a person's profile, Meet up at a place at a time: you choose the plan and the time, among the ones they have free today and tomorrow, and the game looks for a place that suits that plan near where they usually are.
  • Half an hour before, the phone reminds you. If you arrive at the place up to half an hour before or after the time, they're waiting for you, and the plan is lived there, in scenes. If the plan is going for a walk, they come with you.
  • If you don't show up, you've stood them up, and they take it badly. Letting them know beforehand, by canceling the meetup, upsets them somewhat, but much less.
  • On the phone, in Social life or Love life, Your meetups tells you who you've arranged to meet, where and at what time. Enter marks the place on the GPS, and it can also be canceled.
  • The people from the street you have in Your contacts can also be invited to meet up for a drink: at a bar in their town, at the time you choose. If you go, you pay, your mood goes up, you become better friends and they tell you what's being said around. Friendship, from zero to a hundred, appears in the list.
