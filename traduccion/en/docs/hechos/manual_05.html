The railway, and the trains that pass

In each town with a train station there is a track that crosses it from side to side, in a straight line, along the street or avenue that runs in front of the station. The station menu tells you which one.

  • About every minute and a half a train comes in at one end of the track, stops at the platform for a few seconds, whistles and leaves at the other end. You hear it coming from wherever it is, louder and louder, and then fading away: you know where the track runs without having set foot on it.
  • The track can be crossed on foot, like a street. When you step on it the game says Train tracks, and your footsteps sound like metal, as on the tram tracks.
  • If you are on the track and the train is coming toward you, it whistles nonstop and the game warns you which way it is coming from. You have a few seconds: get onto the sidewalk. The train is faster and harder than the tram: if it hits you, you lose a lot of health and you are thrown off the track. It leaves a vehicle badly damaged.
  • When stopped at the platform it doesn't run anyone over.
  • When you travel by bus from one town to another, a train almost always overtakes you on the track that runs alongside the road: you hear it pass from one ear to the other.

If you use auto-walk, with U, your character crosses the tracks without stopping: if you hear the bell, stop with any arrow key.

Train: for going to other towns

Each town that can be reached by road has its train station, which is a different place from the bus station and sounds with the puffing of a locomotive.

  • From a train station you can go to all the others. The neighboring towns come first, as they are the cheapest: the ticket costs more the farther you go.
  • The train doesn't reach the islands or the platform: there is sea in between.
  • The trip has a ride: the whistle on departure and the rattle of the carriage. It lasts longer the farther you go, and with Enter you arrive right away.

It works the same for towns added by mods.

Airports and airfields: fly anywhere

The four cities have an airport on the outskirts, in one corner of the city. The towns that can be reached by road have an airfield: a short dirt strip, also on the outskirts. Both sound with the public address chime: three descending notes.

  • Passenger plane from one airport to another, for 180 euros. It is for long trips between cities. It has a ride: the takeoff and then the hum of the cabin.
  • Scheduled light plane, with a pilot, to the towns: from an airport or from another airfield, for 120 euros. And from a town's airfield, to any city or any other town. It is the flight you get whenever the strip at one of the two ends is short.
  • Scheduled helicopter, with a pilot, to the islands and the platform, where there is no room for a strip: it lands at their heliport. It leaves from any airport or airfield.
  • From an island's heliport, as a passenger, you can fly back to the cities' heliports and the towns' airfields.
  • At airfields only light planes can be rented; rental helicopters are at the airports.
  • Rent a light plane, for 600 euros, or a helicopter, for 900. They leave it for you on the runway, next to the terminal, with the GPS marking it: get in with F and fly it yourself.
  • If you arrive flying a light plane or a helicopter, with Enter at the airport you get the airfield menu: the flight plan to go to any other town, fuel and the hangar. With real flight turned off in Options, you fly in one jump to any other airport, airfield or heliport, for free.
  • The menu of each airport, airfield and heliport also has the hangar, commercial flights for those who have a pilot license, and How flying works.

The Gaviota light plane can also be bought, where the helicopter is. It is flown the same way: by hand, above buildings and traffic, and it only lands on clear ground. It is faster and cheaper.

Swimming and diving

At sea you no longer have to stay on board all the time:

  • With the boat stopped, F drops you into the water. The boat stays where it is.
  • You swim with the arrow keys, slowly, and you hear each stroke. Swimming uses up energy; staying still, hardly any. The V key tells you how much you have left. If you run out of energy, a fishing boat picks you up half drowned, as in a shipwreck.
  • X dives you under to swim underwater and brings you back up. Underwater everything sounds muffled and with bubbles, and you can hold your breath for about thirty seconds. While diving, you sometimes find something of value on the bottom, like some old coins or a doubloon: it goes into your backpack, if it fits, and can be sold at a pawn shop. If you run out of air, you come up swallowing water and lose health.
  • To get back on the boat, go up to it and press F. If you swim to a dock, you get out onto land.

12. Making a living

  • Missions: marked with a warm, pulsing sound. Press Enter at the place.
  • Vehicle jobs, with J: taxi, ambulance, bus, patrol car, truck, ice cream van and, at sea, courier work with any boat.
  • Trucking, step by step: get into a truck and press J. The load is already inside: you don't have to pick anything up. The job is to take it to a shop in another town, and the game says which. The GPS guides you by road to the town's exit; there, without getting out, press Enter twice to take the road, and on the other side the GPS carries on by itself. If the destination isn't the neighboring town, you cross the one in between the same way. When you stop in front of the destination, the load is delivered by itself, you get paid and you are given another job. Don't take the bus or get out of the truck: if you leave it, the service ends. If you clear the GPS by accident, G marks the destination again, and O reminds you of the job. With the GPS autopilot, the Y key, the truck drives itself to each exit.
  • Tavern job boards: contracts of many kinds, harder and better paid each time. It is explained further down, in The job board.
  • Robberies: besides shops and banks, you can rob any business that has a till. It is explained further down, in Robbing any business.
  • Police: with the mode turned on in the options, you can join the force and work as an officer. It is explained further down, in Working as a police officer.
  • Car orders: at a scrapyard, on foot, they ask you for a specific model.
  • Boxing: at gyms, fist fights with a growing prize.
  • Shooting range: at gun shops. A target sounds on one side and you press that arrow key.
  • Street events: now and then there is a mugging, some gang members or a lost briefcase nearby. Helping is optional and has a reward.
  • Delivering pizzas: at Vesubio Pizzeria.
  • Fishing: at sea with P, or from the breakwaters. It is sold at fish markets and fishmongers.
  • Scrapyards: they buy any vehicle.
  • Races: on the Industrial Estate Straight, in San Lucero, in Villaseca and at the regatta buoy.
  • Businesses: buy them and collect every midnight. You don't need to go and get the money, unless you prefer to. There are three ways to collect, and you switch from one to another on the phone, in Properties, or in the menu of any of your businesses:
    • It is brought to you in person: this is the default. Every midnight it reaches your pocket, wherever you are.
    • It is deposited in the bank: it goes into your account, where it also earns interest. To spend it you withdraw it at a bank or an ATM.
    • You go and collect it yourself: it stays in the till of each business. When you go into the business, Collect the till gives you whatever is there, with ten percent more for coming in person. Each till holds seven days; anything beyond that is lost.
    When you change the method, whatever was left in the tills reaches you anyway.
  • Casino with slot machines, blackjack and roulette, bingo and dice.
  • Comarca Lottery, with a draw every day at nine in the evening:
    • Each ticket costs 10 euros and has a five-digit number. The game reads it digit by digit. You can hold up to five tickets per draw.
    • They are bought at lottery offices, where you can ask for a random ticket or type in the number you want, and also in shops, bars and taverns, which have three numbers on display each day.
    • What you buy before nine plays that night. What you buy after, the following night.
    • At nine the game says the winning number, wherever you are, but it doesn't tell you whether you have won: you check that yourself. On the phone, in Lottery, you have the next draw, the jackpot, your tickets, Check your tickets and the numbers of the last seven draws. If you are sleeping or traveling and miss the time, the draw takes place anyway.
    • Prizes go by the last digits that match the winning number, consecutive from the end: the last one, the refund, 10 euros; the last two, 60; three, 600; four, 6000; and all five, the jackpot.
    • The jackpot starts at 20000 euros and grows every day nobody matches all five digits. Sometimes someone in the region wins it: the game says where it fell, and the jackpot starts over.
    • Prizes are collected where tickets are sold, with Check and collect your tickets. Shops, bars and taverns pay up to 600 euros; bigger prizes, only at a lottery office.
    • A winning ticket expires seven days after the draw.
  • Collectibles: cassette tapes, bottles with messages and doubloons, which sound like a tinkle that rises and falls.
  • More jobs with the J key: firefighter with the fire truck, tow truck driver, and courier on a bicycle, scooter or motorcycle.
  • Companion dog: adopt one at an animal shelter. It follows you everywhere, growls when there are enemies nearby and, on foot, with the Y key, searches by scent for the nearest item and shows you the way.
  • Looking after the dog. With Shift + Y, with Enter next to it, or on the phone, in Your dog, its menu opens:
    • The first line says how it is: whether it is hungry and how much it trusts you.
    • Pet: raises its trust little by little.
    • Feed: uses up one portion of dog food. Dog food costs 12 euros and is bought at the animal shelter and in shops. The dog gets hungry as the hours go by: it lets you know with a whimper when it is hungry and, if it runs out of strength, it stops searching until it eats.
    • Send home: it goes to your home and waits for you there, with food and water. You need to have a house. It can also be left there from the house menu.
    • Call: when it is at home, it comes back to you from this same menu or from the house menu.
  • If you hurt the dog, it doesn't turn on you or get lost: it becomes afraid of you. With one hit it yelps and cowers; with two it stops trusting you, follows you from a distance and doesn't warn you about enemies or search. You fix it by making peace: petting, food, a few hours of play or some time at home.
  • Friendly fire. By default, your bullets and explosives also hurt your own side, as in real life. You can hit a bodyguard or a member of your gang twice: they warn you. On the third, in a row, they turn on you; if a couple of minutes go by, they forget it. If you don't want that to be able to happen even by accident, in Options, Combat and difficulty, set Friendly fire to no: then they can't be aimed at or hurt.
  • Turf wars: from the phone you can conquer the neighborhoods of the three cities. There are three waves of gang members, and each conquered neighborhood pays you every midnight. It is explained further down, in Territories at a glance; and in the game, on the phone, Territories, with the first option, How this works.
  • Stock market: at any bank you can buy and sell shares in five companies. You earn by selling for more than you paid, and you lose by selling for less.
    • Prices move every midnight: some companies a little, like Poniente Shipping, and others a lot, like Cala Brava Canned Goods. Now and then a piece of news makes a company jump, up or down. What happens in the story also moves them.
    • Some pay dividends every seven days: a percentage of what each share is worth, which reaches your bank account without selling anything. Each company's sheet says whether it pays and how much.
    • While you own shares, every midnight the game tells you what they are worth, how much they have changed since yesterday and how much you are up or down since you bought them; and if one of your companies has news, it tells you about it.
    • The stock market menu says, for each company, its price, how much it has changed since yesterday, how many shares you have, what they are worth and how much you are up or down; at the top, what your whole portfolio is worth and the dividends collected. It also has the latest news and How the stock market works.
    • The companies, their prices, how much they move, their dividends and their news are in the game data and can be changed with the mod editor.
  • Sound memory machine, at fairs and arcades: repeat with the arrow keys a sequence of notes that gets longer and longer.
  • Treasure hunting: diving at the wreck and the reef, or with a metal detector on some beaches. It is a game of hot or cold by ear: the beep speeds up and gets higher as you get closer.
  • Less honest: robbing shops, pedestrians and banks, or blowing open an armored van.

Bars and taverns also have four games: seven and a half, dominoes, the coin guessing game and the slot machine in the corner.

  • Dominoes: a hand against a regular, with all twenty-eight tiles, for twenty or a hundred euros. On your turn, the menu offers you the tiles you can play and next to which end. If you have none, you draw from the boneyard. Whoever runs out of tiles wins; if nobody can play, whoever has the fewest points in hand. The game says on each turn the ends on the table and how many tiles each player has left.
  • The coin guessing game: each player hides from zero to three coins in their fist and says how many they think there are between the two of you. Whoever guesses right takes the bet, of ten or fifty euros. If nobody guesses right, it is played again.
  • Slot machine: the same one as in the casino.

Seven and a half is played with a Spanish deck, against the bank. You bet ten, fifty or two hundred euros. You are dealt a card and you decide whether to ask for another or stand. Face cards are worth half a point and the rest, their number. If you go over seven and a half, you lose. When you stand, the bank draws cards until it ties you or goes bust; if it ties you, it wins. Winning pays double, and winning with exactly seven and a half, triple.

Collectibles also give a prize of a thousand euros and some street reputation for every five you find.

The job board: contracts of many kinds

The tavern board doesn't always offer the same thing. When you open it there are three jobs pinned up, and you choose one. They stay the same all day; when you complete one, or the next day, they change. Each one says what it is about, where it is and how much it pays. There are eight kinds:

  • Take out: the usual target, in one place, with their bodyguards.
  • Target with a motorized escort: the same, but part of the escort patrols the area in a vehicle and arrives when the trouble starts. You can hear them coming.
  • Target on the run: they have found out and are fleeing in a vehicle. You have to catch up with them in another one and wreck it, ramming or shooting from yours with Q and Control.
  • In their lair: the target doesn't leave a building. You go up to the door on foot, and when you go in they all come out after you.
  • Tailing the errand runner: nobody knows where they are hiding. You follow their errand runner on foot, by ear, without getting too close and without losing them, and they lead you to the hideout.
  • A warning: this one doesn't have to be killed. You teach them a lesson until they give up, and that's it. Their friends, as you see fit. If you kill them, the job fails; better with your fists or a melee weapon.
  • Recover the stolen goods: you take out whoever is guarding them, pick up what was taken and bring it to the tavern.
  • Protect a witness: you get there before the people coming for them, who arrive in a vehicle; you take them out and bring the witness, on foot or by car, to a safe place.

At first only the simplest ones come up; the others appear once you have completed a few contracts. Each completed contract makes the following ones harder and better paid, as always. They are all paid by going back to the tavern. The kinds of contract, what they pay and who defends them are in the game data, and with the mod editor they can be changed or added.

Robbing any business with a till

Robbery is no longer just for shops and banks. You can rob any business that has a till: garages, gyms, gun shops, pharmacies, bars, taverns, restaurants, clothing stores, pawn shops, cinemas, hotels, dealerships, casinos and the rest. The option is at the end of each business's menu, named Rob this business, and F1 says whether the till is guarded and what wanted level you will get. The garage, which until now only served you if you came with a vehicle, can also be entered on foot.

  • You need a firearm in your hand, and you can only do it once a day at each business.
  • If the till isn't guarded, as in a pharmacy or a shop, it's as always: it is emptied instantly, the alarm goes off and the police look for you.
  • If it is, as in a gym, a gun shop or a casino, you first have to take out the guards; then you pick up the till, which is left on the floor, and lose the police. The money is yours when they stop looking for you.
  • What is in the till changes every day and at each business. The more there is, the more guards and the higher the wanted level.
  • The business remembers you. For a few days, between two and four, they won't serve you: some shut the door on you; others let you in, recognize you and call the police, and from then on they shut it too. Shops do the same. The bank doesn't: it has its own robbery, as before.

On an online server it works the same as robbing shops. What is in each till and who guards it is in the game data, in the businesses' till; the days they remember you, in the robbery data.

If you have a gang, below the robbery option there is Plan the heist with your gang: you hand out roles among your people before starting. It is covered in the section Prepared heists: everyone with their role.

Working as a police officer, optional mode

This is a mode that comes turned off. It is turned on in Options, The street: people, police and gangs, Mode: be able to work as a police officer. With it off, none of this appears. It is only available when playing solo.

  • Joining: at any police station, Join the police. It costs a course of 400 euros and eight hours, and the neighbors need to think somewhat well of you and nobody can be looking for you. You start as a trainee officer.
  • The shift: at a police station, Start the shift. You are given a patrol car, which waits for you at the door with its locator sound. You can also get into any patrol car and press J. J in the car, or End the shift at a police station, ends it, and the borrowed car is returned.
  • The radio: on duty, every so often the radio sounds and dispatch passes you a call, with the place and the seconds you have to get there. The GPS takes you. The O key repeats the call. If you arrive late, another unit takes over and you don't get paid. And if you arrive, start and then go far away, the call is considered lost.
  • Robbery at a business: when you arrive one or two people come out with the till. Some give themselves up when they see you, others run off and others stand their ground.
  • Street fight: two people hitting each other. You can hear them. If you order them to stop, they usually break it up and leave; sometimes one turns on you.
  • Reckless driver: a vehicle being driven the wrong way. You stop it by getting behind it with the siren on, which is P, and holding on for a few seconds. It almost always stops; if not, it turns into a chase.
  • Chase: a vehicle that isn't going to stop. You have to ram it or shoot it until it can't go on. Then they either stay inside or run off.

How to stop someone fleeing in a car without the crime being yours: follow them with the GPS, which already has them marked, and ram them with the patrol car or shoot at the vehicle; with Q you lock onto it. While they are fleeing, and within three hundred meters of you, neither crashes nor shots count as a crime, even if they get ahead of you. When the vehicle is badly damaged, it stops or the driver runs off: then you order them to stop on foot. What does count is hitting people or other cars: each offense is a reprimand, and at the third they take your badge and the police come after you. On your first chase on duty, the game explains it to you.

How each thing is resolved:

  • To order someone to stop, on foot, press H near the suspect. Sometimes that is enough, more so if you have a firearm in your hand. Shouting over and over doesn't help: a few seconds have to pass between one order and the next.
  • Subduing: whoever resists is subdued. When they have less than half their health left, they surrender instead of going down. Fists and melee weapons are the safest.
  • Arresting: next to someone who has surrendered, Enter puts the handcuffs on them.
  • Fining: next to the vehicle that has stopped, Enter. You can give them the fine, of which you get a share; leave it at a warning, which the neighbors like; or arrest them, which is only appropriate if they fled.

What you earn and what you lose:

  • Each call resolved is paid, more if you arrive quickly and more the higher your rank. Every six calls you are promoted: officer, senior officer, deputy inspector, inspector and chief. You see it in the police station menu, and it counts on the phone, in Your career.
  • On duty, using force against the suspects of a call is not a crime, and the police don't come after you.
  • Killing someone you could have arrested pays half. And if they hadn't attacked you, or had already surrendered, it is excessive force: a reprimand.
  • Any other crime someone sees also counts. A small offense, like taking a car or shooting for no reason, is a reprimand, and they don't look for you. With three reprimands, or with a serious crime, they take your badge: the shift ends, the police come after you like anyone else and you can't join again for a week.
  • If you start a mission, leave the town or go out to sea, the shift simply ends, like any job: you are still an officer, and you can start another shift at the police station wherever you are.

Without a badge, the patrol car is still good for the usual vigilante job. The calls, what they pay, the ranks and everything else are in the game data.

Skills and achievements

  • Driving, aim, stamina and fishing go up by themselves with practice, up to level ten.
  • Maximum health goes up by training at a gym.
  • There are 40 achievements. You can check them on the phone.

13. Missions

How story missions and jobs work: the vehicle, the ones played by ear, what happens if you fail and the decisions that change the ending.

Missions and your vehicle

  • Before accepting a mission, its summary on the phone, in Available missions, says what happens with the vehicle: whether you are given one, whether you have to take a specific one, whether you can do it with yours, whether you have to go in one or whether there is a chase, for which it's best to bring a fast one. It is also said when you reach the contact's door, before accepting it.
  • Choosing a vehicle: in delivery missions where the vehicle only carries the load, you can choose between the one they give you and one of yours; the game tells you when it starts. They are Urgent Delivery, Urgent Harvest, Fallen from the Sky, The fish van and Crates for the Food Bank. If you want another of yours that you don't have at hand, ask for it on the phone, in My vehicles.
  • You also choose in other places: at the dealership, which model you buy and, if you take it without paying, which one you ask for the keys to, as the more expensive it is, the more the police look for you; and when asking for a driver, whether they come with one of your vehicles or with one of the gang's, and which of the gang's.
  • When the mission gives you a vehicle, yours is not lost. When that step starts, the game puts the vehicle you are driving, or the one you have just left nearby, in your name, and tells you it is parked and safe. You have it on the phone, in My vehicles, and from there you can send it to the garage without going back for it.
  • Some missions don't require a specific vehicle: what matters is carrying the load. In those, drive your vehicle to where the mission's vehicle is, stop next to it, and you do it with yours. When you deliver, you keep it and carry on in it. It has to be of the same class: for a helicopter mission you need something that flies, and a bicycle won't do for carrying a load.
  • Missions where you have to steal or deliver a specific vehicle still require that one.
