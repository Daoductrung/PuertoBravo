Missions played by ear

Besides going to a place, getting a vehicle or facing someone, there are six types of job designed for the ear. In all of them, the O key tells you how you are doing.

  • Following someone. They are walking and you can hear their footsteps. There is no GPS: you have to follow them by sound. If you get closer than six meters, or three and a half while crouching, they notice. If you stay more than fifty-five meters away for a while, you lose them. The game warns you when you are too close or when they are getting away from you.
  • Escorting someone. You go to pick them up; from then on they follow you on foot and get in with you if you take a vehicle. You both have to reach the destination.
  • Getting in without making noise. There are guards walking around the place, and you can hear them walk. Standing, they hear you at six meters; running, at eleven; crouching with X, at less than three. If they hear you, it's over. The game warns you when one is near you.
  • Route against the clock. Several stops in a fixed order, with a time limit for all of them. The GPS takes you from one to the next.
  • Watching a place. You have to stay near the place, within about twenty-five meters, for a while, listening to what goes on there. Every so often someone comes out to walk around and you hear their footsteps getting closer: while they are near, stay still or crouch with X. While crouching you can move. If they see you moving while standing, or if you stray from your post for a few seconds, you fail. When the time is up, the game tells you what you have found out.
  • Race against rivals. You drive a vehicle to the start and stop. After a three-second countdown, you have to pass through the checkpoints of the course and reach the last one before the rivals. You can hear the rivals' engines from where they are, so you know whether they are ahead of or behind you, and the game says your position each time it changes. If a rival gets there first, you fail.

In the main story there are six jobs of this kind, in the Jobs by Ear chapter, which start at the El Faro Newsroom. In Bahía del Ocaso there are another six, which start at the newspaper El Faro del Ocaso once you have done the mission The meter.

If you fail a mission: checkpoints

Long missions save a checkpoint each time you finish a stage. If you fail after that, the game offers three things: retry from the last checkpoint, which takes you back to the start of the stage you were in with what you had then; retry from the beginning of the mission; or leave it for now. There is no checkpoint while you are driving a mission vehicle that still has to be delivered: there, you go back to the previous stage.

When you retry, you go back with the money, health and weapons you had then. Your vehicles don't go back in time, so none is lost: they are wherever you left them, on the street or in the garage. If during the mission you took one out of the garage, it stays out; if you sent one to the garage, it stays stored, even if you sent it after the mission had started. The same goes for a boat of yours: if you were in it, it goes back to a mooring.

The vehicle you were in, or the one you had just left next to you, comes with you: it is parked next to the place you go back to, and the game says so. It doesn't come if it is the mission's, a boat or something that flies. When you retry, the game also reminds you how many vehicles you have on the street and in garages. If during the mission you bought a house and put a vehicle in its garage, the house is no longer yours and the money comes back, but the vehicle is not lost: it goes to another of your garages or, if there is no room, it stays on the street in your name.

If you fail the same mission three times, the option to skip it also appears: it counts as done, with no reward.

If you are arrested or pass out when you have already done what the mission asked for and only its final scene is left, the mission is not failed: after the police station or the hospital, the scene continues and the mission is completed.

In chases, the fleeing vehicle goes slower on easy and normal and, if it gets ahead of you, it eases off. When it starts, the game explains what you need: a fast vehicle, because on foot or with a slow one you can't catch it, and that the GPS and the beacon follow it. Every so often, while it is far away, the game tells you how many meters away it is, in what direction, on what street and where it is heading, and warns you when it is within range. O says that same thing as soon as you press it. To destroy it you can use bullets, with Q to lock onto it and Control to shoot from the car, grenades and ramming: the more damaged it is, the slower it goes.

Chase assist: it is in Options, Combat and difficulty, and comes turned on. It is a gentle aid. With it, the game says where it is every seven seconds instead of every twelve, and from twenty-five meters instead of forty-five; it calls out every turn the fleeing driver makes, with the street; the fleeing driver starts to ease off at sixty meters instead of ninety, and eases off completely at a hundred and ten instead of a hundred and fifty; and, if there is a time limit, you get fifteen percent more. When following someone on foot, it warns you more often, says when they turn a corner, you lose them at sixty-five meters instead of fifty-five, and it takes five seconds longer to lose sight of you. Without the assist, everything works as before. It doesn't apply on an online server. The numbers can be changed with the mod editor.

If a chase is giving you trouble, it isn't the same the second time: whoever already got away from you is more nervous and drives slower, a little more each time you fail, up to three times, and if there is a time limit you get a bit more.

When following someone on foot, they don't start walking until you find them. It doesn't matter how far away you are when you get the job: the GPS takes you to where they are, the game warns you when you are about forty meters away and then, after a few seconds, they set off walking. If you have already lost them before, it takes longer for them to lose sight of you.

Jobs around the region

These are twelve standalone jobs, spread across the towns, that are not part of the main story and can be done in any order after the first missions. They use every type of step: following someone, escorting, getting in without noise, watching, routes against the clock, a race, a robbery job and a trip out to sea. They start at the pharmacy, the park kiosk, the library, the museum, the fire station and the scrapyard in Puerto Bravo; at the Miner's Curve and the offices in Monteferro; at the bakery in Villaseca; at the arcade in San Lucero; at the marine shop in Cala Brava and at the inn in Fuentefría. The phone, in Missions, says which ones you have available.

Decisions, and different endings

In some missions the story asks you for a decision. You choose in a menu you can't leave without choosing, and F1 explains what each option means. It can't be undone. What you decide is noted in the journal on the phone and changes what comes next: which missions appear, how much money and street reputation you have, and how you get on with the gangs.

There are decisions spread throughout the story: what you do with the money you are offered, with an enemy who surrenders, with a treasure or with a gang that wants to talk. There are eight in the main story and one in six of the mods that come with the game. Almost all of them change your money, your street reputation or your mood; some, how you get on with a gang.

Four of those decisions open branches: depending on what you choose, some missions or others appear, each with its own ending, and some leave consequences that last forever, like an income you collect every midnight.

  • The councilman's envelope, in Puerto Bravo: three paths and three endings.
  • The buyer, in Villaseca, once you have done the town's stories: defend the cooperative, and become a partner, or sell it and cash in.
  • The Empty Throne, in San Lucero, once you have done its stories: leave El Caimán's business to the market, keep it yourself or take the books to the police.
  • Around the Circuit, in Bahía del Ocaso: publish the name or get paid to keep quiet.

The journal on the phone notes each decision and what it gives you or costs you every midnight.

The branches cross

The three crossroads of the base game, the one in Puerto Bravo, the one in Villaseca and the one in San Lucero, don't each go their own way:

  • Each branch remembers the others. When you finish the mission of a branch, whoever gave it to you comments on what you did in the other two places, if you have already decided: the editor knows whether you sold the cooperative, the inspector knows who is paying you in Puerto Bravo, and the gangs of one city know how you get on with those of the other.
  • When you have closed all three branches, the mission Three Letters, from the Crossed Accounts chapter, appears at La Gaviota Bar. Aunt Encarna reads you a letter from each place, different depending on what you chose, and tells you what she sees when she puts them together.
  • After that there is one more ending, which depends on what your decisions add up to. If you chose the honest option in all three places, The Region Stands Tall. If in none, Everything has an owner, with one last decision: share it out or keep it all. And if you did some of each, A bit of good, a bit of bad.

With the eighteen possible combinations, there is always one of those three endings, and only one.

The Puerto Bravo one is at the end of the Jobs by Ear chapter and leads to three different endings depending on what you choose. If you want to see the others, start another game, or use New game keeping what you earned.

14. Houses, money, saving and options

In your houses you can save, rest, hide and use the garage. There are two ways to store a vehicle: drive it up to the door and press Enter without getting out, or leave it parked nearby and go in on foot, because the house menu offers to put the vehicle you have left into the garage. The vehicle you get out of waits for you where you left it, even if you go far away, until you get into another one, and it is saved with the game. The ones that are yours are never lost, whichever ones you take: they are on the phone, in My vehicles. In the house menu, Name a vehicle names the one you have brought or any of those in the garage. You can also save anywhere from the pause menu or with F5, except during a mission. The game saves by itself at the end of each mission. If you fail a mission, you can retry it straight away without repeating the journey.

Money: on you, at home and in the bank

  • What you buy is paid with what you are carrying. If you don't have enough, they won't sell it to you, and the game tells you whether you have enough in the bank to withdraw.
  • You withdraw at banks, which also let you deposit, in hundreds, five hundreds, thousands or everything; and at ATMs, which are at gas stations, stations, airports and hotels, where you can only withdraw. In the mod editor, any place can have an ATM.
  • Money in the bank is never lost. What you carry, is: if you are arrested, if you are knocked down, or if you are mugged. At night, on foot and with money on you, someone may step out in front of you: you give them what you have, stand up to them or run away. This happens with street events turned on.
  • You can also keep money in the wardrobe of your houses. You don't lose it if you are mugged or arrested, but a house can be broken into: they take part of the money, the product and some item. With a safe, the money is secure; with an alarm, they break in much less.

The backpack and the home wardrobe

You carry a backpack with limited space: twenty slots. On the phone, in Inventory, the Backpack option says how many slots you have filled, and with Enter you open it: each item says whether it is stolen or found and how many slots it takes up, and Enter on one drops it on the ground.

  • What goes in the backpack: valuables, like a phone, a watch, a gold chain, a tablet, a camera or a laptop, and the product, if you play with adult content, which takes one slot for every ten units. The limit of a hundred units of product stays the same.
  • Where they come from. When you mug a pedestrian, besides the wallet, you sometimes keep something else. When you break into a parked car, sometimes there is something in the glove box. Some of those who go down leave something of value on the ground, which is picked up by itself when you walk over it, like money. And when diving, what you find on the bottom goes into the backpack. What you get diving counts as found; the rest, as stolen.
  • If it doesn't fit, it stays where it was, and the game says so.
  • Selling it: at pawn shops, the pawnbroker buys what you have in your backpack, one by one or all together. For found items he pays quite well; for stolen ones, much less, because he knows where they come from. The money from stolen goods is money you can't account for.
  • A bigger backpack: clothing stores sell one with forty slots, for 120 euros. What you are carrying goes into the new one.
  • The wardrobe of your houses: in the menu of each of your houses there is Wardrobe. It stores items from the backpack, product, weapons and money, and you take them out from there. What is in the wardrobe isn't taken by the police and isn't lost if you are knocked down, although the house can be broken into, and if someone informs on you, the police can search it. If you make product in a house's lab and it doesn't all fit, the rest stays in its wardrobe.
  • If you are arrested, the police keep the stolen goods and the product you are carrying. It is covered in the section Fine or bribe.
  • A house that stops being yours takes with it whatever was in its wardrobe.
  • The numbers, the phrases and the valuables, with their name, slots, value and where they come from, are in the game data, and can be changed or extended with the mod editor.

Renting out your houses

A house of yours that you don't use can earn you money. In its menu, Rent out the house says how much the tenant would pay per day, which depends on what the house is worth. The garage needs to be empty, your partner can't be living there and you need another free house to live in yourself.

  • From then on someone lives there, different each time, and every midnight their rent reaches you, a little different each day. It counts for taxes like what your businesses bring in.
  • Sometimes something breaks, like a broken water heater or a leak, and you pay for the repair. Occasionally the tenant is a day late and pays the next day.
  • While it is rented out you can't sleep, save the game or use the garage there: when you reach the door, the menu says who you are renting it to and how much you have collected.
  • To get it back, in that same menu, Take the house back: the tenant leaves and you return their deposit, which is two days' rent.
  • On an online server houses can't be rented out, because the money is managed by the server.

New game keeping what you earned

In the main menu, New game keeping what you earned starts the story from the beginning with what you had in a saved game: money, weapons, skills, houses, vehicles in garages, boats and achievements. Missions, territories and decisions start from zero. On each playthrough, mission enemies take fifteen percent more damage. The saved game you start from is not touched.

Games saved with earlier versions of the game are converted by themselves when you load them. A game saved with a newer version than the one you have won't load: the game tells you to update.

Options, by section

The options are grouped. Press Enter on a section to go in; inside, the last option is always Go back, and Escape also works. F1 explains the option you are on.

  • Language: it isn't a section, it is the first line of Options. It is changed with the arrow keys: automatic, your Windows language, or the one you choose. It is explained in section 2.
  • Sound and music: the five volumes, open my music folder, the ambient music with its own volume, the sound pack, volume leveling and the adjustment of each sound separately. On an online server, whether you want to hear the radio of other players' vehicles, which comes turned on. Also mono sound, for those who hear with only one ear, and lowering the ambience and music while the voice speaks, which comes turned on.
  • Play online: whether the game waits and reconnects by itself when the server you are playing on restarts or the connection drops, which comes turned on, and how many minutes it waits at most, which is ten by default.
  • Movement and driving: on-foot movement with compass or with turns, assisted or free driving, automatic braking, GPS autopilot and proximity sensor. And the three flight ones: Real flight, with altitude and real crossings between towns; Assisted landing on crossings; and Aircraft burn fuel. All three come turned on.
  • Orientation and alerts: format of directions, GPS voice and beacon, announcements of intersections, places, neighborhoods and ground, the sound of places and audible traffic lights.
  • Combat and difficulty: difficulty, aim assist, chase assist, whether missions adjust to your escort, street events and the two assists.
  • The street: people, police and gangs: what happens around you without you looking for it. It is explained a little further down, option by option.
  • Life, reputation and consequences: the consequence level, which is off, soft, normal or demanding and comes set to soft; taxes and bills, which come set to yes; criminal records, yes; love life suitable for everyone, yes; social life is lived in person, yes; and schedules, which come set to no. The first three are explained in the chapter on honest life; the last two, in the one on social life and in Every day is different.
  • Keyboard: change which key does each thing while you play. You choose the function, press Enter and then the key you want. The two keys are swapped. Escape, Enter, F1, F12, right Shift and the Control and Alt keys can't be changed. Left Shift can: the key you put in its place is used for running and for all the combinations with Shift. With the option Also use the changed keys in menus and dialogs, menus are navigated with your keys; without it, in menus the keys are the default ones. When typing text, the keys are always the normal ones. The F1 help says the keys you have set.
  • Gamepad: what each button does, remapping them, vibration and its strength.
  • Voice: screen reader or Windows voice, whether scenes play with the characters' voices, whether a character you created gets the phrases that say their name generated, whether menus say the position of each option, for example three of nine, and whether the history jumps to the end when a new message arrives, which comes set to yes.
  • People and artificial intelligence: how people in the street answer, with which service and with which keys.
  • Log for reporting bugs: where the session log is, what it contains and how to send it.
  • Updates: check for a new version, choose where to update from, and the separate components: languages, voices, guides, sound packs and mods.
  • Adult content: turn it on or off, and say who you are attracted to.

There are also two assists for those who want to explore without pressure: take no damage and play without police. And street events can be turned off.

Options added in version 1.6.2, each in its section:

  • Sound and music: Help signals volume. Beacon, radar, aiming tone, proximity sensor, traffic lights and the sound of each place have their own volume, in addition to that of their group. And Echo depending on the place, for the surround engine.
  • Movement and driving: Run with one key press, without holding the key. One press of Shift starts you running and another brings you back to walking.
  • Orientation and alerts: when you turn on the beacon, the proximity sensor, the sound of places or the traffic lights, you hear a sample of the sound.
  • Combat and difficulty: Difficulty changes the damage you take and also the damage taken by the vehicle you are driving: on easy half, on normal three quarters and on hard all of it. Friendly fire, which is set to yes by default, decides whether you can hurt your dog, your bodyguards and your gang. Alert when you hit the target, Fine or bribe, and three modifiers for those who want more difficulty: tougher enemies, stricter police and worse-paid missions. Each active modifier makes you earn experience twenty-five percent faster.
  • Voice: How much the game explains when speaking. With little, the phrases that only remind you which key to press are removed. With a lot, in menus the explanation of each option is also read. Also the speed, pitch and volume of the Windows voice, for those who don't use a screen reader.
  • Keyboard: Also use the changed keys in menus and dialogs.

Options in the section The street: people, police and gangs, added in version 1.7.1. In brackets, how each one comes set:

  • Self-defense: defending yourself from whoever attacks you first is not a crime (yes).
  • Say when people run away or step aside (yes). The screams are always heard; this is the phrase.
  • People who talk as you pass (rarely). You choose between never, rarely, now and then and often.
  • Fallen enemies drop ammo (yes).
  • Difficulty: fallen enemies sometimes drop their weapon (no).
  • Police patrols driving around the streets (yes).
  • Ambulances that come when there are injured people (yes).
  • Police helicopter at the highest wanted level (no).
  • Mode: neighborhood wars on the street (no).
  • Gangs try to reconquer your neighborhoods (normal). You choose between off, quiet, normal and hard. Only when playing offline, and it is only noticeable when you have a whole city or many neighborhoods.
  • The people you talk to act on their own too (yes). After talking to you, someone may go along with you for a while, hit you if you keep pushing after offending them, or report you.
  • Mode: be able to work as a police officer (no).

The ones set to no change how the game is played, and that's why you have to turn them on on purpose. The rest can be turned off to leave the game as it was before 1.7.1.

Two other options from 1.7.1 are in their own sections, and both come set to no: in People and artificial intelligence, The AI can suggest actions, and the game decides; and in Adult content, Life as a couple: being able to ask your partner to move in with you.

Walking around your house

In a house of yours, the option Walk around inside the house takes you inside. You go from one room to another with the left and right arrow keys, and with Enter you use the room you are in: in the living room you rest for a while and your mood goes up, in the kitchen you eat something from what there is at home once a day and, if you have bought food, you put the shopping away, open the fridge and the pantry and cook, in the bathroom you take a shower, in the bedroom you sleep eight hours and in the garage you see which vehicles you have stored. Escape takes you back to the house menu.

In the house menu, Furnish the house lets you buy eight things, each for one room, which stay in that house:

  • Plants, pictures and a rug, 300 euros: when you sleep you wake up in a slightly better mood.
  • A big sofa and a new TV, 600 euros: watching TV raises your mood twice as much.
  • Fitted kitchen and a big fridge, 700 euros: what you make at home gives you twice the health, burns less often and the fridge holds more.
  • Renovated bathroom, with a bathtub, 500 euros: a long bath raises your mood more and heals some bruises.
  • A really good bed, 800 euros: sleeping here raises your mood quite a bit.
  • A workbench, 900 euros, only if the house has a garage: while you sleep, the vehicles stored in that garage are repaired.
  • A built-in safe, 1200 euros: the money you keep in that house's wardrobe is safe from burglars.
  • A connected alarm, 900 euros: that house is broken into much less often.

What you buy shows when you walk around the house: each room says how it is.
