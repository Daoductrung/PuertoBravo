My vehicles: yours are never lost

A vehicle is yours when you buy it at a dealership, when you fit an upgrade at a repair shop, when you take it out of a garage or when you give it a name. This applies to any land vehicle and to those that fly, including work vehicles, such as an ambulance or a taxi you have upgraded. Boats are separate, moored at their pier. Mission vehicles cannot be yours: the repair shop tells you so before charging you anything.

  • A vehicle of yours doesn't disappear when you move away or take another one. It stays where you park it, in whatever town, and it is saved with the game: when you load it, or when you go back to that town, it is still in its place, with its condition, its upgrades, its fuel and its name.
  • On the phone, the My vehicles section lists them all: name, model, where it is, condition and upgrades. For those on the street it says the street and how many meters away; for those in another town, the town and the street; for those stored away, which garage.
  • With Enter on a vehicle you choose what to do: mark it on the GPS, have it brought to you, send it to a garage or rename it. Bringing it costs 150 euros and sending it to a garage, 100, wherever the vehicle is, including in another town.
  • The last vehicle you used also appears in the list even if it isn't yours, with the warning that it is lost if you take another one. Right there you can put it in your name.
  • Six of your vehicles fit on the street. When you go over six, your mechanic takes the oldest one to the garage of one of your homes, and tells you. If there is no room in any garage, the game warns you and none is lost: sell one or buy a home with a garage.
  • If you are knocked out or arrested in another town and you had a vehicle of yours, it stays parked where it was.
  • A vehicle of yours can still be wrecked: if it blows up, it is lost, along with its upgrades, unless you have insurance, which the next section explains.
  • Games saved with earlier versions load as usual. If they had a vehicle with repair shop upgrades, it becomes yours.

Insurance for your vehicles

A car with every upgrade still catches fire if it takes too many hits or shots. When the game warns you that smoke is coming from the engine, take it to a repair shop, which leaves it as good as new. And if you never want to lose it, take out insurance.

  • You take it out on the phone, in My vehicles: it is the option after the list, Vehicle insurance. Enter takes it out, and Enter again cancels it. The option says how much you pay per day and how much you have paid so far.
  • There is only one, and it covers all your land vehicles, including the ones you make yours later. It doesn't cover aircraft, which go to their hangar, or boats.
  • It covers you from the day after you take it out: taking it out with the car already on fire doesn't work.
  • Every midnight it charges 12 euros for each vehicle of yours that is on the street, from your pocket or, if that isn't enough, from the bank. The ones you have in the garage don't pay. If one day you have nothing to pay with, your insurance is canceled and the game says so.
  • If a vehicle of yours explodes, the insurance tow truck takes it away. The next day they return it to you repaired, with all its upgrades, in a garage of yours with room; if you have no room, at the door of the nearest repair shop in the town you are in.
  • When they return it you pay the deductible: ten percent of what that model costs at the dealership, and at least 150 euros. If you don't have it, they keep it for you until you do, even if you have canceled the insurance.
  • Without insurance, when a vehicle of yours explodes, the game tells you it has been lost and that with insurance it would have been returned to you.
  • On an online server there is no insurance, because the vehicles on the street are handled by the server.
  • The fee and the deductible are adjusted in the mod editor, in My vehicles and driver.

Upgrading your vehicles

At any repair shop, besides repairing and painting, you can upgrade the vehicle you arrived in. There are seven upgrades. Each one has its levels, and each level costs more than the one before: the second, twice the first, and the third, three times.

  • Engine, three levels: higher top speed and more acceleration. You can hear it, because the engine sounds higher-pitched.
  • Brakes, three levels: it stops in fewer meters.
  • Armor, three levels: it withstands more hits and more shots.
  • Reinforced tires, three levels: in the rain you brake almost as on a dry road and, if you play with breakdowns, you get far fewer flat tires. You can hear it: the wheels sound deeper and with less noise.
  • Reinforced suspension and bumpers, three levels: crashes into walls and other vehicles do less damage to yours, and to you inside. You can hear it: the impacts sound duller and lower.
  • Exhaust silencer, two levels: the engine sounds much quieter. When the police are after you, they hear you from less far away and lose you sooner when you drive off.
  • Bigger fuel tank, two levels: it holds more liters. It only matters if you play with fuel.

The last line of the repair shop menu says which upgrades the vehicle has and at what level.

Upgrades go with that particular vehicle. From the first upgrade, the vehicle is in your name: it no longer disappears even if you leave it on the street and take another one, and you have it on the phone, in My vehicles. This also applies to work vehicles. If you store it in the garage of a home, it keeps its upgrades, also when you ask by phone to have it brought to you. If it gets wrecked, they are lost with it, unless you have vehicle insurance, which returns it to you repaired and with its upgrades.

A mission vehicle, or the one your driver brings when it belongs to the gang, can't be kept: the repair shop says so before charging, and doesn't charge.

At the repair shop you can also give the vehicle a name, for free.

Naming your vehicles

You can name any vehicle of yours. There are three places: in your home menu, Name a vehicle, which works for the one you arrive in and for those in the garage; at the repair shop, Name it; and on the phone, in My vehicles, Rename it. You type up to thirty letters and press Enter. If you don't want to type, Tab suggests random names, different each time; with a controller, X or Square suggests them and you can also type it with the letter wheel, as explained in Playing with a controller. If you leave it empty, it is called by its model again. The game says the name when you get in, followed by the model, when you locate it with Shift L and in all the lists. Naming a vehicle puts it in your name. What you type is not written to the game log.

U-turn

If you go past your destination, the GPS tells you to make a U-turn: brake almost to a stop and press U. With the GPS autopilot, it makes it on its own: it brakes, turns and says so.

Fuel, optional

It is turned off by default. You turn it on in Options, Combat and difficulty: Fuel. With it, each car, motorcycle or truck has its own tank.

  • It burns fuel as it drives, more the faster you go. A full tank lasts about nine kilometers at a normal pace.
  • The V key, at the wheel, says how much is left. When you hit the reserve a warning sounds and the game tells you where the nearest gas station is. With the tank empty, the engine coughs and stops.
  • You refuel at gas stations. There is at least one in each town with cars. Drive up with the vehicle, stop and press Enter, without getting out: fill the tank or put in ten liters. You can also leave the vehicle parked next to it and go in on foot.
  • Gas stations sell ten-liter cans to carry with you. If you are stranded, you pour it in from the phone, in Services, while in the vehicle or next to it.
  • Roadside assistance: on the phone, in Services. It takes half an hour, brings fifteen liters and doesn't come if the police are after you.
  • Vehicles you take on the street have their tank as their owner left it: between half and full. The ones you buy, full.
  • Boats, anything that flies, bicycles and mission vehicles don't use fuel. It doesn't count when playing on a server either.

Breakdowns, optional

It is turned off by default. You turn it on in Options, Combat and difficulty: Breakdowns. With it, each vehicle counts the kilometers it drives.

  • Flat tires: every now and then, a tire bursts. You hear it, and the vehicle can't go above half speed and brakes worse until you change it.
  • There are three ways to fix it. With a spare tire you carry: stop the vehicle, open the phone and, in Services, choose Replace the flat tire; it takes you a quarter of an hour. At any repair shop. Or by calling the tow truck from the phone, which costs twice as much and takes half an hour.
  • Spare tires are sold at repair shops and gas stations.
  • Inspection: every fifty kilometers the game warns you that it is due. You get it at a repair shop. If you skip it, the brakes slowly get worse.
  • The V key, at the wheel, says the kilometers it has done and whether anything is pending. All of this is saved with the vehicle, in the garage and in the saved game.

Traffic rules and the points license, optional

It is turned off by default. You turn it on in Options, Combat and difficulty: Traffic rules.

  • Running a red light, or going more than thirty percent over the limit, which is fifty an hour, for a few seconds, costs a fine and license points. But only if the police see you: a patrol or an officer nearby. With no witnesses, nothing happens.
  • You start with twelve points. A red light takes away four; speeding, three. The fine is taken from what you are carrying and, if that isn't enough, from the bank.
  • With no points, your license is taken away. You can keep driving, but if an officer sees you, they come after you.
  • You get points back with a course at any academy: four hours, and you have eight again.
  • There are no fines with the siren on, when the police are already after you, or during the tutorials. Traffic lights count in assisted driving.
  • The V key says the points you have left.

Getting a vehicle

Find it with the radar, stand next to it and press F. A parked car has to be broken into: it takes a few seconds and the alarm may go off, unless you carry lock picks. If it is moving, you pull the driver out. You can also buy them at a dealership: the ones you buy are yours and are never lost even if you leave them on the street. You can keep them in the garage of your homes. In front of hospitals, police stations, stations and taxi ranks there are service vehicles.

Helicopter

The helicopter flies over buildings, traffic and police. It is flown by hand, like a boat: left and right turn, up accelerates, and the game announces the heading. It doesn't crash into anything, but you can only get out if you are stopped over a street or an open space; the C key tells you what you are over. There are helicopters at the heliports. The same goes for the light plane. Since version 1.7.1 you really fly, with altitude, fuel and crossings between towns: the next section explains it. If you turn off real flight in Options, everything is as before: you always fly at the same altitude and, at a heliport, Enter takes you to another town in one jump, free with your own aircraft, or paying for a flight if you are on foot.

Flying for real: altitude, fuel and crossings

With the Real flight option, which is on by default and is in Options, Movement and driving, a helicopter or a light plane take off, have altitude, burn fuel and really travel from one town to another. Everything is done by ear. The whole explanation is also inside the game: at any heliport, airport or airfield, How flying works.

Taking off, altitude and landing:

  • When you get in with F you are on the ground. Up Arrow takes off. The helicopter climbs vertically. The light plane builds up speed down the runway: hold the arrow until it lifts off; if you let go earlier, it doesn't take off.
  • In the air, D climbs and A descends. With a controller, the right stick to the sides: right climbs and left descends.
  • Altitude tone: a note plays every fifty meters you climb or descend, higher-pitched the higher you are. The game says the altitude every hundred meters.
  • Below 150 meters you are flying low, and everything is as usual. Above that you are flying high: the ground police lose sight of you, and the game tells you so. Each aircraft has its ceiling: the helicopter, 900 meters; the light plane, 2600.
  • To land, slow down and press F: the aircraft descends on its own, you hear the altitude tone going down, and when it touches the ground you get out. You don't need to descend first with A.
  • A helicopter can set down at a heliport, on a street or in an open space; not on a building. A light plane needs the runway of an airport or an airfield: anywhere else the game warns you that it will be a forced landing and, if you press F again, you do it, with damage to the aircraft.

Fuel:

  • Each aircraft has its own tank. When full, the helicopter covers about 260 kilometers and the light plane about 520. Flying over a town uses little; what uses fuel is the crossings.
  • You refuel with Enter at a heliport, an airport or an airfield, on board the aircraft, and also inside the hangar. The menu says how much is left and what it costs to fill it up.
  • The game warns you when you hit the reserve, at twenty percent, and sounds the cockpit alarm when eight percent is left.
  • If it runs out, the engine stops and you glide: you steer the aircraft with left and right and set down wherever you get to. Over a street or an open space it is a forced landing; over a building you crash, the aircraft is badly damaged and you come out injured.
  • An aircraft of yours with no fuel isn't left stranded: on the phone, in My vehicles, you can ask for half a tank to be brought to it.
  • It can be turned off in Options, Aircraft burn fuel.

The crossing to another town starts in two ways: by flying to the edge of the town, or with Enter at a heliport, an airport or an airfield, on board, choosing Flight plan. In the flight plan you choose where to go and how you arrive. For each town it says the kilometers, the heading and the minutes, and warns you if you don't have enough fuel. There are two ways to arrive at each place:

  • Land at the airfield: you end up on the ground, at the heliport or on the runway, with the tower guiding you.
  • Fly in low over the town: you end up in the air, on the side you come from, and you set down wherever you like. To an island that has no runway, a light plane can only arrive this way.

During the crossing the aircraft has a position in the region, a heading, altitude and speed, and there is wind, different on each flight and stronger when it rains. The autopilot is on by default and does everything: it climbs to cruising level, corrects for the wind, gives way to traffic and, with assisted landing, descends and lands. You can speed it up. A forty-kilometer trip by helicopter lasts just over a minute, and sped up, less than half a minute; the game clock moves forward by as long as the flight really takes, a quarter of an hour in that case. The crossing keys:

  • Up Arrow and Down Arrow: climb and descend a hundred meters.
  • Left Arrow and Right Arrow: turn ten degrees. They turn off the autopilot.
  • Tab: turn the autopilot on or off.
  • Enter: how the flight is going: the distance left, the heading, whether you are lined up, the altitude, the wind and the fuel. Any other key says the same.
  • N: speed up the crossing or leave it at its own pace. While something is happening it goes at its own pace, and afterwards it carries on sped up by itself.
  • Home: repeat the last thing the tower said.
  • D or Escape: the flight menu: change destination, also to divert to a closer airfield; how you want to arrive; which airfield, if the town has more than one; and assisted landing.
  • F1: help for the crossing.

What you hear: the aircraft's engine and the wind; the radio before each message from the tower; the altitude tone; the cockpit alarm when there is danger; and, flying by hand or when approaching the airfield, the alignment tone: two clicks that sound toward the side you have to turn to. When they sound in the center and higher-pitched, you are on the right heading, which already accounts for the wind.

What happens in the air, different on each flight:

  • Turbulence: the aircraft is shaken about for a few seconds.
  • Traffic: the tower warns you of another aircraft at your same altitude, and you have to climb or descend a hundred meters. The autopilot does it on its own. By hand, if you don't do it, the other one passes very close and you get a penalty.
  • A storm on your heading: the tower warns you in good time and a menu opens for you to decide. Go around it, with a few extra kilometers; go over it, if your aircraft's ceiling allows it, like the light plane's; or fly through it, with strong turbulence and damage. There is at most one per crossing, and they are more likely when it rains.
  • When you pass near another town, the game says which one and on which side it is.

Flying low on a crossing, below 150 meters, has its price: the autopilot doesn't do it, so you fly by hand; the aircraft goes slower and uses more fuel; and every so often the alarm goes off because of an obstacle, a power line or a hill, and you have four seconds to climb with Up Arrow or you clip it. It is useful for one thing: keeping off the radar.

Arriving: at six kilometers the destination tower gives you the approach. With assisted landing, the aircraft descends and lines up on its own, even if you are flying by hand. Without it, and without the autopilot, you do it yourself: descend to 200 meters or less and keep the clicks in the center. If you come in too high or crooked, the tower tells you to go around; the second time, it guides you itself. If you come in just right, you land, but the landing is hard and the aircraft pays for it.

If you run out of fuel in the middle of a crossing, the engine stops and you glide about ten kilometers for every thousand meters of altitude. If the airfield is within your reach, you make it. If not, you set down where you can: the aircraft is badly damaged, you are injured, and you end up on the outskirts of the nearest town.

Hangars

At each heliport, airport and airfield you can have a hangar space. It is in the airfield's menu, on foot or on board.

  • Renting a space costs 120 euros a day, charged every midnight. Buying it costs 26,000 euros, and then there is no rent.
  • Each space holds two aircraft. A light plane, which needs a runway, doesn't fit in a heliport's hangar.
  • Storing: take the aircraft to the airfield and choose Store in the hangar. Taking it out: in the same menu, on the aircraft, Take it out to the runway; the GPS marks it for you.
  • Inside the hangar you refuel, service and repair it, paying according to what it needs, and you can rename it.
  • What is in a hangar is never lost. If one day you don't have enough for the rent, the debt is recorded: your aircraft stay there, but you aren't allowed to take them out until you pay.
  • Your aircraft appear on the phone, in My vehicles, with the others. Those in the hangar say which airfield they are at, and you can mark it on the GPS. One that is on the street, here or in another town, can be sent to a hangar of yours for 250 euros: a company pilot takes it there. An aircraft isn't brought to your door, like a car: you go and get it at the airfield.
  • A home's garage no longer takes aircraft: their place is the hangar. Any you had stored in a garage are still there and can be taken out.

Hijacking an aircraft, and the radar

Stealing a parked helicopter was already possible. Hijacking an airliner is something else, and it is a major crime. At a heliport, an airport or an airfield, on foot and with a firearm in your hand, the airfield's menu has the option Hijack the airliner that is about to depart. The pilot jumps out onto the runway and the aircraft is yours: a helicopter at a heliport, a light plane at an airport or an airfield.

  • You get wanted level 4 and a serious criminal record entry, if records are turned on. If you have the police helicopter turned on, your wanted level rises to the level at which it comes out, and it comes after you.
  • From that moment the radar tracks you. While you fly above 150 meters, the radar sees you: you can't shake off the police, and if you land at a heliport or an airport, they are waiting for you.
  • To lose the radar, fly below 150 meters: twenty-five seconds in a row over a town, or twelve on a crossing. The game tells you. If you climb again, it sees you again.
  • Where to land: far from airfields. In the flight plan, choose to fly in low over the town and set down on a street or in a vacant lot. At an airfield, even if the radar has lost you, someone recognizes the aircraft.
  • A hijacked aircraft can't be yours or be stored in a hangar. If you leave it and walk away, it disappears.
  • The job: every day, at each airfield, someone pays between 3500 and 7000 euros for the aircraft delivered next to a discreet spot in another town. It is chosen when you hijack. They only pay if you set down there without the radar on you. The O key reminds you where, and the flight plan puts it first.
  • At each airfield, one hijacking a day. On an online server and when playing with friends it isn't possible, and the game says so.

Commercial pilot

Flying can also be a legal job. At an academy you get the Commercial pilot license: six sessions, one a day, like the other qualifications. With it, the menu of every heliport, airport and airfield has Commercial flights.

  • Each airfield offers three flights a day, different each day and at each airfield: passengers or cargo to another town, with their kilometers and their pay, which grows with the distance.
  • If you have an aircraft of yours at the airfield that is suitable for that route, you fly with it and get a twenty-five percent bonus. If not, the company leaves you one on the runway, which you return when you arrive.
  • You take off, make the crossing, which the flight plan puts first for you, and land at the airfield for the job. You get paid when you touch down. The O key reminds you where you are going.
  • If the flight has been smooth, there is a ten percent bonus. If it has been rough, because you flew into a storm, had a scare with traffic or made missed approaches, they deduct the same.
  • It is legal money, and each completed flight raises your prestige a little. If the police are after you, the company takes the flight away from you.
  • On an online server there are no commercial flights.

On an online server you fly the same way, with altitude, fuel, crossings and hangars; what there isn't is hijackings or commercial flights. Games saved with earlier versions just load: aircraft start on the ground and with a full tank. The numbers and the lines for all of this, how each aircraft flies and where each town is in the region are in the game data, and they can be changed with the mod editor.

8. Combat

  • Q locks on to the nearest enemy. You will hear a tone coming from where they are: low and double if they are within range, high if they are out of range.
  • Space shoots the locked-on enemy. The closer you are, the more you hit.
  • Half a second before shooting at you, the enemy cocks their weapon. Crouch with X so they hit you less.
  • R reloads. Z uses a first aid kit. With low health you will hear your heartbeat.
  • Explosives are thrown toward the locked-on enemy and damage everything around. Don't use them up close.
  • You can also shoot from a vehicle, without getting out: Q locks on to someone near you and Control shoots, with a firearm in your hand. With an automatic weapon, hold Control. I changes weapon without getting out. The game reminds you of this once, when it is relevant: the first time you are at the wheel with a firearm and there are enemies nearby, or they shoot at you. The F1 help at the wheel also says it. With a controller, the reminder and the help name the buttons.
  • In Options you can switch aim assist to manual and choose the difficulty.

If you go down, you wake up in the nearest hospital and pay the bill. If you are arrested, you lose your firearms and pay a fine. The money in the bank is always safe.
