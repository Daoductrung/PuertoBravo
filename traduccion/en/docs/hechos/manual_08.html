Reconquest: the gangs try to win back your neighborhoods

Conquering isn't the end. When playing offline, the gangs you have defeated regroup over the days and come back for what was theirs: whoever runs a city has to keep watch so as not to lose it. It's the option Gangs try to reconquer your neighborhoods, in Options, The street, with four values: off, calm, normal, which is the default, and hard. Everything is handled with menus, on the phone, in Territories, Neighborhood defense; with a controller, just like any other menu.

When it starts

  • When you conquer the last neighborhood of a city. Before that there are days of calm, between three and five, and the game warns you only once, with its own sound, that this exists and how to defend yourself.
  • With four neighborhoods or more, even if you don't hold any whole city, a mild version starts: the gangs regroup at half the pace.
  • With all the cities conquered, they regroup a little faster.
  • In a game saved with an earlier version that already had the neighborhoods: when you load it there are three days of calm and the same warning, only once.
  • With fewer neighborhoods none of this happens, and the usual one-off attacks continue.

The gangs regroup

  • Each gang has a strength, from 0 to 100, which grows every midnight, sometimes more and sometimes less. On calm it grows more slowly and on hard more quickly.
  • When a gang feels strong enough, it plans an assault, which takes two or three days to arrive. It chooses the neighborhood by its own rules: the one that used to be theirs, the worst defended, the one you have neglected most or the one that brings you the most money.
  • Sometimes two gangs team up for a big strike: they arrive with more strength, and it's announced.
  • While a peace pact is in force, one of those they sometimes offer you, nobody plans a new assault.
  • A gang that pays you tribute doesn't regroup or attack while it pays. One that respects you, or one that has a truce with you, regroups at half the pace.
  • There are never two assaults at once, and after each one there are days of rest: at least seven on calm, four on normal and two on hard. With reconquest under way, the usual one-off attacks don't happen.

How you find out

  • You can always check the threat level of each neighborhood, in Territories and in Neighborhood defense: low, medium or high. If something is being planned against a neighborhood, it says high at least, even if you don't know more.
  • The rest depends on your informants, who get paid every midnight, 45 euros per level. With level 1 you hear the rumor one day before, with the neighborhood. With level 2, a tip-off two days before, with the gang and the neighborhood, and you see the exact strength of each gang. With level 3, a message to your phone as soon as they decide, with the gang, the neighborhood, the day, the time, the strength they come with and why they chose that neighborhood.
  • A neighborhood that is loyal to you warns you itself, without paying anyone: with 50 loyalty, like level 1; with 75, like level 2.
  • With no informants and a neglected neighborhood, the warning arrives late or not at all: at most, a vague rumor on the same day.
  • When the assault starts you always find out: the threat alert sounds and the game says who, where and how many hours you have. It warns you again when two hours are left and when half an hour is left. While it lasts, the O key, the objective key, says who is attacking which neighborhood and how much time you have left.
  • In Neighborhood defense, Latest reports repeats the last things that happened, in case you missed them.

The assault

From the moment it starts you have a deadline: 18 game hours on calm, 12 on normal and 8 on hard. You can do three things.

  • Defend in person: go to the neighborhood. In Neighborhood defense, the option to go in person marks it on your GPS. The fight starts on its own when you enter it, even if you don't have neighborhood war on the street mode turned on: they come in waves, each with one more, and your gang comes to your side. There are two waves; three if they arrive with a lot of strength, and one more if it's an alliance. With good defense in the neighborhood, fewer come in each wave. While you fight, the deadline doesn't run. If you leave the neighborhood or fall, the fight is lost but the assault goes on: you can come back or send your crew. If you win, you take what they leave behind as they flee.
  • Send your crew: in Neighborhood defense, and you need to have someone in the gang. Before you send them, the option tells you the chances, out of ten. The result is calculated on the spot and told with its numbers: their strength against the neighborhood's defense plus what each of yours adds, and each side's luck. One of yours may fall.
  • Do nothing: when the deadline runs out, if the neighborhood has a permanent guard, it defends itself, with somewhat less strength. If it doesn't, the neighborhood is lost.

Rules to keep it fair: in the middle of a mission, no assault starts and the deadline doesn't run. If you sleep, or end up in the hospital or in a cell, the hours go by, but your crew holds out: the first time, when you come back you always have four hours left, and the game tells you so. The same if the assault started while you were away. Short stretches, like a bus ride or a meal, just go by. Nobody loses a neighborhood for sleeping one night; for falling asleep twice in a row with the neighborhood under attack, yes.

Getting ready beforehand

  • Permanent guard: up to three guards per neighborhood, at 60 euros each every midnight; the first day is paid when you hire them. They raise the defense, make loyalty drop at half the speed and defend on their own if you don't arrive. If one midnight you can't pay them, they leave.
  • Reinforce the neighborhood: three levels paid only once, 1500, 3000 and 4500 euros. Each level raises the defense for good; if you lose the neighborhood, it drops one level.
  • Informants: the ones from the previous section.
  • Truce: with each gang, in Neighborhood defense or in Negotiate with the gangs. You pay them and for a few days, between six and ten, they don't attack you; if they were planning an assault, they drop it. They ask for more the stronger they are and the worse you get along, and you can ask for a discount, once. It can be broken: by them, some day, and then you are warned and they can't strike the next day; or by you, if you hit their hideout.
  • Hit their hideout: a short mission, different every time, in the city you are in. If you complete it, the gang loses more than half its strength, it counts as a defeat and, if it was the one planning an assault, the assault is called off. The gang has to be strong enough to have a hideout, and you can't repeat it against the same gang until four days have passed.
  • Your gang members count in the defense of all your neighborhoods, whether you send them or not.

Each neighborhood's loyalty

  • It goes from 0 to 100 and starts at 70. A loyal neighborhood defends itself better, tempts the gangs less and warns you of what is being planned.
  • It drops if more than three days go by without you setting foot in the neighborhood, three points each day, and also if one midnight you can't pay the guards or your gang.
  • It goes up the first time you enter it each day, when you defend it, which is what bonds it to you the most, and when you invest in the neighborhood: 500 euros, once a day per neighborhood.
  • Before it drops too far, the game warns you: once when it falls below 45 and again, more seriously, when it falls below 25. On its own, loyalty never takes a neighborhood from you: it only makes it easier for it to fall.

What you win and what you lose

  • If you lose a neighborhood, you lose what it paid you every midnight and some street reputation, and the neighborhood lost sound plays. It becomes the property of the gang that took it from you, and you win it back the usual way, in Territories.
  • The gang that regains ground gains morale: it regroups faster and comes back sooner.
  • If you fight it off, the neighborhood defended sound plays, the gang loses almost all its strength and a defeat is recorded against it. On the third one, counting the hits on its hideout, it is dismantled for between twenty and thirty days.
  • So that the game doesn't simply end, someone always shows up eventually: when few gangs are left standing, or after a month, an outside gang can settle in the area, with its own name. It's treated like the others; defeated three times, it leaves for good, and in time another will come.

All of this is saved with the game. It doesn't happen on an online server, because there the neighborhoods belong to the players' gangs; nor while you are playing with friends: the reconquest stays paused, with the deadline stopped, and it continues when you go back to playing alone. All the numbers are in the game data and can be changed with the mod editor.

Neighborhood war on the street, optional mode

By default, neighborhoods are conquered and defended from the phone, in Territories, as always. Anyone who prefers to fight for them on the street can turn on the Neighborhood war on the street mode, in Options, The street. With the mode turned on, this changes:

  • Every neighborhood in the cities that isn't yours belongs to a gang, always the same one in your game. The N key, the map and the phone say which one.
  • Its members walk the streets of each neighborhood: of every three people you pass, roughly one is from the gang. They attack you on sight, unless that gang respects you.
  • You conquer a neighborhood by going there. The war starts when you take down two of its members in their neighborhood, and the game warns you the first time; or from the phone, in Territories, once you are already inside the neighborhood, with Stand up to them. From outside, the phone only marks the way on your GPS.
  • The gang comes for you in three waves, each with one more and tougher. The game says how many there are and which way they are coming; in the last one, some arrive in a vehicle. Between waves there are a few seconds to reload. The O key tells you how it's going.
  • If you have recruited people, they come to fight by your side as soon as it starts.
  • Don't leave the neighborhood: the game warns you when you leave, and after twenty-five seconds outside the fight counts as lost. It's also lost if you fall, if you get arrested, if you change town or if you start a mission. Losing it costs nothing else: the neighborhood is still theirs and you can try again.
  • If you win, the neighborhood is yours, with its tribute every midnight, and you take what the gang leaves behind as it flees.
  • The gangs counterattack as always, at midnight. But the neighborhood is defended in person: go there, and when you enter it two waves start. The phone, in Territories, Defend, marks the way for you. If you haven't gone before the following midnight, you lose it.

A war in progress isn't saved: if you save and load in the middle of one, you have to start it again. On an online server the mode doesn't count, because there the neighborhoods belong to the players' gangs. How many gang members walk the street, how many waves there are and what you win are in the game data.

16. The radio and your music

When you get into a vehicle the radio plays. With R you change station. To hear your music, copy your songs into the my music folder and choose the My music station; inside it, R skips to the next song. So you don't have to look for that folder, go into Options, Sound and music, and choose Open my music folder: it opens in Windows File Explorer, ready to copy into, and the option itself says how many songs it has. mp3, ogg, flac and wav files work. You don't need to close the game: when you tune back in to My music the new songs are already there. The folder is data\radio\mi_musica, inside the game's folder; if the station is empty, it explains how to fill it. Any audio file you put directly in data\radio is a new station.

Adding your own music, step by step

You don't need to look for any folder: the game opens it for you. In the game, press Escape and choose My music: put your songs on the radio. These same steps are there, along with the option that opens the folder. It's also in Options, Sound and music.

  1. Choose Open my music folder. A Windows window opens with a folder: it's the one for your songs. The game stays open behind it.
  2. Copy your songs there, as in any folder: find them on your computer, copy them with Control and C, and paste them into that window with Control and V. mp3, ogg, flac and wav files work, and so do whole folders.
  3. Go back to the game with Alt and Tab. You don't need to close or restart it.
  4. Get into any vehicle and change station with R until you hear My music. Inside My music, R skips to the next song.

The option to open the folder says how many songs there are. Your songs don't leave your computer, and updates don't touch them. With a controller, the pause menu and its submenus are handled with the usual buttons.

Songs can be as long as you like: a whole album, a radio show or a recording of several hours in a single file. Those longer than five minutes are read and played in one-minute chunks, without gaps, so they don't take up your computer's memory.

The radio pauses when you get out

When you get out of the vehicle, the song that was playing stops, and when you get into any other one it continues right where it left off. This works for all stations and for My music. Each station remembers its own: if you change station and then come back, it continues with the song it had. That way you can put on a show several hours long and hear the whole thing in pieces, without missing anything.

If you'd rather it behave like a real radio, which keeps going even when you aren't listening, change it in Options, Sound: Radio when you get out of the vehicle. Then, when you come back, you find it further along. Songs shorter than a minute and a half always start from the beginning.

The radio on foot: the music player with headphones

Stores sell a music player with headphones. With it you hear the same stations, and your music, walking down the street.

  • On foot, Shift R turns it on. Each press goes to the next station; one of the stations is off, and when you reach it you take off the headphones.
  • It plays a bit quieter than in the car, so you can still hear the street.
  • If you get into a vehicle with the headphones on, the song keeps playing without a break on the vehicle's radio, and when you get out it continues in the headphones.
  • With the player off, everything is as usual: the radio pauses when you get out.
  • If you save the game with the headphones on, they are still on when you load it.

Music in places

In stores, bars, restaurants, taverns, gyms, repair shops and other places, one of the game's stations plays softly in the background. Each place has its own, always the same. If you have your headphones on, you keep hearing your own.

If the sound clicks or cuts out

On some computers, short on power or with delicate sound cards, the audio can click or stutter. There are two remedies, both in Options, Sound. The first, Relaxed sound: the game prepares the sound in bigger chunks; sounds arrive a few hundredths of a second later and in exchange they stop cutting out. The second, the surround sound engine, which mixes outside the program. Both take effect when you close and reopen the game.

Background music

Outside vehicles, or with the radio off, soft background music plays that changes with the situation: the main menu, exploring by day, the night, the sea and moments of tension, with the police after you or enemies nearby. Each situation has several pieces: it starts with a random one and, when it ends, a different one plays. When you turn on a vehicle's radio the background music goes quiet, and it comes back when you turn off the radio with Shift R or get out. You turn it off and adjust it in Options, Sound and music. You can add your own music to each situation by copying files into data\musica, in that situation's folder: all the files there are used, at random, however many there are and whatever they are called. All the music, both the background music and the radio, is leveled automatically when it loads, so no track sounds louder than another.

Sound engine: classic or surround

In Options, Sound and music, the Sound engine option chooses how each sound is placed around you. You change it with the Left and Right Arrows and it takes effect when you close and reopen the game.

  • Classic: the usual one and the default. It spreads each sound between the left and right ear and muffles a bit what is behind you.
  • Surround: it uses binaural sound, a technique that imitates how sound reaches each ear. With headphones you can tell whether a car is coming from in front or from behind, and not just from which side. It also copes better when many things are playing at once. It needs headphones: with speakers it makes no sense.

With the surround engine, each place also has its own echo: almost none in the countryside, on the beach and at sea; some on the street, between building fronts; more between warehouses and at the harbor; long in the mountains; and short and close inside a house or a place. What flies sounds above you, and if you are flying in a helicopter, the city sounds below you. The echo can be turned off in Options, Sound and music, Echo depending on the place.

If the surround engine can't start, the game uses the classic one without saying anything, so you are never left without sound. On computers with an old processor and with the mono sound option, the classic one is always used. The session log notes which engine was used.

The surround engine uses the OpenAL Soft library, which is free software. Its license comes in the game's folder.

Sound packs

In Options, Sound and music, the Sound pack option switches with the Left and Right Arrows between the default sounds and the packs. They are all in data\sonidos, each in its own subfolder: por_defecto holds the standard ones, which the game recreates if any is missing, and each of the other subfolders is a pack. You hear the change right away. Three complete packs come installed, with all the game's sounds. Realista and Alternativo use real public domain recordings for footsteps, doors, weapons, voices, vehicles, sirens, ambiences, water, wind, alerts and menus; Retro sounds like an old console. All three replace everything that isn't orientation and keep the original sound for beacons, tones, traffic lights, radar, locators and the sounds of places. A pack of your own can bring just some sounds: the rest keep the original. You can add your own by copying their folder into data\sonidos.

The four packs that come with the game bring exactly the same sounds as the standard ones, not one more and not one less. Since version 1.7.1, in all four, these are also real recordings: the police helicopter's loudspeaker, the officer's radio, the house doorbell, the shopping basket, the fridge, the knife on the cutting board, the frying pan, the pot, the plate on the table and the three neighborhood alerts: a kettledrum for the threat, a bell for the neighborhood lost and a drum roll for the neighborhood defended. The tones of the three roleplay channels and the one for waiting for the server are the same in all packs, on purpose: they are a code, and they should always sound the same. Where each recording comes from is written in the CREDITOS.txt file in each pack's folder.

To compare how a sound plays in each pack, choose the pack in Options and go into Learn the sounds, or into Options, Sound and music, Adjust the volume of each sound: both places have all of them, in the same groups and with the same names, and both have a search.

The Envolvente pack is the ambience pack: it carries the sounds of the realistic pack and eleven neighborhood ambiences really recorded in stereo. Its orientation sounds are the standard ones.

Since each recording comes at a different volume, the game matches the volume of each changed sound to that of the original. Also, in Options, Sound and music, Adjust the volume of each sound has all the sounds, in the same groups and with the same names as in Learn the sounds, and in alphabetical order within each group: Enter plays it and the Left and Right Arrows raise or lower that particular sound, from 30 to 200 percent. When you adjust it, it plays, so you hear how it turns out.

  • How it combines with the other volumes: each sound's setting comes first and the general volumes above it, which are the master, effects, ambience, alerts and help cues volumes, are applied on top. If you lower the effects to half, a sound you have at 150 percent still plays louder than its neighbors, but all of them at half.
  • Search for a sound: the first option. Type a word from its name or from what it means and you get the list of those that match, each with its group and its volume, ready to adjust right there. It works the same as in Learn the sounds, also with the controller's letter wheel.
  • What you have changed: a sound that isn't at 100 percent says "changed" after its volume; each group says how many it has changed, for example "changed: 3", in the list of groups and when you enter it; and the Changed sounds option says how many there are in total.
  • Returning a sound to its default volume: with the keyboard, Delete on that sound. With keyboard or controller, go into Changed sounds, which is the list of all the ones you have touched, from any group, and press Enter, or A or Cross, on the one you want: it goes back to 100 percent and plays. You can also bring it to 100 yourself with the arrows.
  • Returning a whole group: inside the group, at the end, Reset this group. The other groups aren't touched.
  • Returning all of them: Reset all, at the end of the list of groups.
  • The settings are saved with your options, by each sound's name: the ones you already had from earlier versions are still there, each in its current group.

Changing the sounds and the music

All the sound effects and all the music are ordinary files, in data\sonidos\por_defecto, data\radio and data\musica. You can replace any of them with a recording of your own. The guide data\docs\cambiar_sonidos_en.html explains how and describes each file.

What format each thing is in, and why

  • The sound effects, in all packs, are in FLAC. FLAC compresses without loss: when it's read you get exactly the same samples as from a WAV, but it takes up less than half the space. Up to version 1.6.1 they were WAV and took up 141 megabytes; now they take up 55, and they sound identical.
  • The background music and the radio are in MP3, and the voices in the scenes and the long ambiences of the Envolvente pack, in OGG. These are formats that already drop some quality to take up little space, and there is no better original: converting them to another format would mean compressing what is already compressed, and they would sound worse, or making them several times bigger for no gain. That's why they stay as they are.
  • When updating from an earlier version, the game deletes only the old WAV files that are the same sound as the new FLAC files, so they don't take up space without being used. If you had replaced a sound with a recording of your own, that one isn't touched and is the one that plays.
  • Your recordings can still be WAV, OGG, FLAC or MP3. If you put one with the same name as a game sound, yours plays; to go back to the original, delete it.

17. Expanding the game: mods

A mod is an expansion: a file that adds places, missions, vehicles, weapons or whole towns to the game, without touching anything original. Mods are in the data\mods folder and load on their own at startup. If a mod has an error, the game says so at startup and keeps working.
