Walking around inside places

You can also walk around inside bars, taverns, restaurants, stores, pharmacies, gun shops, hospitals, police stations, repair shops, banks, casinos, gyms, cinemas, hotels and almost any other place. When you go in on foot, the place's menu has the option Walk around inside the place.

  • You move from one room to another with the Left and Right Arrows, and the game describes each one.
  • Enter uses the room you are in. At tables and in waiting rooms you can sit for a while or listen in on what people are saying. In the restrooms you freshen up.
  • The counter, the bar or the service window is where they serve you: when you press Enter the game says you walk up and you go back to the place's menu, which is where the options to buy or order are. It doesn't throw you out: it's how you go from walking around to buying.
  • Notice boards are read with Enter and tell you what's on that day. The one at the police station: whether you are wanted, at what level and what the fine would cost you, your arrests, the gangs wanted in the city, and the day's alerts and incidents; every time you read it, it says something different. The one at the lottery office: the numbers drawn in the latest draws and the jackpot. The one at the job center: some day jobs with their pay.
  • F1 explains where you are, what Enter does in that room and which room you have to go to in order to be served.
  • Escape goes back to the place's menu.

Walking around a place is optional: everything you can buy or do is still in its menu, as always. Outdoor spots, such as squares, piers or stops, have no inside.

Food stores, supermarkets, markets and gas station stores are walked through differently: aisle by aisle, with a basket, and you pay at the checkout. It's explained in the section Shopping by walking around the store.

15. Territories, your gang and the other gangs

The neighborhoods you conquer, the gang you build with them, the heists you plan and what the other gangs do.

Territories at a glance

On the phone, in Territories, the first option is How this works: it explains it with the numbers from your game. In short:

  • What they are: every neighborhood in the cities can be yours. Towns and islands have no neighborhoods to conquer. You don't need them to follow the story: it's another way to earn money and grow strong.
  • What they're for: each territory pays you tribute every midnight, without you doing anything; it lets you have a gang, with one more person for each neighborhood; and it raises your street reputation. In your neighborhoods, the gang members of the gang you drove out no longer walk around.
  • How to conquer them: on the phone, Territories, Conquer and the neighborhood's name, while you are in that city. Three waves of gang members come, each one tougher; if you have a gang, it comes to help you. If you fall or leave, you only lose the chance. With neighborhood war on the street mode, you conquer them by going to the neighborhood. And without firing a shot, in Negotiate with the gangs you can buy one.
  • How to defend them: from time to time a gang attacks one of your neighborhoods. An alert sounds and it appears at the very top of Territories: you defend it in person or send your gang, before midnight. If you do nothing, you lose it.
  • Reconquest: with many neighborhoods, the defeated gangs regroup and try to win them back, with a warning and a deadline. You prepare for it in Neighborhood defense, and it can be turned off in the options.
  • If you lose a neighborhood: it stops paying you and becomes the property of the gang that took it from you. It can be conquered again.
  • Who owns each place: the N key, the map, the list of places and the notice boards always say who runs each neighborhood right now. Places whose name or description mentioned the previous gang, such as a hideout, also change when the neighborhood changes hands.

Picking a fight with a gang, without conquering anything

If what you want is to fight, with no neighborhoods involved, on the phone, in Territories, there is Look for a fight with a gang. Only when playing offline.

  • You choose the gang, and whether you want a short fight, of a single round, or a long one, of two, with a few seconds to reload in between.
  • They come for you wherever you are, on foot. You have to stay in the neighborhood until it's over: if you leave, it counts as lost, with no other consequences. The O key tells you how it's going. If you have a gang, it comes to help you.
  • You don't win any neighborhood or money, except what they drop when they fall. Your street reputation goes up a little, once a day.
  • It has consequences: that gang dislikes you more, for challenging it and for each one you take down, and if there is shooting the police do the usual.
  • You can't do it during a mission or a job, with the police after you, at sea or inside a place. You have to wait a little between one fight and the next.
  • With a controller: it's a menu like the others, inside the phone.

Missions adjust to your escort

With your whole gang and two bodyguards, which is up to eight people by your side, missions played themselves. That's why, by default, when you arrive at a mission with people by your side, a few more enemies are waiting for you, and they take more damage and aim better. You change it in Options, Combat and difficulty: Missions adjust to your escort.

  • Moderate, which is the default: one more enemy for every two of yours, at most half again as many as the mission brings; six percent more health for each one, up to forty; and somewhat better aim, up to fifteen percent.
  • Strong: one more enemy for each of yours, at most double; ten percent more health for each one, up to eighty; and up to thirty percent better aim.
  • No: the same enemies always wait for you, whoever you bring.
  • The first of yours doesn't count: going with a single companion changes nothing. Your gang members and bodyguards who are nearby when the enemies appear count; the dog doesn't count.
  • When the opposition goes up because of your escort, the game says so, once per mission.
  • It doesn't count in the tutorials, with practice dummies, gym opponents or people you only have to scare, or on an online server or playing with friends. Neighborhood wars don't change: there your gang always comes.
  • All the numbers are in the game data and can be changed with the mod editor.

Your own gang

When you conquer your first neighborhood you can have a gang. On the phone, in Territories, go into Your gang:

  • Recruiting someone costs 400 euros. Each territory lets you support one more: with one neighborhood, two people; with five or more, six, which is the limit.
  • Call your gang makes them come to where you are. They follow you on foot, get into the vehicle with you and fight by your side.
  • Each member is paid 60 euros every midnight. If you don't have enough, one leaves. If you run out of territories, the gang breaks up.
  • You can give it any name you like.
  • Driver: one of yours comes to pick you up and drives. The next section explains it.
  • Your crew, one by one: each person in your gang has a name and four things that make them different, from 0 to 100: marksmanship, driving, nerve and loyalty. They are different in each game and are saved with it. When you call them, each one arrives with their name.
  • Prepared heists: before a robbery you can hand out roles among those by your side. It's explained in the section Prepared heists, further down, and in How heists work, in this same menu.

A driver from your gang

If you have someone recruited, you can ask them to come pick you up with a vehicle and drive. You ask on the phone, in Services, Your gang's driver, or in the Your gang menu. It costs 80 euros each time.

  • You choose what they come in: a gang vehicle, or one from your garages. You can also ask from My vehicles, on a vehicle stored in a garage.
  • It takes between half a minute and a bit more than a minute to arrive, different each time, and longer if it's raining. When you ask, it tells you roughly how long. When they set off, the game tells you which way they are coming; when they arrive, they honk the horn twice, say a line to you, and the vehicle gives off a tone so you can find it.
  • Get in with F: you ride as a passenger. Set a destination with L or from the phone, as always, and they take you: they speed up, brake, turn, wait at traffic lights and stop when they arrive. If there is no destination, they wait and ask you for one. It works even if you have free driving selected.
  • While they drive, the arrows, U, Y and J do nothing: the game reminds you that you are a passenger. Everything else stays the same: the radio, the radar, and shooting, with Q to lock on and Control to fire.
  • If you get out with F, they wait for you there, however far you go, with the tone sounding so you can find it when you come back.
  • You can dismiss them at any time, from the same menu. If the vehicle is yours, you keep it: if you were inside, you take over the driving; if not, it stays parked, in your name. If it's a gang vehicle, they take it away, and if you were inside they drop you off on the sidewalk.
  • They don't come if the police are after you at level 3 or higher, or in the middle of the sea. If you change town without them, they go back; if they came with one of your vehicles, they leave it parked where it was.
  • The gang vehicle can't be stored, sold or upgraded.

Prepared heists: everyone with their role

A regular robbery, of a bank, of any place with a till or of a supplier, can be planned beforehand if you have members of your gang by your side. It's not mandatory: the regular robbery is still in its option and works the same as before. When it's planned, each of yours does a job, the safe takes time to open, the heist has phases that the game announces, and your crew talks to you over the radio.

In the menu of the bank, the place or the supplier, below the robbery option, Plan the heist with your gang appears. It shows up when you have someone recruited. Inside:

  • How this works: the whole explanation, with the numbers from your game.
  • One line for each of yours who is nearby. With Enter you give them a role, and the menu tells you how good they are at that role. Each role is done by one person only. Anyone you don't give a role to comes with you and fights by your side, as always.
  • Call the missing members of your gang, if not all of them are there.
  • You, inside: you open the safe yourself, or you cover whoever opens it. To cover, someone has to have the safe role.
  • You, on the getaway: you drive, or you ride as a passenger. To ride as a passenger, there has to be a driver.
  • Getaway vehicle, if there is a driver: one of yours that is nearby, or a gang one.
  • The split: what percentage your crew takes and how much you would keep today.
  • Start the heist. You need the same as for the regular robbery, a firearm in your hand and nothing else going on, and at least one person with a role.

The roles:

  • Driver: waits outside, with the engine running, in the vehicle you choose. The vehicle gives off a tone so you can find it. When you come out you get in with F, as a passenger or at the wheel, and the getaway starts with a head start: if the police haven't arrived yet, they take longer to find you; if they are already after you, it can take one wanted level off you. Their performance depends on their driving.
  • Lookout: stays outside, on a corner. When the alarm goes off they hold the police back, about twenty seconds depending on how good they are, and tell you over the radio how long is left, again when only a few seconds remain, and when the police are already there. Without a lookout, the police arrive as soon as the alarm goes off. Their performance depends on their nerve.
  • Escort inside: goes in with you and keeps the people and the guards in check. With them it's much harder for someone to play the hero and attack you while you open the safe, or for someone to press the alarm too early. When they prevent either of these, they tell you. Their performance depends on their marksmanship.
  • The cash grabber: with you, the safe opens quite a bit sooner; and if you choose to cover them, they open it on their own while you stay close, less than twenty-five meters away, free to fight. Their performance depends on their nerve.
  • Shooter: rides in the getaway vehicle and fires through the window at the patrol cars and officers following you. Their performance depends on their marksmanship.

The five phases, which the game says out loud and which the O key reminds you of at any time, with who does what:

  1. Entry: everyone goes to their spot and says so over the radio.
  2. Control: if there is a guard, you have to take them out. If there isn't, the people get down on the floor.
  3. The safe: it's no longer taken instantly. Go up to it, press Enter and stay next to it: it takes between eight and sixteen seconds depending on the place, and twenty-six at a bank. You hear it progress, with a sound that gets higher and higher, and the game says when it's halfway. If you move away, it stops, and it continues when you come back.
  4. Exit: with the loot in hand, the alarm goes off.
  5. Getaway: the police arrive, with that place's wanted level, and you have to shake them off. The money is yours when they stop looking for you.

What can go wrong, different each time and depending on how good each one is:

  • Someone whose nerve fails: the driver arrives late or stalls the engine and there is no head start; the lookout doesn't see the police coming; the cash grabber takes longer than you would.
  • The escort can get wounded if there is a guard: they drop out of the heist and are out of action for a few days, between two and five. They still get paid.
  • The lookout can get arrested when the police arrive: they leave the gang and don't get paid. If their loyalty is low, they give your name: your wanted level goes up one and you get a criminal record entry, if criminal records are turned on. If they are loyal, they keep quiet.
  • If the heist fails, everyone who had a role loses trust in you. Anyone left with none leaves the gang.

The split: when it goes well, each one gets their share of the loot: the driver and the cash grabber, ten percent each; the lookout, the escort and the shooter, eight. Together they never take more than sixty. Also, with each heist that goes well they learn, and they get three points better at everything up to the fifth heist, and become more loyal to you.

On the phone, in Territories, Your gang, there are Your crew, one by one, with how good each person is, how many heists they have done with you and whether they are out of action, and How heists work.

Your crew: pay, loyalty, tasks and betrayals

  • Gang pay, in your gang's menu, is low, normal or high. With low pay you save money, but they cool toward you a little each day; with high pay, they become somewhat more loyal each day. If someone goes days without you talking to them, treating them or giving them something, they feel forgotten and their loyalty drops on its own.
  • If one day you can't afford to pay, one leaves: the least loyal one, not the last to join. The others stay, but angry, and lose loyalty.
  • Their profile says how they are doing: is with you to the death, is happy, is getting by, is unhappy or is badly burned out.
  • Someone badly burned out can betray you: steal what you keep in a house's closet and disappear, go off with another gang, or tip off the police, who search one of your houses, take the merchandise and sometimes shut down the lab.
  • Tasks: in each person's profile, the Task option. They can sell on the street the merchandise you keep in your houses' closets, or cook in one of your labs with the supplies you have, which are bought from a supplier. At the end of the day they bring you what they sold, in cash and minus their cut, and what they cooked stays in that house's closet. A dealer can get arrested: they lose what they were carrying and are out for a few days; and if they aren't loyal to you, they keep a bit more than they should and, if they get arrested, they reveal where you keep the rest. A fire can also break out in a lab. Tasks need adult content.

In Your crew, Enter on someone opens what you can do with that person, by name: talk for a while, and they tell you things about themselves; treat them to something, for thirty euros; give them a bonus, for one hundred fifty; or kick them out of the gang. Each one can be done once a day and makes them more loyal, the bonus most of all, and someone loyal doesn't give you away if they get arrested.

With a controller everything is done from the menus, with the usual buttons: there are no new keys.

On an online server and when playing with friends, heists aren't planned with the gang, and the game says so: there the option doesn't appear, and the regular robbery still works. A game saved with an earlier version, which only knew how many of you were in the gang, gives them names and skills the first time. The roles, their shares, the times and the radio lines are in the game data, and they can be changed with the mod editor.

Owning neighborhoods has consequences

  • Who owns each neighborhood: the N key, the map explorer, the phone's list of neighborhoods and the people you ask say who the neighborhood belongs to at that moment. A gang's, while it runs it; yours, when you conquer it; at war, while you are conquering it or while it's being attacked; and the gang that took it from you, if you don't defend it. On a server, the player gang that holds it.
  • Street reputation: taking over a neighborhood becomes known throughout the city. The first one gives a lot of street reputation; the following ones, less and less.
  • Attacks: from time to time, at midnight, another gang attacks one of your neighborhoods. If you have a gang, it may defend it on its own: the more people, the more likely. If not, you have until the following midnight to go: on the phone, Territories, Defend. There are two groups of gang members. If you don't go, you lose the neighborhood. When you hold a whole city or many neighborhoods, these one-off attacks give way to the reconquest, which is explained further down.
  • Pacts: with three neighborhoods or more, a gang may offer you a pact. If you accept, you get paid and for a week nobody attacks. If you turn it down, you gain some street reputation.
  • The police: an inspector may let you know that you are on their list. If you pay, they look the other way. If not, a raid costs you a day's tribute.
  • Everything is answered on the phone, in Territories, before the following midnight. If you turn off street events in the options, none of this happens.

How you get along with the gangs

You have a relationship with each gang, which you can see on the phone, in Territories. If a gang respects you, its members don't attack you on the street as long as you don't touch them, and they don't try to take neighborhoods from you. If it's out to get you, the opposite happens. The relationship changes with story decisions, with pacts and every time you kill one of its members.

Negotiating with the gangs

Besides the peace pact they sometimes offer you, now you make the proposals. On the phone, in Territories, there is Negotiate with the gangs, with three deals. What they answer depends on your strength on the street, which comes from your gang members, your neighborhoods and your street reputation, and on how you get along with each gang; and it isn't the same two days in a row.

  • Give a neighborhood back for money: you offer one of your neighborhoods to the gang that wants it. If it's interested, it names a figure. You accept it, ask for more or leave it. You lose the neighborhood and what it pays you every midnight, but you get paid in one go and your relationship with that gang improves a lot. If you are at each other's throats, it won't even listen to you.
  • Buy a neighborhood: you get it by paying, without firing a shot. They ask for more if you get along badly and less if they respect you or if you carry a lot of weight on the street. A gang that has it in for you, when you carry little weight, won't sell to you.
  • Collect tribute: you demand that a gang pay you every midnight for leaving it alone. You need to have some neighborhood. If they fear you, they pay for a few days, between five and ten, and while they pay they don't attack your neighborhoods. If not, they refuse, your relationship gets worse and, if you carry little weight, they may send someone after you. Now and then, one digs in its heels and stops paying.
  • Haggle: in deals with a figure you can push once. If it works, it improves by a quarter; if not, they walk away from the table.
  • Make a truce: with neighborhood reconquest under way, each gang also has this option. You pay them and for a few days they don't raid your neighborhoods. It's explained in the next section.
  • When a gang has answered you about something, it won't talk to you about it again until three days have passed.

With the police after you, or outside a city, there is nobody to sit down with. It doesn't exist on an online server: there the neighborhoods belong to the players' gangs. The numbers and the lines are in the game data.
