Slow time, cover and hit alert

  • Slow time: the Backspace key makes everything go much slower, and sound deeper, as long as you have focus left. It gives you time to hear where the shots come from and to lock onto a target. Focus runs out in about five seconds and recovers by itself in half a minute; another press turns it off. The V key tells you how much you have left. It can't be used with friends connected.
  • Taking cover: with Shift X, if you are right next to a wall or a stopped vehicle, you take cover behind it. In cover you take less than a third of the damage, but your aim is a bit worse. If you move, you leave cover. The same key takes you out of it.
  • Hit alert: every time you hit the target you hear a sharp tap and the gamepad gives a short jolt. When you lock onto a target with Q, the gamepad also lets you know. It can be turned off in Options, Combat and difficulty.
  • If you are crouching without realizing it, you walk very slowly: V tells you so, and so does the game if you try to run.

What each weapon does

In the gun shop, in the phone's inventory and in the list of your weapons, which opens with I, each weapon states its damage and its range. With F1 on it you hear the whole sheet, in words and in figures:

  • Damage: the points each bullet, each blow or each explosion takes away, and whether that is low, medium, high or very high. For comparison: a pedestrian can take 50 points and you, 100.
  • Range: how many meters it reaches, and whether that is short, medium or long. Melee weapons are close combat. For explosives it also says how many meters around the explosion reaches.
  • Rate of fire: how often it fires or strikes. For automatic weapons, how many shots per second.
  • Accuracy: how many shots out of a hundred it hits at close range. At long range it hits less.
  • And the rest: the bullets in the magazine and whether it makes noise, a lot of noise or none.

This also applies to weapons added by mods.

Where each weapon is sold

Almost all weapons are bought at gun shops, but not all of them. Each weapon says in its description where it is sold:

  • At gun shops: the usual ones and, in addition, the burst-fire pistol, the semi-automatic rifle and the grenade launcher.
  • In shops: the fire axe, which is with the hardware. It is a slow melee weapon that hits very hard.
  • At pawn shops, second-hand: the pump-action shotgun and the lever-action rifle. The pawnbroker has them on top of whatever you sell him.

Ammo is bought where you bought the weapon and also at any gun shop. The six new weapons each have their own sound:

  • Fire axe: the whistle of the swing and the dull thud of the blade.
  • Burst-fire pistol: automatic. Hold down fire and it empties the magazine in two seconds, with a dry, very short sound.
  • Pump-action shotgun: a deep bang and, after it, the pump, back and forth. Five shells; it hits harder and reaches farther than the sawed-off shotgun. It can't take a silencer.
  • Lever-action rifle: after each shot you hear the lever, two metal clicks. Seven bullets, accurate and long range.
  • Semi-automatic rifle: one shot each time you pull the trigger, and fast. Ten bullets that reach seventy meters.
  • Grenade launcher: a hollow, tube-like thump. It launches a grenade sixty meters, twice as far as throwing by hand. Like all explosives, don't use it up close.

Enemies who arrive in a vehicle, and what they shout

Not all enemies wait for you on foot. Part of the escort in some contracts, the reinforcements in the last assault when you conquer a neighborhood and the enemies in missions that say so arrive in a vehicle. This is how it sounds:

  • In the distance you hear tires screeching: they have just set off toward you. The game tells you how many are coming in the vehicle.
  • You hear the engine getting closer. With Q you can lock onto the vehicle, just like a person, and shoot at it.
  • If you destroy it before they get out, the ones inside go down with it.
  • When it reaches you and you are going slowly or on foot, it stops, you hear the door and they get out. The game says how many and where, and now you can lock onto them one by one. If you are in a vehicle, it rams you.
  • The vehicle is left empty where it stopped: you can take it.

Many enemies shout a phrase when they see you, with their name first: bodyguards, guards, gang members and officers. It tells you who has seen you and what type they are. Some have better aim than others, and a deeper or higher voice. When playing on a server, mission enemies are always on foot.

Self-defense

Defending yourself is not a crime. If someone attacks you without you having touched them first, whether a mugger, a gang member who has spotted you in the street or anyone who turns on you, you can shoot them and knock them down without it counting: neither the shots nor the kill raise your wanted level. The first time it happens, the game tells you. The same goes for the muggers and gang members of street events: stopping them is not a crime.

  • Who started it counts. If you hit them first, or you mugged them and they fought back, what you did is not self-defense.
  • It doesn't apply to mission enemies, or to those in a neighborhood war: there, you are the one going after them.
  • Explosives are always a crime. And if you hurt a passerby in the fight, that does count.
  • Officers go after them too. If there is a patrol car nearby, or someone calls the police, a patrol arrives with its siren on, the officers get out and go after whoever attacked you, not after you. When they are done, they get back in the car and leave. If they are also after you, each officer goes after whoever is closest.
  • What an officer knocks down doesn't count among your enemies, and leaves no loot.
  • Gangs understand it too. Knocking down a member of a gang who attacked you first costs you very little reputation with them, instead of the four points it costs when you start it.
  • If you are a good person, that is, if your standing with the neighbors reaches 40, gangs treat you differently. When one of them sees you in their neighborhood, they often let you through with a scowl, and the game says so; if you provoke them, they come after you as always. And defending yourself from those who do attack you costs you nothing with their gang and doesn't count toward starting a neighborhood war: everyone knows it was self-defense. On top of that, the police come to help you sooner. Your standing is checked on the phone, in Life, reputation and consequences.

It can be turned off in Options, The street: then everything goes back to how it was before, and any shot and any kill count. On a server with shared police, defending yourself is not a crime either, but the officers are run by the server and don't come to help you.

Ammo and weapons from fallen enemies

A fallen enemy, besides money, sometimes leaves on the ground ammo for the weapon they were carrying, if you carry the same one: between half a magazine and a full one. It is picked up by itself when you walk over it, like money; you hear a magazine going in and the game says how many bullets there are and how many you have. The radar and your dog find it like any other item. It can be turned off in Options, The street.

In that same section there is a difficulty option, off by default: fallen enemies sometimes drop the whole weapon, if you don't have it. You keep it when you walk over it, with whatever bullets it had. It makes the game easier and the gun shop less necessary; that's why you have to turn it on on purpose. On an online server it doesn't apply.

Upgrading your weapons

At any gun shop you can fit two upgrades on each firearm you have:

  • Silencer: shots can no longer be heard from far away, so people don't run and nobody calls the police because of the noise. What you do in front of someone is still seen. Shotguns, machine guns and the crossbow can't take one.
  • Extended magazine: it holds half as many bullets again.

Upgrades go with the weapon: if you are arrested and they take it away, they are lost with it.

Fights at the gym

Besides boxing, gyms have mixed martial arts nights: three rounds in a row in the cage, each against a tougher opponent, with no rest and only with your fists. If you go down in any of them, you lose the night. It pays quite a bit more than boxing, and each night you win raises the prize and the toughness of the next one.

Gym fights are sport: knocking down your opponent is not a crime, doesn't call the police and doesn't lower your standing with the neighbors, whoever is watching. That said, punching someone who was passing by is still assault.

9. The police

Crimes only count if someone sees them. The wanted level goes from 1 to 5. At level 1 they try to arrest you; from 2 upward, they shoot. To get rid of them:

  • Get away from the sirens. When there are no police nearby you hear a deep heartbeat: hold on for a few seconds and they stop looking for you.
  • Repaint the vehicle at a garage.
  • Look after the vehicle: every crash lowers its condition. The game warns you at 50 and 25 percent; at 25 smoke comes out of the engine. At zero it catches fire, and you have about seven seconds to get out with F before it explodes. A garage leaves it like new.
  • Hide in one of your houses, change clothes in a shop or pay a bribe at the police station.
  • Leave the town by road or head out to sea.

Who comes after you at each wanted level

The more they want you, the better armed they come. Each patrol sent after you brings officers of one type, and the type depends on the level:

  • Levels 1 and 2: officers with pistols, two per patrol.
  • Level 3: almost all with pistols, and the odd patrol with a submachine gun.
  • Level 4: no more pistols. Some patrols bring submachine guns and others, combat shotguns. They can take more damage.
  • Level 5: the same and, on top of that, the Borrasca Unit, the police assault unit. The game warns you when you reach this level. They come in a van, which sounds deeper than a patrol car and takes twice as much damage. Three officers with assault rifles get out of each van, with better aim and almost twice the toughness of an ordinary officer.

Officers say who they are when they see you: "Stop, police!", or "Borrasca Unit!". Which type of officer appears at each level is in the game data and can be changed with the mod editor. On a server, the police are run by the server: the officers carry the weapons from this same table, but they always come in a patrol car.

Fine or bribe

If an officer catches you with one or two wanted levels, with no mission in progress and enough money, they don't just arrest you: the game lets you choose.

  • Pay the fine on the spot: your wanted level is cleared, you keep your weapons and you don't go through the police station.
  • Try a bribe: it costs less, but it can go wrong. It works more often the more street reputation you have. If it goes wrong, you lose that money and you are arrested.
  • Let them arrest you: the usual.

First, the officer frisks you. If you are carrying product or something stolen in your backpack, they find it, the game tells you and no fine will do anymore: all that's left is to try the bribe, which then costs more than a fine and works less often, or to let them arrest you.

When you are arrested, at the police station they keep your firearms, all the product you are carrying and the stolen items in your backpack, and the game tells you everything they have confiscated. Found items, like what you bring up diving, are not taken from you, and neither is what you have in the wardrobe of your houses.

The menu comes up by itself, as soon as the officer reaches you, and each option says what it costs. With F1 on the bribe you hear how many times out of a hundred it works at that moment. With three levels or more, or with no money, you are arrested straight away: there is no negotiating when you are very wanted. It can be turned off in Options, Combat and difficulty.

If you want to know what it would cost you before you get caught, go into a police station, walk around the place and read the notice board: it says your wanted level and how much the fine would come to.

Police patrols on the beat

A few patrol cars drive around the streets even when nobody is looking for you, as on online servers: two near you by day and one at night. They drive without sirens, stop at traffic lights and the radar announces them as police. They don't do anything to you, but they see what you do: a crime committed less than eighty meters from a patrol counts even if nobody else is watching. With traffic rules turned on, they are the ones who give you the fines. If you are attacked and one is nearby, it comes. And if you earn a wanted level, the one nearby turns on its siren and comes after you.

They are turned off in Options, The street: then the police only show up when they are looking for you, as before. How many there are is in the game data. On a server with shared police, the server puts them there.

Surrendering

With the police looking for you and an officer or a patrol car nearby, Shift H raises your hands. With a gamepad, the combine button and the left trigger. Stop the vehicle first, if you are in one. The officers stop shooting and come for you: don't move or attack. If you move or attack, the surrender is broken, they start shooting again and for a while they won't trust another one. Another press lowers your hands.

Surrendering keeps you from being killed, but it isn't free, and it costs more the more they want you. When you raise your hands, the game tells you what it is going to cost you:

  • At level 1 or 2 it is an ordinary arrest: if you have the option turned on and money, you can settle it with the fine or the bribe.
  • From level 3 there is no deal. The fine is 300 euros per level plus five percent of what you are carrying per level; the cell, eight more hours for each level from the second onward, that is, 16 hours at level 3, 24 at level 4 and 32 at level 5; and you lose your firearms, as in any arrest.
  • At level 5 you also lose all your other weapons and the vest.

Anyone who isn't police, like a gang member, doesn't understand hands up and carries on with what they're doing. On a server with shared police there is no surrender: there, the police are run by the server.

The police helicopter, optional

It comes turned off, because it makes level 5 quite a bit harder. It is turned on in Options, The street. With it on, about twenty seconds after reaching level 5 a helicopter arrives:

  • You hear it coming from far away and circling above you, with an engine deeper and bigger than that of a civilian helicopter. With the surround sound engine, it sounds overhead.
  • When it spots you, it speaks to you through the loudspeaker: you hear the feedback and the game says what it announces. It repeats it every half minute.
  • As long as it has you spotted you can't lose the police, however far away the patrol cars are.
  • To make it lose you: on foot, stick close to a building front, a little over a meter from the wall, and hold on for eight seconds. While it can't see you, you hear, higher pitched, the same heartbeat as when the police are looking for you and can't find you, and the game tells you when it has lost you. Or go into any building, whether a business, your house, a garage or a parking garage: going in makes it lose you instantly. Open-air places, like a monument or a dock, don't count.
  • When it loses you, it circles looking for you for a little over half a minute. If during that time you come out into the open just as it saw you, on foot or in the same vehicle, it finds you in a few seconds. If you come out in a vehicle different from the ones it has seen you in, it doesn't recognize you. But if an officer on the ground has you in front of them, they tell it where you are.
  • If it doesn't find you, it withdraws, and then the police lose you as usual. If you are still at level 5, another one comes back after a minute and a half.
  • It can be shot down: lock onto it with Q and shoot. Pistols, submachine guns and assault rifles do nothing to it, and the game tells you so. You need a weapon with a lot of damage per bullet that can reach it: a rifle, a carbine or a lever-action rifle; or an explosive fired at it, like a rocket or the grenade launcher, which explodes up there, wherever it is.
  • Shooting it down has consequences: it falls in flames and blows up whatever is under it, so get out of the way; for two minutes the police take twice as long to forget you; you gain street reputation and lose it with the neighbors; and the next time you are arrested, or surrender, you will pay 2000 euros more for each helicopter.

On a server with shared police it doesn't appear. Its numbers and what it says through the loudspeaker are in the game data.

Ambulances

A while after a shootout or a hit-and-run an ambulance arrives: you hear the siren getting closer, it pulls up to the sidewalk next to the injured, the paramedics treat them and they leave. While the shooting goes on, it waits. It is just ambience: it doesn't chase you, it brakes if you are in front of it and it doesn't let you get in while they work. It is turned off in Options, The street. Only when playing solo.

10. The phone

With M you open the phone: current objective, available missions, places and GPS, map explorer, personal markers, where is my vehicle, my vehicles, services, your career, life, reputation and consequences, skills and achievements, inventory, properties, neighborhoods and statistics.

Life, reputation and consequences is where you check how the city sees you, how the street sees you and what you gain or lose because of it, along with your criminal record, your contract, your studies, your businesses, your loans, what you are going to be charged and the matters waiting for a decision from you. It is explained in the section on honest life.

With a mission in progress, Available missions starts with it: it says where its current objective is, and Enter takes you back to it, not to the place where it started. The phone also has Back to the objective, which does the same as Shift O.

In Available missions, the summary of each mission says whether you are given a vehicle, whether you have to take a specific one, whether yours will do, whether you have to go in one or whether there is a vehicle chase. You hear the same thing when you reach the contact's door, before pressing Enter to accept it.

My vehicles lists all of yours, whether they are on the street, in another town or in a garage, and lets you mark them on the GPS, bring them to you, send them to a garage and rename them. It is explained in the section on driving, in My vehicles: yours are never lost.

In Services you can ask for a vehicle from your garages to be brought to you, send the vehicle you are driving or the one you have just parked nearby to one of your garages, for 100 euros, ask for a driver from your gang, call a bodyguard, ask for an ambulance to heal you wherever you are or a taxi to travel fast.

In Services you can also dismiss your bodyguards when you no longer want them: they stop charging you.

The journal: what has happened, replaying and skipping missions

  • Journal, on the phone, says at the top how far you are in the story and what comes next. Below are the decisions you have made and the missions you have done, from the latest to the first.
  • With Enter on a mission you have already done you can replay it: it takes you to the place where it starts. Replaying it is just for fun: it gives no reward and doesn't change the story.
  • If you have gone two days or more without playing, when you load the game it reminds you where you were.
  • If you fail the same mission three times, Skip this mission appears in the failed mission menu: it counts as done, with no reward, and the story goes on. You can replay it later from the journal.

11. Traveling between towns

  • By road: drive a vehicle to one of the town's exits and, without getting out, press Enter; a menu with the road opens, and with another Enter you take it. You arrive with your vehicle. Any car, motorcycle or truck will do. You don't need to hug the sidewalk or hit an exact point: when you reach the exit with the vehicle, the game says "you're there now" and which key to press. You can't leave by road on foot: if you try, the game tells you and, if you were traveling with the GPS, guides you to the bus station.
  • By bus: walk to a station and buy a ticket.
  • By helicopter: from a heliport, flying it yourself or paying for the flight.
  • By boat: at a marina you can rent a speedboat, set sail in one of your boats or steal one. At sea, get close to a dock and press Enter to moor.

If you mark a place in another town on the GPS, it guides you to the right exit, station or marina and carries on when you get there. If you are at the wheel, it always takes you by road as long as you can get there that way, even if you have to cross another town on the way: at each exit, Enter, and the GPS continues on the other side. On foot it takes you to the bus. When you reach the exit, the station or the marina, the GPS says what you have to do there, and G repeats it.

Monteferro, the mining city, is in the mountains, beyond Villaseca; Fuentefría is even higher up. The Neptuno Platform is in the middle of the sea, to the southwest.

Isla Tortuga can only be reached by sea or by helicopter. The Mar de Poniente has the coast to the north, with Puerto Bravo to the west, Cala Brava in the center and San Lucero to the east; the island lies to the south.

The taxi and bus ride

When you take a taxi or a bus, you hear the trip: the engine, the road and the radio station the driver has on. A taxi takes between twelve and forty-five seconds depending on the distance, and a bus, about fifty. If you don't want to wait, press Enter and you arrive instantly; any other key tells you how much is left. If you prefer to always arrive at once, turn off the option Taxi and bus rides with a route, in Options, Movement and driving.

Helicopter flights with a pilot also have a ride, with the sound of the rotor. If you fly the helicopter yourself, they don't.

Tram: for getting around within the city

The four cities, Puerto Bravo, San Lucero, Monteferro and Bahía del Ocaso, have a tram line that goes around the city, with stops in every neighborhood: sixteen in Puerto Bravo. Each stop sounds with two rings of a bell and appears in the list of places and on the radar.

  • Walk to a stop and press Enter. The menu offers you all the other stops, from nearest to farthest, and says how many stops there are to each one. The ticket costs two euros.
  • During the trip, the tram stops along the way and announces each stop and the next one. It takes the shorter way round the line.
  • You get off wherever you want: with Enter you get off at the next stop, even if it isn't the one you chose. Any other key tells you which is the next one and how far it is.
  • With the rides with a route option turned off, you arrive at the chosen stop instantly.

The tram doesn't leave the city. There are none in the towns or on the islands.

The tracks, and the tram in the street

The tram tracks run down the middle of the roadway on some streets and avenues, forming a circuit that passes by all the stops. Trams run on them all the time: three in Puerto Bravo, two in the other cities.

  • You hear the tram coming from wherever it is: an electric hum that gets closer, passes and fades away. At each stop it halts for a few seconds and rings the bell when it arrives and when it pulls away.
  • The tracks can be crossed on foot, like any street. When you step on them, the game says Tram tracks, just as it says Sidewalk or Road, and your footsteps sound different: a metallic tap, from the rail, with gravel underneath. When you step off them it says Road again.
  • If you are on the tracks and a tram is coming, the driver rings the bell nonstop and the game warns you which way it is coming from. Get onto the sidewalk. If you don't move, it runs you over: you lose health and it knocks you onto the sidewalk.
  • The tram doesn't ram a vehicle: if you drive along the tracks with it behind you, it brakes, waits and rings the bell until you clear them. There is only a crash if you are the one who runs into it, and it is a small one.
  • When you get on at a stop, the tram that carries you is one of those running: when you get off, it continues on its way from there.

Near the airport you can hear, now and then, a plane taking off.
