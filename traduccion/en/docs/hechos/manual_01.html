Puerto Bravo: Ley del Asfalto

Game manual, version 1.7.2. An open-world audio game, in English and Spanish, and fully accessible: you play it by ear alone, with the keyboard or with an Xbox or PlayStation controller, and with your screen reader.

The other guides are in the same folder, data\docs: keys and buttons, what's new in each version, changing the sounds and the music and mod editor manual.

This manual is organized by headings. With NVDA or JAWS you can jump from section to section with the H key.

1. What this game is

At the start you choose between 4 characters, each with its own advantages, or you create your own, and you decide whether people address you as a man or as a woman. With the first one your name is Dani Roca and you have just got out of prison after serving three years for a robbery you did not commit. You go back to your city, Puerto Bravo, which is now controlled by the businessman Octavio Belmonte. From there, do whatever you want: follow the story, or don't.

  • An open world with 9 maps: the cities of Puerto Bravo, San Lucero and Monteferro; the towns of Villaseca, Cala Brava and Fuentefría; Isla Tortuga, the Neptuno Platform and the Mar de Poniente, which you can sail by boat.
  • 311 places: homes, stores, banks, repair shops, casinos, gyms, cinemas, hospitals, piers, heliports and monuments, each with its own sound and description.
  • 101 missions between the main story and the side stories of the other towns. On top of that, jobs you can repeat endlessly.
  • 40 vehicles: cars, motorcycles, a bicycle, trucks, emergency vehicles, a race car, 8 boats and a helicopter.
  • 28 weapons, from your fists to the rotary machine gun, including silent weapons and explosives.
  • Jobs: taxi driver, pizza delivery, ambulance and bus driver, security guard, hauler, ice cream seller, sea courier and fisher.
  • Repeatable jobs: contracts from the tavern notice boards, car orders at the scrapyards, boxing matches at the gyms and bank robberies.
  • Random street events, four skills that improve with practice and 40 achievements.
  • 9 races and regattas, a casino with slot machines, blackjack and roulette, a shooting gallery, dice, the lottery, a bank, properties, businesses that make money and bodyguards.
  • Police with five wanted levels, day and night, rain, traffic with traffic lights and pedestrians.
  • Two ways of living, which you can mix: the honest life, with a job center, careers with promotions, courses and volunteering, and the life of crime.
  • Background music with several tracks for each situation, which goes quiet when you turn on the radio.
  • An optional mode with content for over-18s, turned off by default.
  • A living city: you can talk to anyone on the street, each with their own name, job and personality, and every day is different: prices go up and down, there are holidays, strikes and market days. No two games are the same.
  • Play with friends over the internet or at home, without anyone's servers: your own game hosts the session.
  • Updates from inside the game, choosing where they download from, and separate components: languages, voices, guides, sound packs and mods.
  • In Spanish and English, with recorded voices in both languages, and ready to be translated into any other.
  • An accessible mod editor to create your own expansions.
  • Seven radio stations with original music, a news station that comments on what you do and another with your own music.

2. Requirements and starting the game

  • Windows 10 or 11.
  • Stereo headphones. They are essential: the game places each sound where it belongs around you.
  • A screen reader running, such as NVDA or JAWS. If there is none, the game speaks with the Windows voice.
  • A 64-bit processor. The game adapts on its own to your processor: on current ones it uses its normal sound engine and, on old ones, from before 2009 or 2010, one made for them. You don't have to choose anything.

Run PuertoBravo.exe. The game installs nothing: everything it uses or creates is in the data folder, next to the executable. You can copy the whole game folder to another computer.

The window shows the last spoken message in large, high-contrast letters, in case you play with some remaining sight or with someone who can see.

The game's language

The game is in Spanish and English, and more languages can be added. When it opens it looks at your Windows language: if it is any variant of Spanish, it runs in Spanish; if it is another one and the game has that language, in that one; and if it doesn't, in English.

  • To choose it yourself, go into Options: the first line is Language, and you change it with the left and right arrows. Automatic is what was just explained; if you choose a language, the game always runs in that one, even if your Windows is in another. The change takes effect when you open the game again.
  • Everything that is said and read is in the chosen language: the menus, the story, the places, the items, the keys, the mod editor and the server panel. This manual and the other guides too: each document has one version per language, and the game opens the one in its language.
  • The story scenes have recorded voices in both languages. If you play with a character you made yourself, the lines that say your name are generated on your computer with voices in the game's language.
  • Your saved games work in any language: a game saved in Spanish loads in English, and the other way round, with your clothes, items and everything else in place.
  • On an online server each player plays in their own language, and what the server says reaches you translated into yours.

If it's your first time: the playable tutorial

The game starts in Grandma's Apartment. Right there, with Enter, you can start the mission Learn to get around: a tutorial you play. It is three short exercises, with no time limit, no enemies and no police, in which you cannot fail:

  1. Walk to a pharmacy using the GPS and the beacon, or letting the character walk on their own.
  2. Find a store with the radar and mark it. This exercise checks that you use the radar: if you reach the store another way, it tells you, and you only have to press Tab right there.
  3. Get into a car and take it to a gym, driving yourself or with the autopilot.

It is optional and you can do it at any time: it is on the phone, in Missions, under Learn to play. It takes about five minutes.

There are three more tutorials, also optional, and a briefing. You start them from the phone, in Missions: they appear with the word Tutorial in front, and with Enter they start right away, wherever you are: if you are far from where it takes place, or in another town, the tutorial itself takes you to its starting point and tells you. You cannot fail any of them, and while they last the police do not come. All of them check that you do what they teach: just reaching a place is not enough.

  • Learn to defend yourself, at the Olympic Shooting Club: lock on to a target, hit with a bat, shoot and reload, against dummies that don't fight back.
  • Learn to earn a living, at the Job center: you get into a practice taxi, start work with J and do a real fare: pick up a customer and take them to their destination. The tutorial doesn't move on until you do it, and whatever you earn is yours. When it ends you can keep working with that taxi.
  • Learn to sail, at the Harbor Pier. There are three exercises: set sail, in a practice boat the pier lends you for free while the tutorial lasts; steer, sailing about two hundred meters and changing course; and dock at a pier, whichever one you like. Just walking somewhere no longer completes it.
  • Learn to play with friends is no longer a mission, because without a friend there is nothing to check: it is a briefing. It appears in that same list with the word Briefing in front, and also on the phone, in Play with friends, under the name How to play with friends. You read it right there, without moving from where you are.

3. The menus

  • Up Arrow and Down Arrow: move through the options.
  • Enter: choose.
  • Left Arrow and Right Arrow: change the value of an option.
  • Escape: go back.
  • Home and End: first and last option.
  • A letter: jumps to the next option that starts with it. Accents don't count: A also takes you to what starts with Á.
  • Delete, in Adjust the volume of each sound: sets the chosen sound back to its default volume.
  • F1: explanation of the current option.
  • Page Up and Page Down: review previous messages. Control Page Up goes to the first one in the history and Control Page Down to the last one.

The history keeps the last three hundred messages. By default, each new message takes you to the end. If you prefer to stay where you were while you review, in Options, Voice, set When a new message arrives, the history jumps to the end to no; then you go to the last one with Control Page Down. With a controller, the actions menu has Go to the first message in the history and Go to the last.

When you type text, such as your character's name or a message, accented vowels typed with the accent key also work, as do Windows input methods.

In the story dialogues, Enter moves to the next line, Up Arrow repeats the line, Left Arrow goes back to the previous one and Escape skips the whole dialogue. No dialogue moves on by itself: you read at your own pace.

4. Finding your way

The city

The cities and towns are a grid. Streets run from west to east and avenues from south to north, with an intersection every 90 meters. Each street has the roadway in the middle and a sidewalk on each side. The blocks are solid buildings, except for squares, parks, beaches and piers, which you can walk through.

The sound

What sounds to your right is to your right. What sounds muffled, as if through a blanket, is behind you. The farther away, the quieter. On foot, in compass mode, your hearing always faces north: north is in front, east is to the right. At the wheel, the sound turns with the vehicle.

Orientation tools

None of them are required and almost all can be turned off in Options. Use the ones you like.

  • C, where am I: street, between which avenues, neighborhood and which way you are facing. With Shift it adds the coordinates.
  • W, A, S and D, directional scanner: your cane. It says what is in front, to the left, behind and to the right: people, vehicles, doors, where the roadway begins and how many meters away the wall is. A tone also plays from the wall.
  • Tab, radar: goes through everything nearby, from nearest to farthest, with distance and direction, and plays each thing from its position. Shift Tab goes backwards. F6 filters by places, vehicles, people or items.
  • B: points the GPS at the last thing the radar said. If you were already going somewhere, it is a detour: the game says so, and when the detour ends the GPS goes back on its own to the destination you had, and says that too. A detour to a vehicle ends when you get into it; to a person, when you reach them; to an item, when you get on top of it or pick it up; and to a place, when you arrive. If what you were looking for disappears, it also ends. It is useful for going after money, an item or a car nearby without losing your route. With Shift G you cancel the detour and go back to your destination; with another press you clear the GPS. The detour notice always reminds you of this, whether you are going after something that stays still or following someone who moves.
  • L, list of places: by category and in alphabetical order. It includes a list of unvisited places and the other towns. Enter sets the destination.
  • F9, map explorer: moves a cursor from intersection to intersection with the arrows, without moving your character, so you can learn the town. Tab goes through the places next to the cursor, N describes them, D says how far it is from you, Home goes back to your position and Enter sets the destination. It also works at sea.
  • Personal markers: on the phone you can save the exact spot where you are and return to it with the GPS.
  • G, GPS: repeats the direction. It says the distance and the next turn. Shift G clears the destination: if you were on a detour, you go back to what you had, and with another press the GPS is left with no destination. Control G brings back the last destination or detour you cleared, without having to look for it again; if you are now going somewhere else, it comes in as a detour. During a mission, if the GPS is left with no destination, after two seconds it goes back on its own to what the mission asks for and says so. If you clear it yourself with Shift G, it doesn't come back on its own: you get it back with G or with Shift O. With no mission and no destination, G brings back the last one you cleared.
  • O, objective: first says where the thing the mission asks for is, with its name as it appears in the list of places, how many meters away, in which direction and on which street, and then the rest. If the objective moves, as in a chase or when following someone, or if the clock is running, one press says only where it is and where it is heading, and two presses in a row read the whole explanation. If the GPS is on a detour or doesn't have the objective set, O says so.
  • Shift O, back to the objective: always does the same thing. It drops any detour, sets on the GPS what the mission or job asks for now and, if it is within radar range, leaves it selected on the radar. The same is done, on the phone, by Back to the objective and by the current mission, which comes first in Available missions; and, in the list of places on L, by the first entry, Mission objective.
  • Sound beacon: a little bell that rings in the direction you have to go, faster the closer you are. On foot it rings at the corner of the sidewalk, not in the middle of the intersection. You turn it on and off with F4.
  • Sounds of places: each type of place has its own sound, which you hear as you get close. A gun shop sounds like a gun being cocked, a bar like music through the wall, a store like a door chime. They are all in the Learn the sounds menu.
  • Learn the sounds: it is in the main menu and in the pause menu, and it has every sound in the game, with none missing. First comes the group The most important, with the ones you hear most and that help most to find your way and survive, in the order in which it is best to learn them. Then comes Search for a sound, and then all the sounds divided into groups, according to what you are doing in the game when they play. When you enter a group the game says how many sounds it has. Inside, they are in alphabetical order of their names, and a letter jumps to the next one that starts with it, whether it has an accent or not. Press Enter on a sound to hear it: the game says what it is called and what hearing it means, and when you press, only that sound plays, with the sound pack you have set. The groups, which are the same and in the same order in Options, Adjust the volume of each sound, and in the guide to changing the sounds, are these:
    1. Orientation and GPS
    2. Sounds of places
    3. Moving on foot and swimming
    4. Vehicles and driving
    5. Breakdowns, repair shop, gas and vehicle theft
    6. Boats, helicopters, train, tram and plane
    7. Weapons, the shot of each one
    8. Combat, aim and wounds
    9. Police and emergency services
    10. People on the street and animals
    11. Home, kitchen and food
    12. Stores, money and items
    13. Menus, phone and game alerts
    14. Missions, work, reputation and mood
    15. Territories and gangs
    16. Online play and roleplay
    17. Fishing, casino and games
    18. Ambience and weather
    19. Relationships, only with adult content
    20. Substances and dealing, only with adult content
    Search for a sound saves you from guessing the group: type a word or part of one, for example siren, door or police, and press Enter. You get the list of sounds that have it in their name or explanation, each with the group it belongs to. It doesn't matter whether you type it with or without accents, or in upper or lower case; with several words, you get the ones that have all of them. With a controller you type with the letter wheel: D-pad up and down to choose the letter, D-pad right to type it, and A or Cross to search.
  • Footsteps: they sound different on sidewalk, asphalt, grass, sand and pier.
  • Accessible traffic signals: at each intersection, a fast ticking means you can cross the street walking north or south, and a slow chirp means you can cross the avenue east or west.
  • N: description of the place or the neighborhood. For the neighborhood it also says who it belongs to at that moment: a gang, you, or at war. K: which way you are facing, with a tone that plays from the north. T: time and weather. V: your status.
  • Shift N: the next intersection. It says which street is the next one you are going to cross in the direction you are facing or driving, how many meters away it is and which street you are on. It works the same on foot and at the wheel, and says nothing else.
  • Shift L: where your vehicle is. It says where you left the vehicle you got out of and sets it on the GPS. If you were going somewhere else, for example to a mission location, when you reach the vehicle the GPS goes back to that destination on its own. It is also on the phone: Where is my vehicle. If you have more vehicles, it tells you how many, and that they are all on the phone, in My vehicles.

The GPS has a single destination

The GPS always guides you to one single place, and that is the one G tells you: the directions, the beacon and the autopilot all go there. Underneath, another one can be waiting, which you go back to when the one on top ends, and the game always says so.

  • What you set yourself while a mission or a job has its destination set is a detour: when you arrive, the GPS goes back to the mission's destination.
  • What you set with the radar's B or with Shift L is a detour from whatever you had, whatever it was.
  • If the mission changes destination while you are on a detour, the game tells you that you are still on the detour and where the GPS will go afterwards.
  • If the mission ends during a detour, your destination stays and there is nothing underneath any more.
  • O talks about the mission objective and G about the GPS destination. When they don't match, O says so and reminds you that Shift O goes back to the objective.

Walking on your own

With a destination set on the GPS, the U key on foot makes your character follow the route on their own until they arrive, along the sidewalks and turning the corners. Hold Shift to make them run. Any arrow, or U again, stops them. You still hear the intersections, the places and the traffic, and cars don't stop for you: when crossing, listen. At the wheel the same thing exists with the Y key, the GPS autopilot.

How directions are given

In Options you choose how they are said: with compass points, with clock positions or with in front, right, behind and left.
