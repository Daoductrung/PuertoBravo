Playing on a server

As well as playing with friends, you can play on a server: a world that never shuts down, which anyone can set up with the server program, and which anyone who has its address can join. On a server you do everything you do playing on your own, missions, jobs, homes, businesses, and on top of that there are the other players, with whom you can do all sorts of things.

Joining

  • In the main menu, Play on a server. Add a server with the address given to you by whoever runs it; it starts with puertobravo, colon, slash, slash. It stays in your list: with Enter on it you join.
  • The first time you choose a character. That character belongs to that server: it isn't any of your single-player games, and it's saved on the server, not on your computer. When you join again you carry on where you left off, with what you had.
  • There are no passwords or email. The first time, the game creates a key, your identity, and saves it in your data folder; each server recognizes you by it. If you delete the data folder or change computers you lose it, and with it your characters: in Play on a server, Your identity, you can copy it to keep it and restore it later. Treat it like a password.
  • Your player name is set in that same menu. If someone on a server already has your name, the server adds a number to yours.
  • A server may ask for a join password or an invitation code. The game asks you for it when you join.
  • The game tells you whether the connection is encrypted. It remembers the server's certificate and, if one day it's a different one, it warns you and doesn't join unless you accept it.
  • The server isn't tied to a version of the game, and people with different versions can play together on the same server. This is explained in the section "Playing with people on another version", a little further down.
  • Homes and businesses on a server: if you buy them while playing on it, when you sell them to the market you get your share back, and you can sell them to another player. The ones you bring from playing on your own are still yours and you use them the same way, but selling them there gives no money and they can't be sold to others: the server has no record that you paid for them on it.
  • The shops are set by each person's game, not by the server. Whatever your game or your mods sell, clothes, accessories or anything else, you can also buy while playing on a server: the server keeps track of the money, always lets you spend and saves your character with whatever they're carrying.
  • How another player is dressed: get close and press Shift J. The game says their name, how many meters away they are and what they're wearing. It only tells you when you ask. It also works when playing with friends. On a server with role-play channels, it also reads their profile.
  • The game can only be opened once, so each person plays one character.

What belongs to everyone and what is yours

  • Everyone's: the players, the vehicles you leave on the street, the shots between you, the gangs, the neighborhoods, the time, the day, the rain, the day's prices, the events and the lottery.
  • Homes and businesses also belong to everyone: each one has a single owner. And so do a few characters run by the server, with a name and a personality.
  • Traffic is everyone's: the cars on the road are run by the server, they stay in their lane, stop at traffic lights, which are the same for everyone, and brake if you step in front of them. The car you hear go by is the same one that the person next to you hears. To take one, step in front of it, wait for it to brake and press F beside it: the driver runs off and the car becomes yours and everyone's.
  • The police chasing a player are also in the game of those nearby: you hear their patrols with the siren and their officers are where they are. If you mess with them, what you do to them really happens to them, and the police start looking for you too.
  • The server keeps track of the money. You can always spend. What you earn from your story missions, what other players give you, loot and sales is justified. What you earn on your own from jobs, sales, gambling and what you pick up on the street has an hourly cap, set by whoever runs the server. Anything above that, the server doesn't recognize and it's withdrawn.
  • When the server withdraws money, the game tells you with figures: how much you've just earned, how much is accepted and how much is withdrawn, what that server's hourly cap is, how much margin you have left and how much you recover each minute. The margin you don't use is kept for you, for up to a few hours, also while you're away: after a while without earning anything you can have a whole good streak. You can check how you're doing on the phone, Online, Data and rules of this server.
  • Repeatable jobs, which are contracts from the board, gym fights, bank and shop robberies and car jobs, don't use up that cap: the server recognizes them, up to a number per hour of each type that also appears in Data and rules. If you go over that number, the game warns you and that job counts as any other earnings. On a server that isn't updated to 1.7.1 there's none of this: everything counts against the cap, and the warning doesn't include figures.
  • The people on the street are everyone's: pedestrians are run by the server and walk along the same sidewalks in every game. The person you hear go by is the same one that the person next to you hears. If someone shoots or shouts, they run away for everyone; if you mug one, the next person finds they have nothing left; if they go down, they go down for everyone. At night and when it rains there are fewer of them.
  • Each town also has some server patrol cars driving around, like one more car in the traffic. If you shoot near one, it sees you and the police start looking for you. If you're wanted and it passes near you, it comes after you.
  • The police are everyone's. Your game counts your crimes, but the wanted level is kept by the server, and it's the server that sends the patrols. The one coming after you is the same in the game of whoever is next to you: they hear it arrive with the siren, stop, and the two officers get out. With one wanted level they try to arrest you; with two or more, they shoot, and before each shot you hear them cock their gun. What happens to you when you're arrested is the usual: the fine, the bribe or the cell.
  • If the patrol car has stopped, the officers have got out and you escape in a vehicle, they don't just stand there: the ones still next to the car get back in and the patrol car goes after you. You hear the door.
  • Anyone can mess with those officers, not only the person being chased: if another player takes one down, the officer goes down for everyone, and the police start looking for that player too. With the officers out, the patrol car is left empty and anyone can take it; stealing it is one more crime.
  • Whether you've lost them is decided by the server: get away from patrol cars and officers until the alert that you're wanted stops sounding. On foot you need less distance than in a car. Painting the vehicle at a repair shop still works.
  • Leaving the server with the police on your tail doesn't clear it: if you come back within ten minutes, they're still looking for you. And the option to play without police doesn't apply on a server that runs the police itself.
  • Yours: your missions and your jobs.
  • The time is the server's and it doesn't stop. Sleeping restores you, but doesn't move the clock forward. If you go days without joining, when you come back the midnights you've missed count, up to a limit of three. The game tells you what has happened while you were away: what was stolen from your businesses, which is deducted from you; who took the vehicle you left on the street; and whether someone bought a property you had for sale.
  • The character is saved automatically every minute, a few seconds after buying something and when leaving. Saving at home or with F5 saves it on the server. If the connection drops right after buying something and there wasn't time to save, when you come back you won't have what you bought, but the server refunds the money it cost you.

The other players

  • To your game, another player is one more person on the street, except that a real person is controlling them. Everything you can do to a character you can do to them: lock on to them, shoot them, hit them, run them over, mug them, talk to them, pull them out of their vehicle or take the one they've left. And they can do the same to you.
  • You hear them where they are, on foot or in whatever they're driving, they appear on the radar and the GPS takes you to them. You also hear what they do: their shots, their shouts, their horn and their explosions sound in your game from where they are, and the people on your street get scared just the same. Whoever is being chased by the police can be heard coming with the sirens.
  • J next to another player, if there's no one else to talk to, writes a message just to them.
  • E next to another player, with a weapon in your hand and both of you on foot, mugs them: you take part of what they have in their pocket. They find out who it was and Q locks on to you. The same player can't be mugged again until five minutes have passed.
  • Open the phone with M and go into Online. In Who is here you'll find each player and what you can do with them: mark them on the GPS, write just to them, give them money, hand them a weapon, ammo or an item if they're next to you, invite them to your gang or to your mission group.
  • Messages: to everyone, with F10 or from Online; to those less than eighty meters away; to your gang; to your group; or to a single person. If the server has role-play channels, there are also in-character messages, actions and out-of-character messages: they're further down, in Role-play.
  • The shared activities are those from Play with friends: a race with a bet, a team delivery run and riding as a passenger.
  • Someone riding as a passenger in another player's vehicle is inside: neither that vehicle nor any other runs them over, they can't be mugged like someone on foot, and the bodywork protects them from shots just like the driver. When they get out they're one more pedestrian again.

Vehicles

  • The vehicle you get into becomes everyone's. If you leave it on the street, it stays there in the others' games too, and anyone can get in with F and take it. If it's taken from you, the game tells you who.
  • To pull another player out of the vehicle they're driving: on foot, next to them, Shift + F. They have to be going slowly. If you manage it, they're left on the street and the vehicle is yours. With F alone, next to someone else's stopped vehicle, you get in as a passenger.
  • Another player's parked vehicle can be wrecked. What you keep in your garage nobody touches.
  • Mission vehicles aren't shared: nobody can take the one you have to deliver.
  • Vehicles nobody uses disappear after a few hours, and each player can only have a few parked at a time. Whoever runs the server decides.
  • On a server, street vehicles are kept by the server, not by your game. My vehicles, on the phone, lists the ones the server has registered in your name and that are parked, as well as the ones in your garages; if another player takes one, it stops appearing. Whatever you want to keep safe, put it in a garage. The name you give a vehicle is kept when you leave it on the street, if the server is up to date.
  • From that list, with Enter on a vehicle of yours that's parked on the street, you can rename it, ask your mechanic to bring it to you, even if it's in another town, or send it to the garage of one of your homes, without having to go to it. They cost the same as when playing solo. The server saves the name and the other players see it too. If in the meantime another player has taken it, the game tells you and nothing is charged. On a server that isn't updated to 1.7.1 the three options appear the same, but when you press them the game tells you that server doesn't know how to do it yet: there you have to go to the vehicle.

Homes and businesses

  • On a server, a home or a business belongs to a single player, like a car: if you buy it, nobody else can have it. Grandma's apartment is everyone's own.
  • Online, Homes and businesses, tells you what is still available in your town, what another player is selling and at what price, and who owns the rest. Enter marks the place on the GPS.
  • At the door of a place that already has an owner, the menu tells you who it belongs to. If it's for sale, you can buy it from them right there: the money goes to them, whether they're connected or not.
  • You can put what's yours up for sale to other players, at whatever price you want, or sell it to the market: you get part of it back and it becomes available again for anyone. Both options are in the menu of your home or your business.
  • The properties of someone who hasn't joined for many days go back to the market, and their share is kept for them for when they return. That way there's always something to buy in the end.
  • Robbing another player's business: at its door, on foot and with a weapon, the menu offers Rob the business. The alarm goes off, the owner and their gang get an alert with the place marked on the GPS, and you have to hold out for twenty seconds without moving away. If you manage it you take the day's takings. If they take you down or you leave earlier, nothing.
  • What was stolen is deducted from the owner at midnight, or when they join again if they weren't there. Each business's till can only be emptied once a day, and you can't rob the business of someone in your gang or in an allied gang.

The server characters

  • As well as the players, the server runs a few characters, the same for everyone. They have names, walk around the town and appear in Online, Who is here, marked as server characters.
  • Each one has their own personality, and they appear at random: the one who strolls around and says hello; the pickpocket, who comes up to you and mugs you; the gunman, who goes after anyone with a price on their head; the one who takes the cars you leave parked and leaves them somewhere else; and the one who robs players' businesses.
  • You can do the same to them as to a player: lock on to them, shoot them, run them over, mug them. Some run away and others fight back. Taking down one who hasn't done anything to you counts like taking down anyone: wanted level and a price on your head.
  • Whoever runs the server decides how many there are, or that there are none.

Fighting other players

  • Whether a shot or a blow of yours reaches another player is decided by the server, not by your game: it looks at the weapon, the distance and the rate of fire.
  • Q locks on to whoever attacked you. Shift + Q cycles through everyone near you, including players who haven't done anything to you. From Online, Who is here, you can also lock on to someone.
  • When you're attacked you hear it, the game says who it was, and from that moment Q locks on to them.
  • Safe zones: next to hospitals, police stations, homes, animal shelters and soup kitchens, and inside any venue, nobody can hurt anybody. The game warns you when you enter and leave.
  • When you arrive on the server and when you respawn you have a few seconds of protection.
  • Passive mode, in Online: you neither hurt nor get hurt. It can't be changed in the middle of a fight, nor more than once every two minutes.
  • If you're taken down by another player you wake up in the hospital, as always, and they take part of what you had in your pocket. What's in the bank, your homes and your businesses are not touched.
  • Whoever takes down someone who wasn't defending themselves becomes wanted by the police and gets a bounty on their head, collected by whoever takes them down.
  • Leaving the game in the middle of a fight doesn't save you: the character stays there for fifteen seconds.
  • Grenades, Molotov cocktails and rockets hit players within their radius. Running people over also counts.

Gangs and neighborhoods

  • In Online, Your gang, you can found one, with whatever name you want. It costs money. You're the boss.
  • The boss and their right hand invite other players. The boss hands out the ranks, can hand over command, kick someone out and disband the gang.
  • The cash box is shared: anyone can pay in; the boss and the right hand take money out. Every transaction is recorded with the name of whoever made it.
  • Gang friendly fire: the boss decides. By default it's off and gang members can't hurt each other.
  • Neighborhoods: while inside a neighborhood that belongs to no one, the boss or the right hand can claim it. The cash box pays for it, and from then on the neighborhood leaves money in the cash box every server midnight. The gang can have one more neighborhood than it has members.
  • Wars: to take a neighborhood from another gang you have to declare war on it, while you're in it. It doesn't start right away: the server alerts both gangs and it starts a few minutes later, so nobody has a neighborhood taken while they're asleep. During the war, each gang scores one point per second for each member who is inside the neighborhood and on their feet, and thirty for each rival they take down inside. Whoever has more wins; if it's a tie, the neighborhood stays with whoever had it.
  • Alliances: they're sealed when both bosses propose one to each other. Allies can't hurt each other or declare war on each other.
  • Online, Gangs, neighborhoods and wars, tells you which gangs there are, who owns each neighborhood and which wars have been declared or are under way.

Group missions

  • From Online, Who is here, invite someone to your mission group. Up to four fit.
  • Whoever leads the group starts one of their missions, one of the usual ones. The others hear what each step requires and the GPS takes you to the place.
  • The mission's enemies also appear in the others' games, and whatever one player takes off an enemy is taken off for everyone.
  • Group members can't hurt each other.
  • When it's completed, whoever led it collects the full reward and each of those who were with them in the town gets half. It's only collected once per mission.

Role-play: speaking in character, actions and profile

If whoever runs the server has the role-play channels turned on, which is how they come, as well as the usual chat you can play your character in writing. Everything is read by your screen reader, and each channel sounds different so you know what it's about before you hear the sentence.

  • With a controller, the three channels and the usual message are in the actions menu: View, or Create or Share, held, and the right trigger or R2. Also on the phone, Online, Role-play. You type with the letter wheel or choose one of the ready-made phrases with X or Square; with the keyboard, the ready-made phrases come up with Tab.
  • Speaking in character: Shift F10. It's what your character says. Only those near you hear it, within about thirty meters if whoever runs the server hasn't set another distance. Two low notes play.
  • Describing an action: Control F10. You write what your character does, for example "opens the door", and those nearby hear your name followed by that: "Ana opens the door". It has the same range. A short rustle with a note plays.
  • Speaking out of character: for what you say as a player, a question or a notice. The whole server hears it. Two dry taps play.
  • If there's nobody close enough, the game tells you, so you don't talk to yourself without knowing it.
  • Commands: in any message, including the F10 one, you can start with slash me (or slash yo) and a space for an action, slash ic (or slash ep) to speak in character, slash ooc (or slash fp) to speak out of character, or slash ficha to change your profile.
  • Your profile: a sentence, of up to two hundred characters, that says who your character is. You write it on the phone, Online, Role-play, Your profile. It's saved on the server with your character.
  • Reading another player's profile: on the phone, Online, Who is here, choose that person and Read their profile. Or, if they're near you, Shift J: after telling you how they're dressed, the game reads their profile, if they've written one. With a controller, in the actions menu, or with View held and the Share button, the touchpad or the microphone button.
  • Reviewing what was said: on the phone, Online, Role-play, there's a list with what was said in character and the actions, and another with what was said out of character. Online, Review the latest messages, has everything together.
  • If the server has the role-play channels turned off, or is from an earlier version, the game tells you when you try to use them, and Online shows it.
  • The game doesn't have microphone voice chat. To talk out loud with your people while you play, use any separate voice program.

The radio in another player's vehicle

  • When you're near another player's vehicle that has one of the game's stations on, you hear it faint and muffled, the way you hear a radio from outside, from where their vehicle is. When you move about thirty meters away it fades out.
  • It only plays if you don't have your own radio on, and only one at a time: the one in the nearest vehicle. If you're riding as a passenger with them, you hear their radio.
  • The first time, the game tells you what's playing and whose it is.
  • It only works for the stations that come with the game. If you have My music on, or a station made with your own files, the others hear nothing and nothing leaves your computer, not even the name.
  • The only thing shared with the others is which station you have on. Each game plays its own copy, so the song may not be the same one you hear.
  • To stop hearing it: Options, Sound and music, On a server, hear the radio of other players' vehicles. Whoever runs the server can also turn it off for everyone.

If the server restarts or the connection drops

The game no longer sends you back to the menu. It stays on a waiting screen, with your game just as it was and the world paused, and it rejoins by itself.

  • When whoever runs the server is going to restart it to update it or for maintenance, the server warns beforehand with a countdown: the game tells you and you don't have to do anything. Your character is saved just before it closes.
  • When it closes, the waiting screen opens. It says what is happening: that the server is updating, that it's restarting, or that the connection has dropped. And that it will reconnect you as soon as it's back.
  • While you wait a faint two-note tone plays every few seconds: it means it's still waiting. At thirty seconds, at one minute, at two minutes and then every two minutes, it tells you how long you've been waiting. Any key repeats how it's going: how long you've been waiting, what happened on the last attempt, when it will try again and how much longer it will wait. F1 explains the screen.
  • The game tries to join every few seconds, more patiently each time: after three seconds, after five, after eight, after twelve, and then every fifteen.
  • When the server comes back, you join by yourself, with your usual identity, without the game asking you for anything. You carry on just as you were: same town and same spot, the vehicle you were in, your health, your weapons, your money, your mission with its step and the station you had on. The time is the server's, which saves it when it stops.
  • The server still decides about the money: when you come back, your game tells it what you have and it checks it with its usual rules. If everything was saved, which is normal, nothing changes.
  • If the police were looking for you: on a server that runs the police itself, the server saves the wanted level and they're still looking for you when you come back; on the others, your level stays as it was.
  • Whatever depended on other players is rebuilt if they come back too. The mission group is rebuilt automatically only by whoever led it, as the others join, for three minutes; if someone doesn't come back, the game says who is missing and how to invite them later. If you were riding as a passenger in someone else's vehicle, you're left on foot where you were, and the game tells you. If while the server was coming back another player took the vehicle you were in, it tells you that too.
  • Escape stops waiting and goes to the main menu. Your character stays on the server as it was at its last save, and the game tells you when that was.
  • With a controller, the waiting screen works like the others: A or Cross repeats how it's going, B or Circle stops waiting and goes to the main menu, and Y or Triangle explains the screen. With a controller connected, the game says those buttons instead of the keys.
  • If the server doesn't come back within the maximum time, which is ten minutes by default, the game says so and goes to the main menu. Your character is still saved on the server.
  • If the server comes back asking for a newer version of the game, the screen says so clearly and offers to look for the update with Enter. After updating, when it opens, the game offers to rejoin that server. If you'd rather do it by hand: main menu, Play on a server.
  • If it comes back but doesn't let you in as before, for example because its certificate or its joining rules have changed, the game explains why and goes to the main menu. Your character is still saved on the server.
  • If whoever runs the server shuts it down completely, or you're kicked, there's no waiting: you go back to the main menu, as always.
  • In Options, Play online, you choose whether you want the game to wait and rejoin by itself, which comes turned on, and how many minutes it waits at most, from 1 to 120. With the option turned off, everything is as before: you go back to the main menu and join yourself.
  • With a version 1.7.0 server, which doesn't warn or say why it's closing, the game waits anyway and tries to get back in, in case it comes back.

Leaving

Online, Leave the server, or the pause menu. The character is saved: the latest was saved less than a minute ago, and what you bought, a few seconds after buying it.

Setting up your own server

Anyone can set up a server, for two friends or for a hundred people. Everything you need comes with the game, in the servidor folder.

  • On Windows, the easiest way is the panel: in the servidor folder, open Panel del servidor (the server panel). It's a window that works with a screen reader. F5 starts the server and Shift F5 stops it.
  • From the panel you can see who is inside, send an announcement to everyone or a message to one player, kick, ban, appoint administrators, create invitations, change the configuration by tabs, and make and restore backups.
  • Those who prefer it have the same server in a text window: Servidor por consola (the console server), in the same folder.
  • It can also be set up on Linux, on a Mac, on a Raspberry Pi, on a NAS, on a rented server or with Docker. There you don't need the game: Python and the otros_sistemas folder, which is inside the servidor folder, are enough.
  • There's no fixed player limit: whoever runs the server sets it, and can set none.
  • The configuration, the characters and the backups are saved in the servidor folder. Before updating the game, stop the server and close the panel.
  • The complete step-by-step guide, with how to open the router port and all the options explained, is in the servidor folder, in documentacion_en.html, and also in data, docs, servidor_en.html. From the panel it opens with F1.

Playing with people on another version

You don't all need to have the same version of the game. The server doesn't turn anyone away because of their version, unless its owner asks for a minimum version, and then the game tells you which.

  • The server's world (places, weapons, vehicles, missions) is provided by the game: that of the first person to join and, when someone with a newer version arrives, that of that version. Nobody has to do anything, and nobody gets kicked.
  • What affects both players is decided by the server with the same figures for everyone: the damage, range and rate of fire of each weapon, and the money. Having the new version gives no advantage in a fight.
  • If another player uses a weapon or a vehicle your version doesn't have, you hear and see them as the closest thing your game knows, and the damage is what the server sets.
  • If you're the one who has something the server's world doesn't know, the game warns you: against other players that weapon counts as being bare-handed, that vehicle is seen as a compact car, and in a town the server doesn't have nobody sees you.
  • You can't give another player a weapon their version doesn't have: the server gives it back to you and tells you.
  • Your character lives on the server and loses nothing by changing version. If one day you join with an earlier version, you won't see the weapons or vehicles that version doesn't know, but they're still saved, and when you come back with the new one you have everything, along with whatever you did in the meantime.
  • You can also join with mods that change the world, unless the server owner doesn't want that: you play in what you have in common and what your mod adds doesn't count against the others.
  • When you join, the game and the server tell you what doesn't match. You also have it on the phone, Online, Data and rules of this server.
  • What only exists in the new version and doesn't depend on the server is available to whoever has that version. A server from an earlier version still lets you in, but doesn't have the new things: the game tells you when you try.

What's new in 1.7.1 on a server

  • Shopping by walking around the store, the pantry, the fridge and cooking work the same as in solo play, and what you keep in your kitchen is saved with your character. Two differences: the server clock is everyone's, so cooking doesn't move it forward, and food goes off with the server's days. If a home stops being yours, what was in its kitchen is lost.
  • These work the same as when playing solo: food delivery, robbing any venue with a till, contracts from the board, asking people for a favor and couple plans. Enemies who arrive in a vehicle in solo play arrive on foot on a server.
  • The new weapons, repair shop upgrades and vehicles work on a server that has the 1.7.1 data: they're brought by the first player who joins with that version. On one still running earlier data, a new weapon counts as being bare-handed against other players, and the game warns you. Those playing with 1.7.0 get them as the closest thing their game knows.
  • Permanent contracts work on a server that pays payrolls: the server pays the payroll. It's in the Life, reputation and consequences section.
  • In group missions, someone who helps in another player's gym fight no longer gets a crime counted for taking down the opponent. Your teammate's enemies also have, in your game, the aim and voice of their type.
  • With the server's police, officers withstand what the police table says for each wanted level, not always the same. What there isn't on a server: the special unit van, which there is always a patrol car.
  • These don't exist on a server: robbing a supplier, negotiating with gangs, recruiting people from the street, living together, working as a police officer and the reconquest of neighborhoods, which also doesn't progress while you're playing with friends. There, the neighborhoods and gangs belong to the players, and the police may be the server's.
